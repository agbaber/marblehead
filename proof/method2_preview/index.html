Property Tax Burden by Household Income

Method 2 first-pass. Data: ACS B19001 (Marblehead, 2024), DOR DLS FY26 average bill, April 15 2026 override presentation.

A single dollar amount of property tax is a very different share of household income at $42,500 than at $250,000. The override increment ($1,498 per year at the median home, tier 3) has the same one-size effect, but is dramatically more burdensome on lower-income households.

Number of Marblehead households (total: 8,289) in each post-override property tax burden bucket. At the median home, 2,356 households would spend more than 10% of income on property tax alone. At a small ($500K) home, that figure falls to 1,330.

Method. Tax bill as a share of household income, computed at each ACS income bracket midpoint. Base tax rate of $8.56 per $1,000 derived from DOR's FY26 average single-family tax bill ($11,055) divided by the town's average single-family home value ($1,291,507, per the April 15 2026 override presentation slide 9). Tier 3 override adds $1.50 per $1,000 of assessed value (same source). Household counts from ACS B19001 (2024 5-year estimate) for Marblehead town. The $200K+ bracket uses a conservative $250,000 midpoint, which makes the regressivity look smaller, not larger.

Caveats. Income brackets are estimated by midpoint. Bracket-to-home-value mapping is illustrative, not a joint distribution. Renters (about 14% of Marblehead households) pay property tax indirectly through rent. Does not yet net out residents protected by the Clause 41C senior exemption or the Circuit Breaker tax credit (Method 3 work to follow).
