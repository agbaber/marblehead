Who Already Can't Afford Marblehead

Before any override: 1,672 owner households (24.7%) already pay more than 30% of income on housing. Source: ACS B25106, Marblehead 2024 5-year estimate.

The US Department of Housing and Urban Development defines a household as "cost-burdened" when housing costs (mortgage, taxes, insurance, utilities) exceed 30% of household income. ACS measures this directly. In Marblehead, before any override, 1,672 of 6,776 owner-occupied households are already cost-burdened. Another 1,357 sit between 20% and 30% – the band where modest tax increases push households over the line.

What an override adds, by income

The override adds $1,498 per year ($125/month) at the median Marblehead home (tier 3, $998,600 assessed value). That increment is the same dollar amount for every household, but a different share of income at each level.

What this is and isn't. The first chart uses ACS-measured housing-cost-burden directly. It does not model the override. It establishes baseline: how many Marblehead households are already at HUD's cost-burdened threshold before the override comes up.

The second chart models how the override increment lands on each income bracket. It assumes everyone is at the median home, which overstates burden for households in smaller homes and understates it for those in larger ones. The honest read is that real burden sits between this scenario and a smaller-home scenario.

What's missing: relief mechanism overlay. 626 cost-burdened owner households earn under $50K. Of those, some qualify for the Clause 41C senior exemption, the Circuit Breaker tax credit, or Clause 41A deferral. Computing the relief-gap (people in 30%+ with no relief available) is the next analytical step.
