What we paid, and the choices we made

Through Jun 9, Marblehead has paid $99.9M to vendors in FY26. Inside General Fund – Town alone, $37.6M went to four line items the town can't meaningfully alter in a single year: state-set insurance premiums, PERAC pension, bond debt service, and Cherry Sheet assessments. The rest is bucketed below by the authorization that put it on the books, and tagged with when that authorization was made.

Voted by you, at the ballot

$6.73M6.7% of FY26 spend

Who chose this: Marblehead voters at past ballot elections, approving debt exclusions to fund specific capital projects outside Proposition 2½.

When setPast ballot elections (2015–2025). Once a debt exclusion passes, the bond term locks payments in for 10–30 years. FY26 is the year on the amortization schedule, not the year of the decision.

Ratified at Town Meeting (this year's annual budget package)

$54.29M54.3% of FY26 spend

What actually happened at Town Meeting: voters who showed up to the May 2025 Annual Town Meeting approved the omnibus budget article (one dollar amount per department) and a handful of capital articles. Marblehead runs Open Town Meeting under MGL c.39: any registered voter can attend and vote, no delegates. Town Meeting's authority is to ratify (or amend on the floor, rarely) the FinCom-recommended package. Granular wage scales, position counts, and vendor selection happen upstream of the warrant.

When setAnnual cycle, May 2025 for FY26. But the package Town Meeting voted on was mostly already locked: CBAs that set wages were signed in prior years; bond debt service was scheduled when the bonds were issued; GIC premiums get set by the state board; state assessments come in by formula. See the choice qualifier below for the carve-out.

Choice qualifier: essentially none of this $43.91M was a fresh Town Meeting decision this year

Inside General Fund – Town, $37.63M (86%) was set by outside boards or past ballots, $4.43M (10%) is multi-year contracts the Town Administrator signed in prior years (trash, IT/SaaS, dental, deferred-comp admin, audit, legal), and $1.85M (4%) is recurring professional services, refunds, commodities, and interfund transfers. The portion that was actually a fresh choice at the May 2025 Annual Town Meeting: close to zero.

GIC + insurance $14.67M · 33%

Premiums reset every fiscal year by the state GIC board. The town's choice to use GIC at all, and the 83/17 employer/employee split, were settled in earlier rounds of collective bargaining.

Bond debt service (U.S. Bank) $14.24M · 32%

Each bond series locked in when issued (mostly 2008–2024). Excluded-debt series authorized by ballot vote on the date the project was approved; in-levy series via Town Meeting article.

Pension assessment (PERAC) $5.38M · 12%

PERAC framework dates to 1945. The FY26 dollar amount was set by the 2024 actuarial valuation of the Marblehead Retirement System, no local discretion in any single budget year.

State assessments (Cherry Sheet) $3.35M · 8%

Recalculated each fall by state DLS via long-standing formulas (MBTA assessment, charter school tuition, state retirement, etc.). Town Meeting cannot alter the amount, only acknowledge it.

Town Administrator multi-year contracts $4.43M · 10%

Trash hauling (Republic Services $895K, Waste Management $546K), Munis SaaS via Tyler Technologies ($204K), Future Technologies IT ($226K), Altus Dental ($396K), Voya deferred-comp admin ($312K), outside legal Mead Talerman & Costa ($166K), outside audit CliftonLarsonAllen ($116K), and others. Signed in prior years by the Town Administrator with Select Board approval; Town Meeting did not choose these line by line.

Other recurring (refunds, commodities, transfers) $1.85M · 4%

Real-estate tax refunds owed back to overpaying property owners ($268K), interfund transfers where the town pays itself ($229K), commodity purchases like fuel and road salt within existing appropriations ($386K), and small professional services. None of this is a discretionary year-to-year choice either, structurally speaking.

Ledger excludes payroll. The actual fresh annual decision at Town Meeting is the departmental appropriation level (which mostly drives staffing and salaries, paid through payroll), not these vendor checks.

Paid by ratepayers, not your tax bill

$31.25M31.3% of FY26 spend

Who chose this: the Municipal Light Board, Water & Sewer Commission, and Harbors & Waters Board set rates and budgets for the self-funded utility enterprises. Town Meeting accepts the rate schedules but residents pay through utility bills, not the property-tax levy.

When setContinuous, by appointed boards. Each enterprise's rates are reset on its own board's schedule (typically annual). Wholesale power supply contracts (Berkshire Wind, etc.) are signed for multi-year terms. Capital articles get a Town Meeting vote when they come up but are repaid from rates, not the levy.

Revolving funds (paid by users of the service)

$1.28M1.3% of FY26 spend

Trusts, donations, and small fundraising funds

$1.24M1.2% of FY26 spend

Who chose this: original donors, donor families, and people contributing to specific causes (Larz Anderson Fund, Shattuck Memorial Fund, Pickett Charity, Glover/Village student activities, fireworks, youth baseball, opioid settlement, etc.). Each fund has its own donor-restricted purpose.

What we can't fully account for

Interfund transfers (the town paying itself)

$1.37M"Town of Marblehead" as vendor, 863 rows

When money moves between funds (GF-School → HS Student Activity, or Town → Enterprise for shared services), the ledger records both sides as outflows. These rows are not external spend; they're internal economic shuffles. Naively summed in the buckets, they create modest double-counting (roughly 1.4% of the total).

Fix: tag interfund-transfer rows and exclude from "external spend" totals; show them in a separate "internal movement" line.

PII-redacted descriptions

313 rowsamounts & vendor preserved

The redaction script masks descriptions on out-of-district SpEd placements (student initials in the original ledger) and any rows in the two medical-claim funds (111F Injury Leave, Workers Compensation). For these rows we know the vendor and the dollars but intentionally don't show what for.

Won't change, this is the privacy gate. Disclosed in the existing redaction note.

Bottom line: the $99.93M total is fully present in the ledger and bucketed; what's incomplete is the per-fund vote citations on the 2.2% long tail, the per-bond split on the $14.24M debt service, and the cosmetic mislabeling of ~$2.5M of interfund transfers and ONE-TIME-PAY catch-all rows. No dollars are missing from the published checkbook; the gaps are about how cleanly each dollar traces back to the vote that authorized it.

About this sketch

All bucket totals are real, computed from the checkbook_FY26_2026-06-09.csv ledger. Fund-to-vote provenance is in data/checkbook_fund_provenance.json (top 20 funds, 97.8% of paid spend). Vote citations cross-reference data/dor_debt_exclusion_all.csv (MA DOR Proposition 2½ Debt Exclusion Votes) for the ballot bucket.

Only 14.3% of vendor checks out of General Fund – Town are subject to year-to-year Town Meeting discretion. The other 85.7% (GIC + pension + bond debt + state assessments) is set by outside boards or prior contracts. Payroll, the largest discretionary line, is paid via a separate system and isn't in the ledger.
