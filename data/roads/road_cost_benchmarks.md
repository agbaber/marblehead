# Road cost benchmarks (for roads.html "Is $3M a lot?" section)

External benchmarks used to contextualize Marblehead's stated ~$3M/yr road
figure. Marblehead's own numbers are in `chapter90_apportionment_MHD.csv`,
`road_ownership_2024.csv`, and the Article 11 authorization ($12,475,000 over
five years, 2022 Annual Report / Town Meeting warrant). Per-mile figures below
use Marblehead's 68.99 accepted centerline miles (2024 Road Inventory).

## Marblehead, derived
- ~$3M/yr stated effective spend ÷ 68.99 accepted mi = **~$43,000 / accepted mile / yr**
  (÷ 80.22 total centerline mi = ~$37,000/mi).
- Composition of the ~$3M: Article 11 $12,475,000 ÷ 5 yr = **~$2.5M/yr local**
  + **~$0.5M/yr** Chapter 90 (base era) ≈ ~$3M. (Derivation, not a town-stated split.)

## Peer comparator: Natick, MA
- **128 miles of accepted roads** (plus 26 unaccepted).
- Pavement program **funded ~$3.45M/yr: ~$2.5M town + ~$1M state**.
- Current-year treatment mix: crack sealing 7 mi, mill-and-overlay ~3 mi,
  **reclamation 0.6 mi = 44% of total cost** (reclamation is the expensive tier).
- Derived: $3.45M ÷ 128 accepted mi = **~$27,000 / accepted mile / yr**
  (town-only $2.5M ÷ 128 = ~$19,500/mi).
- Source: Natick Report, "Smoother roads may be coming to a Natick neighborhood
  near you," March 2024, quoting the Natick DPW.
  https://www.natickreport.com/2024/03/smoother-roads-may-be-coming-to-a-natick-neighborhood-near-you/
  Town program page: https://www.natickma.gov/2370/Annual-Paving-Program

## Read of the comparison
Marblehead's ~$3M is close to Natick's ~$3.45M in absolute terms and nearly
identical in *town* contribution (~$2.5M each). Per mile Marblehead is higher
(~$43K vs ~$27K) because it has roughly half the network, so fixed program costs
spread over fewer miles, and because a deferred, coastal network needs more of
the expensive reclamation tier. Conclusion for the page: ~$3M is in a normal
range for a Massachusetts town of this type, not an outlier.

## Pavement preservation / deferral cost curve (authoritative)
- Every $1 spent to keep a road in good condition avoids an estimated **$6 to
  $14** needed later to rebuild it once badly deteriorated (AASHTO, as cited in
  Smart Growth America & Taxpayers for Common Sense, Repair Priorities 2014).
  https://smartgrowthamerica.org/resources/repair-priorities-2014-transportation-strategies-to-improve-road-conditions-and-state-fiscal-outlooks/
  Conservative floor: FHWA puts it at "up to $6" (GAO-13-32R, Nov 2012).
- Deterioration curve: pavement loses ~40% of its quality over the first ~75% of
  its life, then the next ~40% in only ~12% of its life, which is why catching a
  road before the curve turns down is far cheaper. Source: APWA Pothole Fact
  Sheet, 2015. https://mtc.ca.gov/sites/default/files/APWA_Pothole_Fact_Sheet.pdf
- These are inflation-neutral ratios. Ties to Marblehead's own guide language
  that "surface patches masked deeper structural failures" and that skipping
  maintenance makes repairs more expensive (Marblehead Roads Information Guide).

## Category caveat (do not conflate on the page)
National/state "road maintenance" benchmarks (~$14,500-$18,700 per lane-mile/yr;
Reason Foundation Annual Highway Report state disbursements; national averages)
measure **routine operations & maintenance** (patching, sweeping, drainage,
striping), which town crews do out of the operating budget. Marblehead's ~$3M is
a **capital resurfacing/reconstruction** program, a different category. The
apples-to-apples comparator is another town's *capital paving program* (Natick),
not the O&M per-lane-mile benchmark.

## What $3M/yr buys: MA treatment unit costs (Randolph, MA PMP, BETA Group, 2020)
Per square yard, 2020 dollars (add ~30-40% for 2025). Per-mile derived at
~15,840 SY/mile (27-ft road); flagged as derivation, not published per-mile.
- Crack seal: $0.50/SY (~$7,900/mi)
- Chip seal: $2.40/SY
- Mill & overlay (2"): $12/SY (~$190,000/mi)
- Full-depth reclamation: $32/SY (~$507,000/mi)
- Reclamation: $40/SY (~$634,000/mi)
- Full reconstruction: $50/SY (~$792,000/mi)
- Source: Town of Randolph, MA Pavement Management Program (BETA Group), Jan 2020.
  https://www.randolph-ma.gov/DocumentCenter/View/168/Pavement-Management-Program-Draft-Report-PDF
- Corroboration (Amherst, MA town engineer, 2026): crack seal ~$1/SY up to
  reconstruction ~$100/SY, a roughly 100x cheap-to-expensive spread.
  https://www.amherstindy.org/2026/04/10/town-engineer-explains-how-road-repairs-are-prioritized-2/
- LABEL CAVEAT: "reclamation" is not standardized ($40/SY in Randolph vs
  $136/SY in Merrimac's worst-condition band). Prefer citing full-depth
  reclamation ($32/SY) or reconstruction ($50/SY) for the "expensive tier."
- **What $3M buys (derived):** at ~$190K/mi that is ~15 miles/yr of mill-and-
  overlay; at ~$634K/mi, ~4.7 miles/yr of reclamation. Over ~69-80 town miles
  that sustains roughly a 15-20 year resurfacing cycle if mostly overlay, less
  if heavy reclamation. Reasonable for a network of this size.

## Statewide context (MMA)
- MA municipalities need ~$715M/yr to maintain ~30,000 miles of local roads
  (~$23,800/mile/yr statewide), while base Chapter 90 was level-funded at $200M
  from FY2012 until recent increases. Secondary figure often cited: ~$340,000 to
  repave one mile of road in MA.
- Source: Massachusetts Municipal Association, Chapter 90 advocacy.
  https://www.mma.org/advocacy/chapter-90-funding-is-essential-to-repair-our-roads/

## New England / coastal cost drivers (for the "why it costs more" deep-dive)
Confidence flags: freeze-thaw, road-salt cost, and paving-season physics are
well sourced; coastal-corrosion magnitude and the "colonial narrow streets cost
more" claim are weak and should be stated qualitatively or omitted.
- Freeze-thaw / frost heave: water freezes in the subgrade, heaves and cracks
  pavement; in spring thaw roads lose ~45-50% of bearing capacity (why towns
  post load limits). Source: Pavement Interactive, Frost Action.
  https://pavementinteractive.org/reference-desk/design/design-parameters/frost-action/
- Road salt / winter maintenance: US agencies spend >$2.3B/yr on snow & ice
  control; winter maintenance is ~20% of state DOT maintenance budgets; deicing
  chlorides accelerate pavement and rebar deterioration. Source: FHWA Road
  Weather Management. https://ops.fhwa.dot.gov/weather/weather_events/snow_ice.htm
- Short paving season: hot-mix asphalt is placed at 275-300F and must compact
  before ~185F; most MA paving stops when nights drop below ~40F, compressing
  work into ~mid-spring to mid-fall. Source: Pavement Interactive, Cold Weather
  Paving. https://pavementinteractive.org/reference-desk/construction/compaction/cold-weather-paving/
- Coastal salt air (mechanism only, magnitude thin): airborne chloride +
  moisture corrode embedded steel; state to say qualitatively, no hard number.
- Freeze-thaw cycles and frost heave crack and buckle pavement each winter.
- Road salt / deicing chemicals accelerate surface and joint deterioration.
- Coastal salt air adds corrosion/moisture exposure.
- Older, narrow street networks on rocky terrain complicate drainage and widen
  the scope of any rebuild.
- A short New England paving season compresses the work window.
- Marblehead's guide cites coastal climate, salt air, freeze-thaw, and rocky
  terrain as local deterioration drivers (Marblehead Roads Information Guide).
- General engineering context: FHWA Pavement Preservation (above) on how
  climate-driven deterioration compounds when maintenance is deferred.
