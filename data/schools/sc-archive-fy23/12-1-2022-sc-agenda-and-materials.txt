                                                MEMORANDUM

TO:               Marblehead School Committee
FROM:             Michelle Cresta, Assistant Superintendent for Finance & Operations
DATE:             November 28, 2022
RE:               FY23 Financial Report as of September 30, 2022 and October 31, 2022 Update



Attached please find a year-to-date expenditure report as of September 30, 2022 which marks the end
of our first quarter of the fiscal year. We have spent a total of $4,792,512 or 10.9% of the FY23
budget at the end of September and a total of $8,212,683 or 18.7% at the end of October. This
reporting packet also includes a grant and revolving status report which will be provided each quarter.


Please make note of the following.
         We continue to operate with vacancies in the areas of special education teachers,
          paraprofessionals, custodians, and cafeteria workers. We currently have 30 staffing vacancies
          and 14 of those vacancies are in the area of paras and tutors alone. The lack of staffing will
          create some excess available funds, but there are also increased costs such as substitute teachers
          and compensatory services that we will incur as a result of these staffing shortages. It should be
          noted that we have recently filled two bus driver positions and expect to have our new drivers
          fully trained and licensed in the very near future.
         The following budget accounts will need to be watched closely as we progress throughout this
          year:
              o   Substitutes – we continue to have a significant number of teaching absences.
              o   Custodian salaries and custodian over-time – we continue to operate short-staffed. We
                  are contracting with an outside agency to provide a couple of contracted custodians and
                  are also covering shifts with existing custodians which causes additional over-time
                  costs.
              o   Utilities – the cost of utilities for the current year is expected to be higher than
                  anticipated. The next quarterly financial report will provide a projection of the
                  anticipated costs for the remainder of the fiscal year.
            o   Unemployment Costs – As of the end of October we have spent $58,085 or 48% of the
                budget. As the year goes on our costs will decrease as the maximum benefit period for
                qualifying employees is currently at 26 weeks.


            o   Out of District Transportation and Tuition Costs – As we all know there is a current
                mental health crisis occurring and Marblehead is no stranger to this unfortunate trend.
                However, it is fortunate that we are in a good place with carry-over special education
                funds as we are able to fund the current projected short-falls.


                The shortage of drivers and escalation in fuel costs has caused contracted special
                education transportation costs to significantly increase. At the current time we are
                transporting more students than planned. We had budgeted a total cost of $690,000 for
                special education transportation costs. The current projected cost is anticipated to be
                $1,148,000 which is $458,000 over budget. In regards to out of district tuitions, our
                total current student needs are projected to be $4,013,500. We budgeted $3,500,000 for
                out of district tuitions. The resulting short-fall is $513,500.


                Even with the stagging higher than anticipated budget numbers with respect to both
                transportation and tuition, we continue to be in good shape at the current time. We are
                ale to cover this short-fall with a number of funding sources. We were able to prepay
                tuitions at the end of fiscal year 2022 in the amount of $250,000, we were able to carry-
                over available balances of $820,000 in the circuit breaker fund and $160,000 in tuition
                revolving funds from FY22, and lastly our FY23 circuit breaker approved
                reimbursement is $220,000 higher than budgeted. All of these funds total $1,450,000
                leaving an available balance of $478,500. These funds are in addition to the Town’s
                Special Education Reserve fund of $250,000. It is good that we have these available
                funds; however, this available balance is not ideal. My goal is to maintain a balance of
                approximately $1,000,000 in the circuit breaker fund to be able to offset the following
                year’s anticipated budget needs. We will continue to monitor these costs closely and
                hope that the fiscal situation improves.


If you should have any questions, please feel free to contact me at any time.

Dear members of the Select Board and School Committee,

I would like to register my interest in ﬁlling the vacant seat on the Marblehead School
Committee.

My wife, Julie, and I are longtime residents of Marblehead (22 years) and former local
business owners who always supported the local schools with gift cards and teacher
luncheons at both the Gerry and Coﬃn schools.

We have two sons, Silas and Wendell. At present, Silas is a freshman at American
University in DC and Wendell a 6th grader at the Village school.

I am currently the Head of School at the Devereux and Devereux@Waring Schools. In
addition to my day position I volunteer my time serving as a member of the MCCPS
Board of Trustees here in Marblehead. I am in my 3rd term (7th year) and have served
both as Board Clerk(twice) and Vice-Chair as well as Chair of the Development &
Communications Committee and Chair of the Governance Committee during my time
on the Board.

I believe that I have learned many of the intricacies of working with others as part of a
Board/Committee (some years as many as 13 fellow members) during my tenure at
MCCPS. I am well versed in Roberts Rules, Ma. Open Meeting and Conﬂict of Interest
Laws and have experience in the interview and hiring process at the Head of School/
Superintendent level.

I was asked to sit (and served) on the hiring/interview committee for the current
Assistant Superintendent, Nan Murphy and have been vocal, both in print and in
meetings, with regards to the goings on within the Marblehead Public School District
going as far back as the pre-closure of the Gerry School.

After living in Massachusetts as a “Permanent Legal Resident/Green card holder” since
1998, I ﬁnally became a US citizen this year. My new citizenship status aﬀords me two
things; the ability to vote in all elections, local, state and national and the opportunity
to serve/run for election on town Boards and Committees.

At my “swearing-in ceremony” at the JFK Building in Boston, the proctor urged all of
us newly minted citizens to “vote, to make a diﬀerence, to get involved and to give
back” to our new country.

I have supported, held signs, hosted lawn signs, sent emails, written “letters to the
editor” and “talked up” many of you who are reading this letter, so too have I disagreed
with and criticized others of you. However, as a resident, business owner and tax payer
I believe that I have spoken plainly, factually and been as involved as possible without
actually being able to vote or run for oﬃce.

All this being said, I view this vacant School Committee seat as a chance to truly give
back and make a diﬀerence to our town and would welcome the opportunity to ﬁll the
seat until elections are held in June, 2023.

If selected, I would of course, relinquish my seat on the MCCPS Board of Trustees and
give my all to the School Committee position for the next six months and hopefully
beyond.

Conversely, if not selected, and I’m sure there are many qualiﬁed residents vying for
the opportunity to serve, then so be it. Either way, I will continue to stay involved,
campaign for and champion others, but above all exercise my right to ﬁnally attend
and vote at Town Meeting!

Thanks so much to all of you for both your consideration of my candidacy and all that
you do for our town.

Sincerely,



Paul Baker
