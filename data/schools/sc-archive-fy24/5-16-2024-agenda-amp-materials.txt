Schoo! Committee Memorandum

To: Marblehead School Committee
From: Theresa McGuinness, Ed.D

Re: Public Hearing - Schoo! Choice
Date: May 13, 2024

Under the provisions of the Education Reform Act of 1993, the Department of Elementary and Secondary
Education presumes that each school committee will admit non-resident students under the School Choice
Program unless there is a vote to the contrary. Under M.G.L. Chapter 76, Section 128, the School Committee is
obliged to hold a public hearing and vote on School Choice participation prior to June 1* of each school year.

| recommend that the School Committee take action on this matter at the School Committee meeting
scheduled for May 16, 2024 in order to notify the State of our decision in accordance with the deadline.

In May of 2023, given the pending override and the potential for instability with the FY24 budget, former
Superintendent Dr. John Buckey recommended not to participate in School Choice for that upcoming
2023-2024 school year.

As the decision to participate relates to enroliment numbers and available classroom space, it is my
recommendation not to elect to participate in Schoo! Choice due to the lack of available classroom space
particularly at the elementary level. Additionally, as per the recommendation of the former Superintendent,
policy JFBB should be reviewed and updated to address considerations for admissions deadlines, attendance,
sibling attendance, transportation and safety that the current policy does not address prior to MPS becoming
a School Choice district. It is my recommendation that the School Committee work with the new incoming
Interim Superintendent and administrative team to determine appropriate policy adjustments to policy JFBB
prior to electing to participate in School Choice for the 2025-2026 school year.

Proposed Motion:

Move that the Marblehead Public Schools do not participate in the School Choice program for the
2024-2025 school year.

Special Education Program Review

The evaluation team for the special education program review met last week to finalize its ratings of
the five firms who submitted proposals. The highest ranked firm was Academic Discoveries, a
Massachusetts firm that has done this type of work for a number of districts in the state. We have
contacted Academic Discoveries to commence negotiations for contract terms and conditions. The
proposed cost for their work with the district was $66,000, We hope to award the final contract next
week so they can begin their work in June. It would be our recommendation that the cost be
allocated to the ESSER and/or IDEA grant and the special education tuition revolving fund, since
funds were not allocated in the FY2025 budget.

Principal Search Updates

The Glover School Principal Search continues and the job posting was reopened after our finalist
declined the job offer. We will review the new application packets as they come in and re-assemble
the hiring committee.

The Village School Principal Search is moving along and the search committee completed the second
round of interviews this week. We are doing reference checks and will have more information in the
next few days about the finalists for that position.

This report was written by Gina Hart, LICSW, LADC1, Social Worker at Marblehead High
School and Julia Ferreira, Assistant Superintendent of Marblehead Public Schools. Resources for
this project are provided by the Massachusetts General Hospital Center For Addiction Medicine.
This is a grant-funded project. Resources include: Survey of Substance Use and Related Risk
Factors in Massachusetts Schools and the Marblehead High School Survey Report.

BACKGROUND

In order to best inform members of our community about the strengths and needs of our student
population, collecting data around substance use, mental health and protective and risk factors to
these issues is paramount. This data will be used not only to share with students, staff and
community members, but to assess the need for programming through tiered interventions. This
is the third consecutive year that data has been collected at Marblehead High School and the
longitudinal data will be used to view trends over time and relative differences. Since 2021, we
have improved upon the survey questions based on feedback. If the questions were not exactly
the same, we did not compare to 2022-2023 in order to maintain the validity of the data
collection.

METHODOLOGY AND SURVEY DISTRIBUTION
Marblehead High School students were offered 2 surveys during “Magic block” on October 11,
2023. Students were notified that they could opt out at any time and emphasis was put on the
anonymity and confidentiality of the survey, and watched a short video presentation of last year’s
data prior to taking the surveys.
® MGH SURE: 743 students (85% response rate), 8.3% opted out by parent/guardian

platform for electronic data capture. The results are shared back with MHS
identified staff (Gina Hart) via the MGH SURF survey report.
e Supplemental questions via Anonymous Googie Form: 677 responses

Gina Hart and central administration, and consult with other experts in the field.

LIMITS OF THE STUDY

One of the most asked questions about the survey is, how do you know students are honest? We
can never ensure that every student is fully honest. However, when we view relative differences
and trends over time we can use this data as a baseline. We also improve our delivery every year
to focus on the anonymity and confidentiality of the study and evidence is shown by the increase
in participation: up from 75% last year to 85% this year.

Current Programming District-wide:

As a District, we are focused on developing our Multi-Tiered System of Support framework to
ensure all students meet or exceed academic and social-emotional learning expectations.
Furthermore, we are working to reduce at-risk measures and outcomes for all marginalized
and/or at-risk students to the point where all students feel safe, supported, and included in school.

In addition to the programming at MHS, there are several district-wide efforts in effect to
provide students in Kindergarten through 8th grade opportunities to develop social-emotional
skills and support their mental health. For example, we integrate Responsive Classroom practices
in our elementary classrooms, embed Advisory programs in our upper elementary and middle
school, and work to foster a sense of belonging through school-wide initiatives at all schools.
Many of our schools offer extracurricular activities, like MHS, for students to participate in
outside of the school day.

For the 2024-2025 school year, we are expanding our Health Education program to Village
School so there will be a dedicated Health Educator who will teach a standards-based aligned
curriculum designed to meet the needs of our students in grades 4 through 6. The addition of the
Village school Health Educator will now allow for collaboration among our Health Educators in
grades 4 through 12 to offer a vertically aligned program to support our students,

We are also continuously working to support our educators through professional learning to in
turn support all learners in our schools. Professional development is offered to all educators to
expand their professional learning and collaborate with fellow educators and mental health
clinicians.

Recommendations for Next Steps:
Continuing to build upon and enhance the existing programming around the following topics:

Early use issue: Evidence based prevention programming at HS but also lower level schools.
Harm Reduction: Education around reducing the risks of use as opposed to scare tactics or
awareness campaigns. Unrealistic to expect students to never try things.

Social/Emotional Learning and Mental Health Support: Programming at all levels that
supports the needs of our students in these areas to enhance protective factors and reduce risk
factors: specific focus on anxiety, depression, suicidality.

LGBTOQIA and DEI Programming: Enhancing current and including further training and
programming to support our staff and students to address inequities, biases, microaggressions,
and inclusion.

Body Image and Eating Disorders: Integrate tiered preventative and educational programming.

Parent and Community Programming: Addressing Parental attitudes and beliefs around
substance use and mental health and enhancing education: working together with the community
and school to address substance use concems as a 3-pronged approach.

Grade 12 Programming: Thoughtful programming to support our senior class transition.

The schoolwide survey as an annual tool: the only way to assess where our community stands
and analyze trends over time; serves as a way to see if implemented programming is effective.
