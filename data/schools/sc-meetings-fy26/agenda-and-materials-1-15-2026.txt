Frequently Asked Questions

Why did Essex Tech change its admissions process?

Essex Tech is required by the Commonwealth of Massachusetts to use a lottery-based admissions system.
In Spring 2025, the Massachusetts Department of Elementary and Secondary Education (DESE) directed all
vocational and agricultural schools to move away from selective admissions models and adopt a random
lottery when applications exceed available seats. This change is designed to increase fairness, equity, and
consistency statewide, ensuring all students have an equal opportunity for admission.

When does this new admissions process take effect?
The lottery-based admissions process applies to ail applicants for Fall 2026 admission.

What does “allocation process” mean?
Because Essex Tech is a regional school serving 17 member communities, the allocation process ensures
that every sending community has access to Essex Tech, rather than seats being filled solely on a first-come

or selective basis.

How does the lottery actually work?
All students who submit a complete application by the deadline are entered into a random, electronic

lottery, Seats are first filled using community-based minimum targets. Remaining seats are filled through an
at-large lottery process. Waitlists are maintained to ensure all available seats are filled fairly and efficiently.
The lottery does not consider grades, test scores, attendance, or discipline.

Why can’t Essex Tech admit everyone who applies?
Essex Tech has limited capacity. Each year, we receive around 1,500 applications for a limited number of

freshman seats, resulting in a significant waitlist.

Does this process advantage or disadvantage certain communities?
No. The process is designed specifically to prevent advantages or disadvantages based on lacation, school
district size, and number of applications. The allocation model ensures predictable, equitable access for all

member communities.

When and how will families receive lottery results?
Families and sencing-school counselors will be notified of lottery results by-March 1, 2026. Students who
are conditionally accepted must still meet promotion and residency verification requirements as outlined in

Essex Tech’s Admissions Policy.

MEMORANDUM

TO: Marblehead School Committee

FROM: Michael Pfifferling, Assistant Superintendent for Finance & Operations

DATE: January 8, 2026

RE: Natural Gas Contract Renewal

Since 2014, the Marbiehead Public School Department has been purchasing our natural gas supply
and natural gas delivery separately. We have been utilizing National Grid for the delivery and NRG
for the supply.

The current 3-year contract for the supply portion is set to expire in March, 2026. We were fortunate
to be able to lock in new pricing on January 8, 2026 for the period of April 1, 2026 through March 30,

2029 at a lower price than we had in our previous contract.

We are currently using approximately 34,000 dekatherms of Natural Gas per year and our current

contract is priced at $8.54 per dekatherm. This is for the supply only, not the delivery.
The new contract we have executed as of today for the period 4/1/26 — 3/3 1/29 is at $7.10 per
dekatherm. This equates to an approximate savings of $49,000 annually over the current expense for

the next 3 years.

As the delivery costs are regulated and set by the Government, we cannot lock in prices for that aspect

of our heating fuel costs.
Attached you will find a copy of the executed contract referenced above.
