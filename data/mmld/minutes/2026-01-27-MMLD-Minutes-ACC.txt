Public Comments:

Albert Jordan expressed concerns about Marblehead joining the Green Communities program,
questioning whether the town’s financial contributions would be spent locally or allocated elsewhere.
He urged the board to consider whether funds could instead be retained in town to improve local
energy efficiency. As an example, he pointed to simple measures such as ensuring exterior lights at
Village School are turned off during the day. He also raised broader concerns about potential fraud
within Mass Save and other state energy programs.

Eileen Mathieu of Sustainable Marblehead thanked the board and MMLD for hosting the strategy
session and praised the organization for its professionalism, leadership, and progress toward
Marblehead’s 2040 Net Zero goals. She highlighted MMLD’s support of electrification efforts, the hiring
of Michael Hall, and commended his new newsletter for effectively promoting electrification, while
noting that MMLD will play a critical role in guiding the town’s transition to meet its goals. She closed by
voicing strong support for the Light Board helping Marblehead pursue Green Community status; a full
transcript of Eileen’s comments is available and attached.

Updates to MMWEC Voting Representative

   •   Chair Yarmoff noted that MMLD’s list of representatives to MMWEC is outdated and has not
       been updated in 4–5 years, prompting the need for a formal refresh.
   •   He clarified, in response to Commissioner Smith, that MMWEC requires one Principal
       representative with up to five alternates, and that each of the 18 MMWEC members has equal
       voting power.
   •   Chair Yarmoff observed that current voting representatives across MMWEC are all General
       Managers and expressed a desire for better balance between commissioners and GMs.
   •   General Manager Blair highlighted his prior service on the MMWEC Board of Directors and
       ongoing discussions to rejoin, emphasizing that MMWEC provides far more than power
       purchasing by offering expertise and resources that expand MMLD’s capabilities.
   •   While acknowledging that the voting role is a significant time commitment better suited to a
       GM’s schedule, Blair respected interest from the board, leading Chair Yarmoff to volunteer as
       MMLD’s voting representative; Commissioner Frechette asked about joint attendance at
       meetings, which Blair indicated is likely feasible, and Chair Yarmoff thanked Blair for bringing
       greater transparency to MMLD’s relationship with MMWEC.

   •   Village 13 Substation Project
            o Minor switchgear and breaker issues remain; high-voltage tie-ins underway and
               transformer foundations being prepared.
            o Bond released but final payment (~⅓) is being withheld pending resolution; projected
               completion is May/June after ~60 days of delays.
            o Chair Yarmoff raised concerns about summer reliability; General Manager Blair
               confirmed MMLD will not be operating on a single supply line.
   •   ISO-NE & Capacity Market Changes
            o Wholesale power costs include energy, transmission, capacity, and environmental
               attributes.
            o Wilkins currently earns ~$100K/year for 5 MW of capacity; capacity market reforms
               (targeted for 2028) may favor seasonal structures and utility-scale batteries.
            o Blair expects limited net ratepayer impact, with potential cost savings offset by new
               compliance requirements; Chair Yarmoff requested more discussion on hedging strategy
               regarding transmission and capacity charges.
   •   BESS Proposal at Village 13
            o Proposed Battery Energy Storage System with Lightshift requires three agreements
               (Energy Storage Service Agreement (ESSA), interconnection, and land-use); some land
               involves a shared utility corridor with Sewer, potentially requiring a 25-year lease.
            o Target is to execute agreements by June 2026 for a June 2027 in-service date; financial
               benefits and a decommissioning fund are outlined in the slides, with increased value
               after year seven.
            o Chair Yarmoff noted prior board authorization to explore this project; Blair is
               comfortable signing the ESSA pending board approval and requested feedback by
               Monday, with additional agreements returning for future votes.
