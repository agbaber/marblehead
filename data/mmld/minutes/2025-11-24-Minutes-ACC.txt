General manager Update (Slides Attached)
   - MMLD has received a bid for the new Digger Derrick truck
   - GM and Team are working on Bid Packages for distribution system upgrades in 2026
   - Provided an update on MMLD Thanksgiving week open business hours
   - Safety Minute:
          o Slips, Trips, and Falls are the #2 cause of workplace injuries
          o Watch out for Ice, wet leaves, ladder falls, etc.
   - A long-range plan of topics likely to be brought to the regularly scheduled board meetings was
       proposed with the goal of allowing all stakeholders to plan in advance for meetings.
          o There was a brief discussion between the Chair and General Manager about
              Department readiness for possible ISO-NE mandated demand management procedures
              (OP-4). The General Manager discussed the proposed Battery Energy Storage System
              (BESS)
          o Light Shift Energy has submitted a proposal and brings relevant experience from
              deploying BESS units with MMWEC and other municipal utilities. The team is keeping an
              eye on shifting federal tariffs and incentives that could affect timing and economics.
              Exact siting and system count are still undecided, though Village 13 is the leading
              candidate, with transformer storage likely shifting to Tioga Way. Early estimates suggest
              about $1 million in annual savings, shared evenly between MMLD and Light Shift for the
              planned 5 MW / 20 MWh BESS.
          o Harrington pressed for clarity on how the BESS delivers savings, what Light Shift
              provides in return for a 50/50 split, and what it would cost for MMLD to go it alone. Blair
              explained that most of the value comes from peak-capacity management, and that Light
              Shift covers at their cost the full lifecycle for the project — procurement, installation,
              operations, and eventual decommissioning. Doing it independently would cost MMLD
              roughly $1 million per MW, plus long-term operational and disposal responsibilities. In
              response to Smith, Blair noted the system’s expected lifespan is about 20 years.
   - Financial Update (See attached slides for full details)
          o Smith asked about why we have been $1million over budget on power costs and
              whether it was due to the gas-produced electricity for which we do not have hedges,
              Blair mentioned that this is a large piece of the overage, but other factors are at play.
          o MMLD receives credits for the Wilkins power plant, but the plant received numerous
              fines this year for non-performance, which mitigated any benefit from credits.




Review Policy on Voluntary Payment-in-lieu-of-taxes (PILOT) (Draft policy attached):


                                                                                                     2/3
   -   Chair Yarmoff emphasized that PILOT payments are voluntary, can only come from surplus, and
       cannot be built into customer rates, noting that four Municipal Light Department do not pay any
       money to their towns and others vary widely, with MMLD sitting near the statewide average.
       (See slides). The goal of the new policy is to formalize payments using a per–MWh-sold formula
       instead of relying on past ad-hoc decisions. A draft policy has been circulated for board review
       ahead of a December vote.
   -   Smith asked whether the funds could be earmarked for specific uses, and Hull recalled a past
       instance where a payment helped fund a vehicle, though that may have been a unique case.



Strategic Plan for MMLD
GM Blair and the board have a desire to create a framework to guide future de decision-making
    - Blair proposed a two-phase plan:
            o Phase 1: Establish priorities for the next 18 months
                    • Complete by February Board Meeting
                    • There is a desire to include the team members from various departments and
                       community members
            o Phase 2: Establish priorities for longer term, work to start after April 2026

   -   Commissioner Hull asked if the plan is operational or elsewhere
          o GM Blair indicated that we have an established mission that we will stand by, but we
             need a plan to decide which of the many options we should prioritize. Without setting a
             destination for the future, we can’t choose a path to get there.
          o Blair used the Wilkins power plant as an example:
                 • If the MMLD’s priority is Decarbonization: This may lead to a decision which
                     would ultimately decommission the plant
                 • If the MMLD’s priority is Resilience: This may lead to upgrades to the plant, such
                     as converting to natural gas
