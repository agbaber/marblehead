                                             G~GREEN NOW!
                                                100% Carbon-Free     Electricity   for MMLD Customers

• MMLD’s power portfolio was ~75% non-carbon in 2025
• For customers who wish to fully offset the carbon footprint of their electric consumption, MMLD
  offers a “Go Green Now” rate rider, which uses Massachusetts Class I Renewable Energy
  Certificates (RECs) to account for the remaining ~25% of their consumption
• MA Class I RECs fluctuate quarterly, but generally are priced around $40/MWh ($0.04/kWh)

     Go Green Now Rate = Consumption (kWh) x MA Class I REC price ($/kWh) x Carbon Intensity (%)

     Go Green Now Rate = Consumption (kWh) x $0.04/kWh) x 25% = Consumption (kWh) x $0.01/kWh

     The current Go Green Now rider is set at $0.022/kWh; the new rate will be priced at $0.01/kWh

    Safety Minute
                                       Know what’s below—call before you dig!
• Calling Dig Safe System before digging is a legal requirement in Massachusetts. It’s an important first step for many spring
  projects and it’s free to use!
• Underground infrastructure—electric, gas, water, sewer, and telecommunications lines—can be just inches below grade;
  striking a buried utility line can result in serious injury, service outages, environmental damage, and costly repairs to utility
  infrastructure. Even small projects like planting trees or installing a fence have potential to cause damage and require a Dig
  Safe!
• Before calling 811, the intended digging area should be marked with white flags.
  Utilities will apply temporary markings in the area within several days to indicate
  where underground lines are located.
• Failure to call 811 can lead to fines, liability for damages, and project delays.
• When digging, always respect the marked lines and maintain safe clearance
  distances when excavating.

   Surface Paving of the Utility Corridor
• The utility corridor is jointly owned by the Marblehead Sewer Department and the MMLD
• In 2018, Town Meeting approved Article 33 – Public Access to Railroad Right of Way, which
  stated that “the Town will change the purpose of the use of the top twelve inches to the
  surface of the [Rail Trail] by adding an additional use, that is the purpose of multi-model bike
  and walking path to be under the care custody and control of the Board of Selectmen”
• Access, use, and maintenance of the corridor remain a priority and concern
• Ongoing coordination between Community Development, Water/Sewer, and MMLD
  • As MMLD’s project to underground the 23kV supply lines advances, we anticipate manholes will be installed
    approximately every 200-300 feet along Salem-Marblehead branch from town line to Village Substation
  • Community Development has concerns about grading around the anticipated manholes, specifically, related to
    exposed covers creating a potential trip hazard for recreational users
      • Community Development’s design for improvements now recommends paving applicable section(s) of the utility corridor
        following the installation of manholes

• In anticipation of the utility-scale battery at the Village Substation, the Marblehead Fire
  Department has requested the utility corridor paved from Bessom Street, under Village Street,
  to the substation to ensure reliable access by any necessary apparatus

   Upcoming Aerial Thermal Efficiency Survey
• As part of its effort to better understand energy savings potential for the local housing stock, the
  Marblehead Municipal Light Department (MMLD) is sponsoring a drone survey to assess thermal
  insulation efficiency of building envelopes in the neighborhoods of the Old Town district.
• External thermal imaging will be
  conducted via drone from an
  altitude of approximately 150 feet to
  identify patterns where insulation or
  weatherization improvements may
  improve energy efficiency, comfort,
  and utility bills. No property access
  is required and no personal images
  are collected.


 Contracted drone operators will be clearly identified and are fully licensed and insured
 Surveys will commence the week of April 6th and are expected to take 2-3 weeks.

  Overdue Customer Bill Collections
• MMLD complies with a winter moratorium on customer shutoffs for nonpayment,
  as directed by the Massachusetts Department of Public Utilities (DPU), from
  November to April each year
• Several hundred customer accounts are currently aged greater than 60 days
• As advised by our auditors and in accordance with good industry practice, MMLD
  will commence enforcement of overdue electric bills next month, in accordance
  with our established policies and procedures
• While customer shutoffs are a last resort, it is important that MMLD recovers
  payment to ensure fairness among all ratepayers
• Customers are encouraged to be proactive in arranging for financial support and/or
  payment plans by calling MMLD customer service team at (781) 631-5600
