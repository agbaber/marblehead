EV Charging Policy Update- Presented by Michael Hall from MMLD

Proposed Changes
   -   Increase peak period rates.
   -   Eliminate overstay fees from 11:00 PM – 7:00 AM, with fees resuming at 7:00 AM.

Feasibility & System Constraints
   -   Differentiating rates between residents and non-residents were considered but deemed
       administratively burdensome to verify users.
   -   Adding tiered overstay fees is not supported by the current ChargePoint system.
   -   Adjustments to overnight overstay rules are feasible.
Board Questions & Clarifications
   -   Secretary Harrington confirmed that rates can be adjusted at any time.
   -   Commissioner Smith and Commissioner Hull requested clarification on overnight overstay
       timing:
           o     If a session begins before the overnight window, standard overstay rules apply.
           o     Overnight fee waivers apply during the designated hours, with fees beginning again at
                 7:00 AM.

Go Green Rate Revision- Presented by GM Blair

Program Overview
   •   MMLD reported that its portfolio was approximately 75% non-carbon in 2025.
   •   The Go Green program allows customers to opt for 100% non-carbon energy.

Proposed Changes
   •   Rate is being recalibrated to reflect the higher baseline of non-carbon energy in the portfolio.
   •   The new proposed rate is $0.01/kWh.
   •   Pricing is based on the cost of Renewable Energy Certificates (RECs) (see slides for details).

Participation & Cost Structure
   •   The program currently has about 50 participants.
   •   GM Blair indicated the rate is essentially a pass-through cost, with only minimal administrative
       expense.

Board Question
   •   Secretary Harrington asked whether the rate includes administrative markups or is purely pass-
       through; confirmed to be largely pass-through.


Vote 2026-8: Motion by Chair to update the GoGreen Rate seconded by Frechette

   -   All in Favor. Motion Passes

GM Update



                                                                                                          3/5
Safety & Utility Corridor Update
   •   Safety Minute emphasized contacting DigSafe.
   •   Undergrounding of the 23 kV lines is being planned in several phases in the utility corridor.
   •   Planned paving of the utility corridor from Village 13 to Lead Mills driven by safety concerns
       around erosion near manholes and Fire Department access needs. The final decision regarding
       the surface of the utility corridor is not determined by MMLD.
   •   Project timeline estimated at 3–5 years (before 2030).
   •   Coordination required with DPW and other stakeholders (e.g., Village Street Bridge, access
       control via bollards or gates).
   •   Paving is expected to reduce future excavation needs if designed properly.
   •   Lead Mills bridges are likely to remain pedestrian-focused.
   •   Alternative surface materials have been tested successfully, but asphalt may be required due to
       manhole infrastructure.

Tioga Way Laydown Area
   •   Site identified for temporary use to consolidate equipment, poles, and transformers.
   •   Plan includes clearing, gravel placement, and temporary fencing.
   •   Enables improved inventory management and disposal of old equipment.
   •   Temporary approach allows flexibility for future permanent use and neighborhood input.
   •   Some abutter concerns are noted and addressed in planning.

Aerial Thermal Efficiency Survey
   •   Drone-based survey (via Kestrix) to identify heat loss and efficiency opportunities for homes.
   •   Pilot project (~800 homes) in partnership with West Boylston, partially grant-funded.
   •   Data to support energy savings and rebate programs.
   •   Non-intrusive approach; no personal data collected, with option to request data removal.
   •   Survey flights planned for mid-April, with homeowner notifications in advance.

Customer Collections
   •   Collection efforts to increase following the Nov–Apr shutoff moratorium.
   •   Prior inconsistencies in enforcement contributed to rising bad debt expenses.
   •   Updated policies and internal coordination aim to ensure consistent and proactive collections.
   •   Requests made to review current policy.


Community Program
   •   “Library of Things” initiative provides energy monitoring devices (Kill A Watt) through the
       library.
