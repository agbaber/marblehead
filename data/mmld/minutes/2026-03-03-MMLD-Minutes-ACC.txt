EV Charging Policy

   Please refer to the slides for details of EV Public Chargers Policy changes proposal.
   •   Policy updates reflect increased utilization, peak demand challenges, and cost recovery
       issues, with the goal of making it possible to increase the number of chargers in a financially
       sustainable manner.
   •   Proposed changes include time-of-use pricing, overstay fees, and differentiated municipal
       rates.
   •   Broader goals include encouraging off-peak charging, improving availability, and increasing
       revenue, particularly from non-residents.
   •   Additional ideas discussed: resident vs. non-resident pricing, free resident charging with
       controls, and expanded infrastructure planning.
   •   Revised proposal to be brought back for a board vote at the March 31 meeting.


Strategic Priorities for the Department (over the next 18–24 Months)

The priorities discussed can be organized in three core focus areas:
             o Grid Modernization: necessary for reliability of system
             o Peak Demand Management: large impact on affordability
             o Inventory, Equipment, and Operational Planning for the Light Department
These priorities emphasize reliability, demand control, and operational efficiency. The Light
Department staff was instrumental in thinking through these priorities.

   Grid Modernization:
   •   Key initiatives include completing Village 13, addressing deferred maintenance, managing
       backyard poles, improving Wilkins, and advancing transmission undergrounding for
       reliability.

   Peak Demand Management:
   •   Focus on battery storage (BESS), dynamic rates, AMI evaluation, and Wilkins transition.
   •   Interest in expanding customer-side solutions such as solar, home batteries, EV integration,
       and demand response tools.
   •   Emphasis on customer communication and engagement as part of demand management.
