## Letter

**To the editor:**

Jack Buba's recent opinion piece included a chart comparing two health insurance time-series for Marblehead. The series labeled "MHDData" came from data I have compiled and published at marbleheaddata.org. I want to clarify what the other series is plotting, because the labels imply two things that the audited record does not support.

**The "$18 million" baseline for FY06.** That dataset is the Massachusetts Department of Revenue's Schedule A "Fixed Costs" rollup minus pension contributions, a state report category that bundles health insurance premiums with retiree health, workers compensation, life insurance, and Medicare employer share. Marblehead's audited group health insurance line for FY06, taken from the town's annual financial audit, is $7,964,196. Across each year's audit through FY12, the line climbs smoothly from $7.96M to $11.74M. There is no FY07 cliff in the audited series.

**The Group Insurance Commission switch timing.** The Public Employee Committee and Group Insurance Commission Memorandum of Agreement that transferred Marblehead's health subscribers to the state plan took effect July 1, 2012. The Select Board vote authorizing it was January 31, 2012, under M.G.L. c. 32B, &sect;19. The chart's drop occurs at FY07, five years before the actual transition. The audited group insurance line across the actual transition (FY12 to FY13) shows a one-time 7.8 percent dip from $11.74M to $10.82M, fully recouped by FY15.

**The cumulative figure.** A claim that the town has "saved $146 million" since the GIC switch takes the $18M FY06 baseline and projects savings forward. Neither the baseline nor the FY07 timing matches what Marblehead's own audits report. The audited group insurance line over the same window grew from $7.96M in FY06 to $13.92M in FY24.

The 83/17 contribution split, the upcoming agreement renewal, and the question of whether to renegotiate the town's premium share are legitimate things to discuss on their own merits. The audited numbers are publicly available in the town's annual financial reports.

A full source trace, including which Schedule A line was being plotted and how it compares year by year to the audited figure, is available on request.

Andrew Baber

## Drafting notes (not part of the letter)

- Did not engage Buba's policy alternatives (consolidate finance and HR, outsource the transfer station, privatize landscaping). Those are preferences, not data claims, and engaging them pulls the letter toward advocacy.

That one is worth a separate letter if I write one, because the wage-offset trade-off (lower employer share is generally exchanged for higher salaries in collective bargaining) is a real economic point but a longer one to make.
