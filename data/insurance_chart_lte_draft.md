## Letter

**To the editor:**

Jack Buba's recent opinion piece "Why Marblehead Should Reject the Override" includes a chart titled "Marblehead Annual Insurance Costs" with a "$146 Million Saved and Spent" annotation. The chart's argument is that the town spent $18M on insurance in FY2006, costs dropped after switching to the Group Insurance Commission, and the cumulative difference between a flat $18M baseline and actual costs amounts to $146 million. I want to flag three things in that chart that do not match Marblehead's own audited financial reports.

**The $18 million baseline for FY2006.** That figure is from the Massachusetts Department of Revenue's Schedule A "Fixed Costs" rollup minus pension contributions, a state-reporting category that bundles health insurance with retiree health, workers compensation, life insurance, and Medicare employer share. Marblehead's audited group health insurance line for FY2006, from the FY06 Annual Comprehensive Financial Report, is **$7,964,196**, less than half the chart's baseline. Across each year's audit through FY2012, the line climbs smoothly from $7.96M to $11.74M. There is no FY2007 cliff in the audited record.

**The timing of the GIC switch.** The Public Employee Committee and Group Insurance Commission Memorandum of Agreement that transferred Marblehead's health subscribers to the state plan took effect **July 1, 2012**, after the Select Board voted January 31, 2012 under M.G.L. c. 32B, &sect;19. The chart's drop happens at FY2007, five years before the actual transition. The audited group insurance line across the real transition (FY12 to FY13) shows a one-time 7.8 percent dip from $11.74M to $10.82M, fully recouped by FY2015. Real savings, modest scale.

**The "$146 million" calculation.** The cumulative-savings number is the area between a flat $18M red line and the chart's blue line over 21 years. It depends on the $18M baseline being correct and on the FY07 cliff being a real budget reduction. Neither is supported by the town's own audits. On the audited group insurance line, costs rose from $7.96M in FY2006 to $13.92M in FY2024 and are budgeted at $16.75M for FY2027.

The 83/17 employer-employee premium split, the upcoming agreement renewal, and whether to renegotiate the town's contribution share are all legitimate things to debate. The audited numbers are publicly available in the town's annual financial reports, which any resident can request from the Town Accountant.

A full source-trace, including which Schedule A line was being plotted and how it compares year by year to the audit, is available on request.

Andrew Baber

## Drafting notes (not part of the letter)

- Word count: approximately 410. Marblehead Independent's typical letter-to-the-editor limit is around 400 words; if they enforce that strictly, the easiest cuts are the introductory framing sentence and the closing paragraph about the 83/17 split (this letter focuses on data, not policy).
- The companion source-trace at [/data/insurance_chart_source_check](/data/insurance_chart_source_check) includes a side-by-side chart of the published series against the audited group insurance line. If a permanent home for the source-trace is wanted, it can be merged into the live site under a neutral title and the letter can link to it directly instead of saying "available on request."
- Did not engage Buba's policy alternatives (consolidate finance and HR, outsource the transfer station, privatize landscaping). Those are preferences, not data claims, and engaging them pulls the letter toward advocacy.
- Did not push back on the 83 percent versus 76 percent contribution-share comparison. That is worth a separate letter if it gets written; the wage-offset trade-off (a lower employer share is generally exchanged for higher salaries in collective bargaining) is a real economic point but a longer one to make.
