Monthly spending and budget pacing

A first-draft view of FY26 monthly spending, variance to budget, and town cash balance, built from data the town has already published.

First draft
This is a first draft of showing what monthly spending, variance to budget, and the town's cash balance could look like, built from data the town has already published. Those reports exist internally but aren't published externally. Below is the closest version possible from public data: a department-level pacing table (current YTD vs. straight-line target), a monthly burn chart by fund, and the annual free-cash series. Each section ends with what's missing for the real version.

1. Variance to budget, by department

FY26 revised budget vs. year-to-date actual.

Department Revised budget YTD actual % used vs. 91%
How to read the pacing column. “+8% ahead” means the department's spend is 8 points past where straight-line pacing would put it, not 8% over budget. Some categories never pace linearly and the column isn't meaningful for them: debt service (a few fixed payment dates per year, mostly early), capital articles (lumpy draws, often year-end), harbor (seasonal), snow removal (winter only). The schools, fire, police, DPW, and water/sewer lines are where the pacing read tells you something useful.

What's missing for the real version

  • Encumbrances. A department can look "under-spent" because purchase orders are still open. The Town Accountant's internal report shows expended + encumbered + remaining; this view only shows expended.
  • Per-month budget. Even for departments that do roughly spend monthly, a flat 1/12 target is a coarse benchmark; the real internal report compares to a department's own historical month-by-month profile.
  • Frequency. This snapshot is from June 1. There is no published monthly feed.

2. Monthly cash-out, by fund

Cumulative vendor payments by month. Dashed line is straight-line target.

Source Town of Marblehead Vendor Checkbook Data as of … (daily auto-refresh)
Payroll isn't here. The vendor checkbook only includes payments to outside companies, not municipal salaries (which run through a separate payroll system). General Fund – Town below captures roughly 80% of actual town spend; the remaining ~20% is police, fire, DPW, and town hall payroll. General Fund – School is dropped from this chart entirely: the checkbook only captures ~22% of actual school spend (the rest is teacher and staff payroll). The schools-side YTD total in the pacing table above is complete.

What's missing for the real version

  • Monthly payroll feed. See the note above – once monthly payroll totals by department are available, those would layer onto this chart and close the biggest gap.
  • Cadence. The underlying CSV refreshes daily, so this chart tracks the prior business day. It's not a per-pay-period payroll cadence; the town would need to publish that separately.
  • Lumpy by design. Some funds spike in July (debt service prepay), some at year-end (capital draws). A flat 1/12-per-month line is the wrong comparison for most of them.

3. Town cash balance

Year-end certified free cash, FY15–FY26. One value per year, certified after the prior fiscal year closes.

Source Massachusetts DOR DLS Gateway (FreeCash2 report) Cadence Annual · certified in the fall after the FY closes

What's missing for the real version

  • Monthly cash position. The Treasurer tracks pooled cash continuously. Nothing month-by-month is published. Free cash is a year-end DOR-certified figure, not a running balance.
  • Investments and reserves breakdown. The Annual Report's Treasurer section gives one year-end pooled cash figure. Stabilization Fund and OPEB Trust balances are in the ACFR, also annually.

What it would take to publish this for real

If the board decided to start sharing this externally each month:

  • Monthly variance report. Town Accountant exports a department-by-department expended + encumbered + remaining at month-end. PDF or CSV posted to the Finance page after each month closes.
  • Monthly Treasurer cash report. Pooled cash, broken out by general fund / enterprise funds / trust funds / restricted. One number per category, end-of-month.
  • Checkbook auto-publish. Right now the vendor checkbook only exports on demand. A monthly automated CSV drop would make a chart like the one above maintainable without a manual pull.
