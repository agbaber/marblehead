Where the money comes from

Most of what the town spends is paid by you, on the house you live in. The rest fills small gaps.

What you'll know after this
  • The three sources that fund Marblehead's $123M budget
  • Why Proposition 2½ is the central constraint on revenue growth
  • Why state aid is small for towns like Marblehead

Marblehead collects about $123 million a year to run itself. Roughly four-fifths is the property tax bill you pay twice a year. The rest is a mix of state aid, local fees, and smaller sources that change with the economy.

Why the cap matters

Proposition 2½ — a 1980 state law — limits how much the property-tax pot can grow each year. The pot can rise 2.5%, plus new construction added to the tax rolls. Anything beyond that requires an override: a separate ballot vote.

That cap is the reason the budget conversation feels constrained. Costs that aren't capped — health insurance, special education, pensions — grow at 5–9% a year. Revenue that is capped grows at 2.5%. The gap compounds.
