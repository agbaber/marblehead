Budget Planning and Reporting Framework (00:02:00–00:10:00)

a. Committee reviewed spreadsheet tracking out-of-district tuition and transportation costs,
showing 12.5% average annual increase over seven years.
   • Funding sources include circuit breaker reimbursement, IDEA grants, special
   education revolving funds, prepaid amounts, and general fund appropriations.
   • Exercise aims to clarify funding sources and demonstrate increasing pressure on
   general fund as circuit breaker and IDEA grant growth does not keep pace with cost
   increases.
b. Committee discussed difficulty of projecting out-of-district costs three years in advance
due to volatility in student enrollment and needs; agreed documenting assumptions for
three-year forecast is necessary for broader budget planning exercise.
c. General fund contribution to out-of-district costs for FY27 projected to approach $4.5
million.

FY27 School Department Budget Planning and Reporting (00:10:00–00:43:00)

a. Committee reviewed draft spreadsheet organizing FY26 salary/wage data by school and
category, sourced from final FinCom-approved FY25 and FY26 budgets.
   • Discussed refining categorization using existing budget codes for school location,
   position type, and department.
   • Michael Pfifferling noted certain positions miscoded; accurate live data unavailable
   until FY27.
b. Discussed challenges categorizing special education staff, EL teachers, school adjustment
counselors, and itinerant providers (OT, PT, speech).
   • Some positions coded district-wide for staffing flexibility based on changing student
   needs.
c. Reviewed RADAR enrollment data showing 22% district decline over 10 years, consistent
with 20% decline in Marblehead school-age children; statewide enrollment declined 5%
year over year.
d. Discussed limitations of state-reported data, including FTE and enrollment discrepancies;
RADAR data approximately two years behind.
e. Analysis purpose is to validate current staffing levels based on student needs, class sizes,
and caseloads, not assume proportional reductions based solely on enrollment declines; will
inform FY27 budget development and multi-year projections through FY28 and FY29.

Enrollment Trends and Demographic Analysis (00:43:00–01:21:00)

a. Committee discussed analyzing staffing levels using FTE data rather than dollar amounts
to validate current staffing based on student needs, class sizes, and caseloads.
   • Current data systems contain inaccuracies that will not be fully resolved until Munis
   implementation in FY27.
b. Committee discussed developing three budget scenarios: level service funding,
recommended budget reflecting identified efficiencies, and level-funded (zero increase)
budget showing impact on class sizes and programs.
   • Recommended budget should reflect district needs after identifying efficiencies, not
   what town can afford.
   • Committee emphasized implementing stated cuts if additional funding not provided.
c. Committee discussed class size targets and establishing clear thresholds for when
additional teachers are required.
   • Current class sizes in some grades average 17 students; analysis should consider
   different standards for elementary, middle, and high school levels.
d. Committee reviewed strategies to reduce general fund pressure, including strategic use of
revolving funds and special education circuit breaker reserves.
   • Discussed moving additional costs into revolving funds where appropriate, such as
   athletics fees and kindergarten instructional assistant salaries.
e. Committee discussed out-of-district special education costs and goal of creating in-district
programs.
   • Creating appropriate in-district programs is a two to two-and-a-half year process.
   • District has no control over out-of-district tuition rates set by state's Operational
   Services Division, which have increased by as much as 14% in recent years.
f. Committee discussed full-day kindergarten funding; Marblehead is one of very few
Massachusetts districts charging fees for full-day kindergarten.
   • Eliminating kindergarten fees would cost approximately $650,000 annually.
