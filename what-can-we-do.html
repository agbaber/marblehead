What can we do?

A working inventory of twelve specific measures Marblehead could pursue in the next 24 to 36 months, alongside or instead of an override. Each one answers two questions: have we tried this, and what is the potential.

5 revenue ideas 5 cost reductions 2 governance changes Cautions How adoption happens

Plausible scale, summed across items • Order of magnitude

$2–5M

per year in new revenue plus avoided cost, if the highest-leverage items land. Not a forecast. An inventory of what is technically and procedurally available.

Every range below is an estimate with explicit research questions attached. Where the citation is missing, the number is a starting point for research, not a finding. Send corrections to the contact at the bottom of this page.

Revenue • 5 ideas

Money the town could raise.

Local-option taxes, fee structures, and existing assets the town could monetize more aggressively. Several of these have already been voted on and narrowly lost.

01 · Local Option Tax

Adopt the room and short-term rental occupancy excise

$200K–$500K/yr

At the 6 percent statutory cap. Range depends on hotel and STR inventory.

Have we tried this?

Narrowly rejectedArticle 25, May 2023 ATM: 391 Yes / 394 No. Three-vote margin. Not on a warrant since.

What is the potential?

Hotels plus inns plus B&Bs plus STRs at 6 percent under G.L. c. 64G § 3A. Stacks on top of the 5.7 percent state excise.

4 open research questions
  • Active STR listing count and revenue from AirDNA or the state STR registry
  • Current licensed hotel and B&B room inventory from the Building and Health Departments
  • Comparable annual room excise collections from Salem, Rockport, Manchester, Cohasset, Nahant (DOR Schedule A)
  • DLS technical assistance memo, if requested by the CFO

02 · Local Option Tax

Community Impact Fee on professionally managed STRs

$20K–$75K/yr

3 percent on operators with 2+ short-term rentals. Statute requires 35%+ to affordable housing.

Have we tried this?

Not separatelyTypically bundled with the room excise above; not separately presented at ATM.

What is the potential?

Modest. Applies only to multi-unit operators in the Commonwealth, so the base is much narrower than the room excise.

2 open research questions
  • Count of professionally managed STR units in Marblehead from the state DOR registry
  • Average rent per night for those units from AirDNA

03 · Existing Asset

Increase the MMLD PILOT beyond the FY27 bump

$150K–$400K/yr

Additional headroom above the recent $330K to $360K increase, benchmarked against peer municipal utilities.

Have we tried this?

PartialPILOT from MMLD to the General Fund was raised from $330,000 to $360,000 in the FY27 budget. The first increase in years.

What is the potential?

Depends on peer-utility PILOT-to-revenue ratios. Functionally a transfer from ratepayers to taxpayers, see cautions below.

3 open research questions
  • MMLD annual operating revenue and net position from the most recent ACFR
  • PILOT-to-revenue ratios from Wellesley, Belmont, Concord, Hingham, Mansfield, Reading
  • Light Commission posture on a further increase

04 · Existing Asset

Audit and revalue the boat excise base

$25K–$75K/yr

Assessor-administered. One-time catch-up plus modest ongoing lift.

Have we tried this?

UnknownNo reference found in annual reports or budget documents.

What is the potential?

Marblehead has a large recreational fleet and the excise can drift from current market values.

3 open research questions
  • Current boat excise commitment and collections from the Assessor
  • Date of the last revaluation
  • Sample comparison of assessed values to current market values

05 · Existing Asset

Mooring waitlist transfer fee

$25K–$100K/yr

Politically contentious. The waitlist is decades long and is viewed as a heritage benefit.

Have we tried this?

PartialMooring fees were raised at the 2022 ATM (harbor $8 to $10/ft, other $7 to $9). The waitlist itself has not been monetized.

What is the potential?

Depends on waitlist turnover. Harbor pricing in general is below market for the supply constraint.

2 open research questions
  • Waitlist length, average wait time, and annual turnover from the Harbormaster
  • Whether a transfer fee is permissible under current bylaws or requires an amendment

Cost reduction • 5 ideas

Money the town could stop spending.

Structural cost work and procurement moves available within existing board authority. Several of these can begin without any Town Meeting vote.

06 · Retiree Benefits

Move Medicare-eligible retirees to a Group Medicare Advantage plan

$300K–$900K/yr

Town share, after factoring premium split. Likely the single largest fast-impact lever.

Have we tried this?

Not triedNo record of a feasibility study or PEC consultation. The town's own page is explicit that no operational efficiency review of retiree benefits has been conducted.

What is the potential?

Leave GIC for the Medicare-eligible cohort and procure a Group MA PPO directly. Downsides include network restrictions, prior auth, and federal CMS rate volatility; see cautions.

5 open research questions
  • FY25 retiree census split by Medicare-eligible (post-65) vs. pre-65 via PRR to CFO
  • Current enrollment distribution by GIC plan for post-65 retirees
  • Town vs. retiree premium share by hire date
  • RFI to 2-3 carriers (Tufts, Blue Cross MA, Aetna, Humana) for an equivalent Group MA PPO
  • RFI to 4-5 actuarial firms (Segal, Aon, Mercer, USI, Hilb) for a feasibility study

07 · Energy

Energy Savings Performance Contract on schools and town buildings

$250K–$500K/yr

In avoided energy cost. Plus $2-4M of deferred maintenance funded from guaranteed savings, not capital.

Have we tried this?

Not on buildingsMMLD received a $1.3M federal grid-resilience grant, but that is not a buildings-side ESPC.

What is the potential?

Town procures an ESCO under G.L. c. 25A § 11I and the MA DOER framework. ESCO eats the difference if savings underperform. Zero upfront capex.

4 open research questions
  • Five-year electric load profiles per municipal and school building from MMLD
  • Five-year heating fuel and oil consumption from DPW and the school facilities director
  • DOER Green Communities preliminary energy assessment (free)
  • Indicative scope from 2-3 pre-qualified ESCOs (Ameresco, Honeywell, Trane, ENGIE, Schneider)

08 · Operations

Town and school shared back office

$200K–$400K/yr

Attrition-based consolidation of finance, IT, HR, and procurement over 24-36 months.

Have we tried this?

Partial2024 CliftonLarsonAllen Finance Assessment touched the town finance side: 15 of 23 recommendations completed by 2024-03-27. School side and joint structure untouched.

What is the potential?

Town and school sides currently run parallel back offices. Higher savings possible if a single ERP replaces parallel systems.

4 open research questions
  • Headcount and salary for finance, IT, HR, procurement on each side
  • List of software systems run separately by each side
  • CliftonLarsonAllen Finance Department Assessment full report
  • Select Board and School Committee posture on a joint MOU

09 · Regional

Inter-municipal agreement with a neighbor town

$50K–$150K/yr

Per shared role under G.L. c. 40 § 4A. First candidates: assistant assessor, veterans agent, alternate building inspector.

Have we tried this?

Not doneThe town's own page states no shared-service agreements exist with Swampscott or Salem for dispatch, IT, procurement, HR, finance, or inspections.

What is the potential?

Statutory authorization exists under G.L. c. 40 § 4A. Each shared role saves a fraction of an FTE on each town's side.

3 open research questions
  • Current cost of each candidate role on the Marblehead side
  • Posture from Swampscott and Salem on a 4A partnership
  • Existing 4A precedent on the North Shore for reference structure

10 · Operations

Modernize permitting and inspections software

$50K–$100K/yr

Through 0.5 FTE saved on counter staffing plus accelerated permit revenue recognition.

Have we tried this?

PartialOpenGov adopted for procurement (100% of formal bids electronic as of 2025). Munis runs financials. No dedicated permitting platform.

What is the potential?

Likely a natural extension of the existing OpenGov footprint. Alternatives: Energov, Tyler.

3 open research questions
  • Current permit volume and average cycle time
  • Vendor quotes for OpenGov permitting module, Energov, or Tyler
  • Inspectional Services staffing baseline

Governance and process • 2 ideas

How decisions get made.

No direct dollar impact from these articles, but they are the institutional vehicles that protect the items above from being picked off individually.

11 · Bylaw

Efficiency and Shared Services Commission with formal standing

$500K–$2M/yr indirect

Indirect potential, based on what comparable commissions in peer MA municipalities have surfaced.

Have we tried this?

Not doneNo standing efficiency commission. The CliftonLarsonAllen assessment was a one-time consulting engagement, not a recurring body.

What is the potential?

The article itself produces no dollars; the commission produces them. Annual reporting requirement creates a forcing function.

3 open research questions
  • Sample bylaw language from peer towns that stood up similar bodies
  • Composition appropriate to Marblehead (size, terms, appointing authority)
  • Reporting cadence and forcing function tied to budget cycle

12 · Budget Process

Zero-based budgeting on the top three cost centers

$500K–$1.5M/cycle

One-time per cycle. Declining as baselines reset. Most useful once every 5 years per department.

Have we tried this?

Discussed, not adoptedSchool Committee twice discussed it (2022-07-19 and 2025-10-17). 3-year forecast for FY26-28 exists, but ZBB does not.

What is the potential?

Schools, Police, Fire account for about 70% of operating. Resetting baselines once per cycle on each.

3 open research questions
  • Past ZBB precedents in MA municipal practice
  • Departmental appetite from Schools, Police, Fire
  • Resource cost of running ZBB (typically 1 FTE-quarter per major department)

Important cautions

This is not a list of free money.

Several entries carry real downsides that deserve to sit on the page next to the upside.

Medicare Advantage carries real member impact.

Network restrictions, prior authorization friction, and provider-relationship disruption for snowbird and multi-state retirees. CMS rate compression since 2024 is shrinking the savings opportunity over time. The decision is not reversible cheaply.

Room and STR excise affects visitor costs.

At the 6 percent cap, the burden is modest relative to seasonal pricing, but the political case has to be made on its own terms. Local lodging operators will be vocal.

Efficiency commissions are mixed.

Some MA towns produce material findings; others produce reports that sit on shelves. Composition, reporting cadence, and a forcing function determine the outcome.

MMLD PILOT is a transfer, not new money.

Functionally it moves dollars from ratepayers to taxpayers. Overlapping populations, but not identical, and the ratepayer side is also organized.

Shared services save money slowly.

Most savings show up through attrition over multiple years, not headcount cuts in year one. The political timeline often outruns the operational one.

All ranges are estimates.

Every dollar figure on this page is order-of-magnitude until research arrives. The bold numbers without their caveats can mislead. Every claim should be traceable to a primary source; where it is not, that is a bug.

How adoption happens

Most of this needs no Town Meeting vote.

Three procedural paths. Two of these items need Town Meeting; the rest can begin at the next board meeting.

Path A · Board can start now

No Town Meeting required.

  • Medicare Advantage migration (06)
  • ESPC on buildings (07)
  • Town/school shared back office (08)
  • IMA with neighbor town (09)
  • Permitting modernization (10)
  • Boat excise revaluation (04)
  • MMLD PILOT renegotiation (03)

Path B · Annual or Special TM

Either ATM or STM works.

  • Room and STR occupancy excise (01)
  • Community Impact Fee (02)
  • Efficiency Commission bylaw (11)
  • Any bylaw amendment

A Special Town Meeting can be called by the Select Board or by petition of 200 voters. Once warned, 10 voters can add an article.

Path C · Annual TM only

Locked to the Annual.

  • The FY operating budget itself
  • Officer elections at the annual town election

Override ballot questions can go on STM warrants. Only the budget appropriation must wait for ATM.
