Mockup C · Dashboard

Marblehead's operating budget grew 2.7× over the last two decades.
Headcount barely moved.

Open with the trend, decompose the drivers, end with the structure that produced them. Same arc as a dataroom briefing.

Brainstorming mockup. Numbers approximate.

Step 1 · The trend

Operating budget: $47M to $127M.

Twenty-one fiscal years of nominal growth. About 5% per year compounded. The line accelerates after FY16 as health insurance and pension assessments grow above the 2.5% levy cap.

Step 2 · Decompose

Three forces, only one moved.

The budget grew 170%. Headcount grew 2%. Loaded cost per FTE grew about 90%. CPI grew about 50%. Whatever is driving the budget, it isn't more people.

Step 3 · The stack

Per-FTE growth lives in the benefit stack.

Salary lines grew with collective bargaining at 2-3% per year. Pension and health insurance, layered on top, grew faster. The cumulative stack on every salaried position is ~33% of base.

Step 4 · Composition

The mix churned underneath.

Special-education-related headcount grew. IT was created from zero. Clerical compressed. Public safety and DPW stayed within their long-run ranges. The total stayed close to its starting band.

Step 5 · Today's snapshot

FY26: ~80% people, ~15% set bills, ~5% everything else.

The familiar department breakdown obscures more than it reveals. Schools, police, DPW, and the library are all mostly the same shape of cost: salary plus the locked benefit stack on top.

Step 6 · Who's in charge

Two pillars, one tax base.

Town side from Select Board to TA to departments. School side from School Committee to Superintendent to schools. FinCom reviews both, recommends to Town Meeting. Voters elect Town Meeting, both boards, the Moderator.

Step 7 · The unpaid roster

~57 residents serve on the elected and appointed boards.

None draws a salary. Between them they hire the senior managers, recommend the budget, set zoning, run the warrant, decide every land-use dispute, and govern the utility.
