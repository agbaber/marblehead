Following the taxpayer dollar
through Marblehead

A primer on where the town's $127 million operating budget actually goes, who decides, and what's locked in by the time anyone votes.

Scroll down

Chapter 1 · Where it goes

Marblehead spends about $127 million a year.

Most of it pays people. Some of it is bills the town doesn't control. A small slice is everything else. The shape of the spending hasn't changed much in twenty years; the size has nearly tripled.

Three centuries of town-meeting government still set the rules. The choices that move the budget happen in places most residents never see.

Chapter 2 · The cut

The dollar splits three ways.

About 80¢ ends up as compensation. About 15¢ is set bills the town doesn't control: debt service, OPEB amortization, state assessments (Essex Tech, MBTA, charter schools). The remaining 5¢ is supplies, contractors, programs, and capital scope.

The split is what makes "cuts" so difficult. The locked share doesn't move when revenue is short.

Chapter 3 · The workforce

Two parallel organizations, one tax base.

The town side runs from the elected Select Board through the hired Town Administrator to the departments. The school side runs from the elected School Committee through the hired Superintendent to the schools.

Each FTE is roughly one dot. Schools left, town right. The two pillars don't connect at the top; voters elect both boards directly.

By the numbers

2.7×

Operating-budget growth from FY05 to FY26. From $47 million to $127 million in nominal dollars. About 5% per year compounded.

By the numbers

+2%

FTE growth over the same span. Headcount has stayed within a 200-person band the entire time. The budget growth came from cost per employee.

Chapter 4 · Twenty years

The total grew. The mix moved.

Special-education support staff grew with student needs. Information technology was built from zero. Clerical compressed. Public safety and DPW stayed within their long-run ranges. Per-FTE loaded cost grew faster than CPI in nearly every year, mostly from health-insurance premium growth.

In closing

Most of the budget is already committed
before anyone votes.

Locked bills, contractual stacks on every employee, lump-sum School Committee allocation, capital scope from prior years. The annual choice space is small. Knowing how small is the first step in any honest budget conversation.
