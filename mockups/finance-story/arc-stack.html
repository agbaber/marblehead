Mockup A · Editorial

Marblehead spends $127 million a year.
Where does it go?

Follow one dollar from the levy through the people it pays. About eight scroll-stops, ~3 minutes of reading.

Brainstorming mockup. Numbers are approximations; the final treatment will tie every figure to a primary source per site convention.

Step 1 · A dollar

Start with a single dollar from your tax bill.

About 75¢ of every dollar Marblehead collects comes from the property-tax levy. The rest is state aid and local receipts. Same destination.

Step 2 · The three shapes

Almost every dollar pays a person.

About 80¢ of every dollar the town spends ends up as compensation, salary plus the automatic stack of benefits on top. About 15¢ is set bills the town doesn't control: debt service, state assessments, OPEB.

The remaining 5¢ is everything else: supplies, contractor services, programs, fleet, building maintenance, capital scope.

Step 3 · The stack on every person

A $75K hire is closer to $100K to the budget.

Every full-time employee comes with an automatic stack: a pension contribution to Essex Regional Retirement (~17% of pay), the town's share of the GIC health-insurance premium, Medicare, workers' comp, life.

That stack is locked by the GIC's rate cycle, by collective bargaining, and by state law. So when premiums rise 8% in a year, the line item rises across every FTE on the payroll.

Step 4 · How many people

About 1,020 people work for the town and schools.

FY26 figures. Roughly 626 on the school side, 394 on the town side. Counts have hovered between about 1,020 and 1,210 for two decades, with a known FY23 reporting glitch and the post-2024 budget pressure on the way down.

Step 5 · The hierarchy

All of them report up to one of two elected boards.

The town side runs through the Select Board to the Town Administrator to the departments. The school side runs through the School Committee to the Superintendent to the schools. The two pillars don't connect at the top. They share a single tax base and not much else.

FinCom reviews both. It's appointed by the Town Moderator and is the only body that looks across both sides before Town Meeting votes.

Step 6 · The unpaid layer

The decision layer is unpaid.

Around the perimeter of the paid org chart sit about sixty residents: Select Board, School Committee, FinCom, Planning, Conservation, ZBA, Board of Health, Recreation, Light Board, the Moderator.

They write the warrant, set zoning, recommend the budget, hire the senior managers, and decide every dispute over what gets built and where. None of them draws a salary for it.

Step 7 · Twenty years

Total headcount stayed roughly flat. Composition changed underneath.

School support staff grew as special-education needs grew. IT was created from zero. Clerical roles compressed. Public safety and DPW stayed close to flat. Library, recreation, and COA programming have all moved in narrow ranges.

Per-FTE loaded cost grew faster than CPI in nearly every year, mostly driven by health-insurance premium growth.
