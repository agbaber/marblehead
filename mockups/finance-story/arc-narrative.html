Mockup v3 · Narrative motion

Marblehead spends $127 million a year.
Where does it go?

Same arc as before; this time every transition is choreographed to make an argument. Each motion is a sentence. Built to work on any screen size.

Brainstorming mockup. Numbers approximate.

Step 1

Start with a single dollar.

About 75¢ of every dollar Marblehead collects comes from the property-tax levy. Follow it.

Step 2 · The cut

The dollar splits three ways.

About 80¢ of every dollar ends up paying a person. About 15¢ is set bills the town doesn't control: debt service, OPEB, state assessments. The remaining 5¢ is supplies, contractors, programs, capital.

Step 3 · Zoom into “people”

Every salary carries an automatic stack.

Watch the pieces build. Pension at ~17% of pay. Then the town's share of the GIC health premium. Then Medicare. Then workers' comp and life. The base salary line at the bottom is what the employee sees on their paycheck. The whole stack is what the budget pays.

Step 4 · Multiply

Now do that ~1,020 times.

Each dot is one full-time-equivalent position on the FY26 payroll. About 626 on the school side, 394 on the town side, color-coded by category.

Step 5 · The split

School-side dots flow right. Town-side dots flow left.

The two pillars don't connect at the top. Voters elect both boards directly; the Select Board hires the Town Administrator, the School Committee hires the Superintendent, and neither one supervises the other.

Step 6 · Decision layer drops in

Sixty unpaid residents sit above all of it.

Select Board, School Committee, FinCom, Planning, Conservation, ZBA, Recreation, Board of Health, Light Board, Library Trustees, Moderator. None draws a salary. Between them they write the warrant, set zoning, hire the senior managers, and recommend every dollar in the budget.

Step 7 · Twenty years

The total barely moved. The mix moved a lot.

School support staff grew with special-education needs. IT was created from zero. Clerical compressed. Public safety and DPW stayed within their long-run ranges. Per-FTE loaded cost grew faster than CPI in nearly every year, mostly from health-insurance premium growth.
