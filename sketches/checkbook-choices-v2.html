A choice still to be made

Snow Removal is +$582K over its FY26 appropriation (654% overrun)

Town Meeting voted the original snow appropriation last May; this winter cost more than planned. Under MA law, an overrun has to be closed by a supplemental appropriation at a Special Town Meeting. The vote isn't “should we have plowed” — the plowing already happened. It's about which other line gets reduced, or whether free cash absorbs it.

About this sketch

All bucket totals are real, computed from the checkbook_FY26_2026-06-09.csv ledger. Fund-to-vote provenance is in data/checkbook_fund_provenance.json (top 20 funds, 97.8% of paid spend). Vote citations cross-reference data/dor_debt_exclusion_all.csv (MA DOR Proposition 2½ Debt Exclusion Votes) for the ballot bucket.

The carve-out inside the Town Meeting card is the substantive finding: only 14.3% of vendor checks out of General Fund — Town are actually subject to year-to-year Town Meeting discretion. The other 85.7% (GIC + pension + bond debt + state assessments) is set by outside boards or prior contracts. Payroll, the largest discretionary line, is paid via a separate system and isn't in the ledger — that's a real limitation of using the ledger alone.

Things that change vs. today's live /checkbook/: the 5-toggle “Budget vs actual” section is gone, replaced by the choice-bucketed stacked bar plus the Snow Removal callout. “Top vendors” ranked-bar list is gone (it mixes apples and oranges — Commonwealth of MA is GIC + Cherry Sheet, Berkshire Wind is Light Dept wholesale, neither is “a vendor we paid for services” in the resident's sense). The 15,364-row data table is demoted to a disclosure below the fold.
