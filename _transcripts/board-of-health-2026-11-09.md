    summary: |
      The board held an extended discussion on the social hosting bylaw, with the town's legal representative summarizing her review of the bylaw, the Massachusetts social hosting law, and a prior town counsel opinion. Key findings:
      
      - The bylaw's penalties are too small to deter violations.
      - Age limits in the bylaw are inconsistent with state law.
      - A 'lack of knowledge is not a defense' provision is vague and potentially unenforceable, though unlikely to be struck down by the attorney general.
      - The bylaw is civil rather than criminal; violations leave no record.
      - The state law, by contrast, has real teeth.
      
      The legal representative's conclusion was that the most effective approach would be to **repeal the bylaw entirely**, leaving only the stronger state law in place. However, she cautioned this is a 'heavier lift' at town meeting.
      
      Board members noted:
      - The current police chief views the bylaw as a tool he wants available.
      - Enforcement has been effectively nonexistent; the few tickets issued may not have gone to Marblehead residents.
      - Mothers Against Drunk Driving have been contacted and consider 2027 town meeting critical.
      - The town meeting warrant opens in November and closes in January.
      
      The board agreed to:
      1. Postpone further town counsel consultation for now.
      2. Have a board member prepare a scientific/neurological profile of teen alcohol harm for the November 23 meeting.
      3. Research whether surrounding towns have similar bylaws.
      4. Keep the social hosting topic as a standing agenda item.
      5. Build community coalition support (including MADD) before advancing a warrant article.
      
      No vote was taken on the bylaw itself.
