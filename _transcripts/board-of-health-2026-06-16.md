      The bulk of the meeting was a detailed public discussion of Marblehead's transition to automated curbside collection under a new contract with Republic Services, effective July 1.
      
      **Key contract and operational details explained by staff:**
      - The town is staying with Republic Services and moving to automated collection using carts owned by the town.
      - Standard allocation: one 65-gallon trash cart and one 95-gallon recycling cart per eligible unit (single through four-family residential).
      - Weekly recycling collection was chosen over every-other-week, in part due to resident feedback following the prior year's strike.
      - The historic/Old Town downtown district will continue with manual (non-automated) collection due to narrow streets; automated trucks handle the remaining four days of routes.
      - The town ordered 500 smaller 35-gallon trash carts and 500 smaller 65-gallon recycling carts, prioritized first for Old Town residents, then elderly and mobility-impaired residents elsewhere. Residents must email Health2@marbleheadma.gov to request smaller carts.
      - Carts are assigned to the address, not the resident, and switching to a smaller cart is generally not reversible.
      - Old barrels from the previous contract are being redistributed; a grassroots effort has placed approximately 500 barrels with Peabody residents who still use manual collection.
      - Disposal cost for returned old barrels: approximately $116 per ton; estimated 45–90 tons total.
      - New cart procurement cost was noted as over $900,000.
      - Printing/packet costs for the rollout: $16,200.
      
      **Public concerns raised:**
      - Multiple residents in Old Town and on hills described difficulty moving 95-gallon carts long distances or storing large carts on small properties.
      - Elderly residents cited physical limitations (joint replacements, narrow driveways, steep terrain) as barriers.
      - One resident on Mill Pond described a ~100-yard carry to Beacon Street with a stone wall and winter snow berms.
      - Concerns about the ballot question language for the override being unclear (voted June 9).
      - Criticism that communication about the rollout was insufficient, including calls for better use of social media and the town's inactive Board of Health Facebook page.
      - Neighbors sharing barrels was clarified as now permissible since the program is tax-funded rather than fee-based.
      
      **Business curbside collection vote:**
      The board voted 4-0 to allow eligible non-food businesses (no food establishments, no liquor stores, no chemical businesses) to subscribe to curbside collection at **$160 per month** for one 65-gallon trash cart and one 95-gallon recycling cart. Implementation will begin when staff capacity allows, not necessarily by July 1. Approximately 165 downtown businesses were cited as potentially eligible. Staff noted a waste revolving account may be proposed at a future town meeting to hold business subscription revenue.
      
      **Transfer station update:**
      Construction at the transfer station is substantially complete; gate installation and striping remain. The project was described as improving separation of residential and commercial traffic flows.

      A board member delivered a public health update on tick-borne diseases. Key points:
      
      - Lyme disease (caused by *Borrelia burgdorferi*, transmitted by the deer tick *Ixodes scapularis*) is the most common tick-borne illness in Massachusetts; approximately 30,000–50,000 cases per year nationally.
      - Other diseases discussed: Rocky Mountain spotted fever (20–30% mortality, rare locally), and alpha-gal syndrome (an acquired allergy to mammalian products affecting ~500,000 Americans, increasing northward).
      - Prevention: avoid tall grass, use 0.5% permethrin on clothing, use EPA-approved repellents (DEET, picaridin), wear light-colored clothing, tuck pants into socks, perform thorough tick checks.
      - Removal: fine-tipped tweezers, grasp close to skin, pull upward steadily; do not use Vaseline, oil, or matches.
      - Single-dose doxycycline prophylaxis is effective if the tick is a deer tick attached 36+ hours and removed within 72 hours, in a Lyme-prevalent area.
      - A Lyme vaccine is in development by Pfizer.
      - Bathing beach water quality testing occurs weekly; closures will be posted at the beach, on the town website, and on the Marblehead Police Department Facebook page.
