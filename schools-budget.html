Methodology

Panel 1: audited actuals, fiscal year 2001 to fiscal year 2024

Numbers in Panel 1 come from data/education_expenditure_FY01-24.csv, which was compiled from twenty-four consecutive Marblehead Annual Comprehensive Financial Reports (ACFRs). For each fiscal year, the value is the Education line from that year's Schedule of Expenditures by Function. Source pages per ACFR are documented in data/SOURCE_LOOKUP.md.

Two years get inline annotations rather than being treated as trend points. Fiscal year 2022 reflects a likely Governmental Accounting Standards Board pension or OPEB liability adjustment. Fiscal year 2023 includes prior-year adjustments per the auditor's note in the source CSV. Both are one-time reporting effects. Excluding them, the underlying trend fiscal year 2015 to fiscal year 2024 is broadly linear.

Panel 2: superintendent's proposed budget by category, fiscal year 2026 and fiscal year 2027

Panel 2 uses data/mps_proposed_budget_by_category.csv, extracted by scripts/extract_mps_proposed_budget.mjs from the February 5, 2026 School Committee meeting packet 2 (the only Feb packet used in this v1). Each of the twelve rows carries the source packet slug and the packet line numbers where each section subtotal appears, along with an extraction_confidence field. All twelve rows in v1 are marked high confidence because they come from a native (non-scanned) PDF whose section subtotals are legible without OCR.

The six buckets are DESE-Chart-of-Accounts-inspired but pragmatic for what the packet actually breaks out:

  • Regular instruction: teachers, aides, textbooks, classroom supplies. Broken out per school in the packet.
  • Special education: SPED specialists, BCBAs, and out-of-district tuitions. Managed at the district level.
  • Student services: nurses, counselors, guidance, library, athletics, cocurricular.
  • Operations: facilities, transportation, technology infrastructure.
  • Administration: central office and business office.
  • Capital: equipment purchases. Small in this budget.

Bucket names loosely track the DESE Chart of Accounts function codes (1000 through 9000, minus Fixed Charges); the exact mapping per packet line is in the extractor script.

Fixed Charges (DESE 5000) is not included. Health insurance, retirement contributions, and Medicare are budgeted at the town level in Marblehead, not on the school side. See the Group Insurance pad finding for the operational reason this matters, and for why proposed and actual education totals will differ by more than a straight comparison would suggest.

Why the Panel 2 total does not equal the packet's headline

The February 5, 2026 packet reports a fiscal year 2027 grand total of $49,120,285, the level-funded target set by the town. Panel 2's bucket sums are higher: about $48.6 million for fiscal year 2026 and $50.0 million for fiscal year 2027. Two separate effects account for the gap.

  1. Fixed Charges are in the packet's grand total but excluded from the six buckets. That accounts for roughly $883,000 in fiscal year 2026 and $901,000 in fiscal year 2027.
  2. The bucket sums here are the level-funded gross before end-of-cycle reductions. The packet's grand total is the adopted budget after roughly $1.6 million in cuts were applied entity-by-entity (pages 14 through 30 of the packet). Because the cuts are listed at the entity level, not the DESE-function level, they cannot be cleanly attributed to individual buckets without reconstructing every position line. Follow-up work may add a post-reduction view.

Why proposed budgets never match audited actuals

When more historical proposed budgets land in a follow-up PR, the gap between Panel 1 and Panel 2 for a given fiscal year will be visible. Three structural reasons that gap exists in every district:

  • Positions budgeted but unfilled for part of the year.
  • Group insurance premiums coming in under the budgeted rate.
  • Encumbrance rollovers and end-of-year free cash reclassification.

Marblehead's group insurance pattern is the largest of the three: an average 18.7 percent under-budget from fiscal year 2014 to fiscal year 2024, with the surplus recycled through free cash to balance the next year's operating budget. The pad finding has the full arithmetic. None of these three effects are unique to Marblehead; every district budget shows some version of them.

Peer block: fiscal year 2024 Schedule A

Peer education totals come from data/peer_schedule_a_expenditures.csv, a DOR Schedule A extract for a peer set of Massachusetts municipalities used elsewhere on this site. Population figures come from data/dor_income_eqv_pop_FY27.csv, the DOR fiscal year 2027 income, equalized valuation, and population release. Per-capita is the education total divided by that population; every peer town in the block has a value.

Per-student uses DESE enrollment from data/dese_peer_teachers_enrollment.csv. Only four peer towns (Marblehead, Melrose, Stoneham, Swampscott) are on file for fiscal year 2024, so the other thirteen rows show a dash rather than a fabricated value. Pulling the missing enrollment for the rest of the peer set is follow-up work.

The table sorts on per-capita rather than raw total. Sorting by total dollars puts Newton and Framingham on top because they are big; sorting by per-capita compares fiscal effort at similar scale.
