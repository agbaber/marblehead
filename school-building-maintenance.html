School building maintenance

Marblehead schools have no real preventative maintenance program, the routine kind that catches things before they break. Repairs happen when things break, not on a schedule. The only comprehensive condition assessment on record was done in 2021. The district calls it "the EBI report," though the minutes never spell the acronym out. It flagged roughly $5M+ in known problems. Plenty of those have been fixed since, but the fixes live in scattered transcript updates, not in one central list. FY27 is the year the school facilities team is finally trying to buy maintenance-tracking software, so they can schedule things like filter changes and belt swaps before parts fail.

Repairs happen, but nobody maps them back to the 2021 list

Plenty of work has been done since 2021. The Director of Facilities reports out at the School Committee each fall on summer projects. Reading those transcripts, several items from the 2021 list have been addressed:

  • Village security cameras updated (2023)
  • Veterans security cameras ~95% complete (2023)
  • High school card-access elevators and new fire alarm system (2023)
  • Veterans gym wall padding, PAC seat reupholstery, mini-splits (summer 2025)
  • High school fire doors and new entrance doors, Glover HVAC finalization (summer 2025)
  • High school roof and HVAC (design contract 2024, construction summer 2026)

What's missing is a single document a resident could read to answer "of the roughly 50 items in the 2021 plan, which are still open?" The list lives in transcript fragments spread across four years of meetings, not in one place. Last summer, the Facilities Subcommittee asked the Director of Facilities, Todd Bloodgood, to write that document up by August 31, 2025. Three facilities subcommittee meetings have come and gone since (September, November, February), plus eight School Committee meetings between September 2025 and February 2026. The document never showed up in any of them.

The longer-term fix on the table is a CMMS, a computerized maintenance management system. It would barcode every asset, schedule routine work like filter changes and belt swaps, and track which jobs have actually been done. The plan is to buy one in FY27.

The town did make a major investment: Brown opened in 2021

This isn't a "nothing has happened" story. On June 18, 2019, voters approved a $54.8 million debt exclusion to build a new elementary school. The ballot question called it the "New Gerry School," after the oldest of the three schools being replaced. Gilbane built it on Baldwin Road and finished 40 days ahead of schedule. By the time it opened, it had a new name: the Lucretia and Joseph Brown Elementary School. Students walked in on October 13, 2021.

Brown replaced three older schools: Gerry (built 1906), Coffin (1949, with a 1963 annex), and Bell (Upper Bell 1970, Lower Bell 1958). Both Bell buildings were torn down to make room for Brown. Gerry students had already moved out in 2018 after a steam pipe leak. Coffin went vacant when Brown opened.

The 2021 baseline, and what it didn't cover

The 2021 assessment looked at eight buildings: the high school, Veterans Middle, Village, Glover, plus four older buildings that were closed or about to close (Coffin, Gerry, Upper Bell, Lower Bell).

Brown is missing. It opened the same fall the report was compiled, and assessors seem to have focused on the buildings being closed instead of the new one. So the only condition assessment on record skips the biggest elementary school by budget. Nobody has done a new one.

Brown Elementary is not in the 2021 baseline. FY27 budget: $5,554,252, the largest of the three elementary schools.

The former school buildings carry most of the 2021 dollars

Most of the 2021 dollars are at buildings that aren't schools anymore. They aren't really "deferred maintenance" at this point. They're decisions about what to do with empty buildings.

Coffin. Vacant since Brown opened in October 2021. The Select Board took over from the School Committee and is figuring out what to do with the building. The Town calls this process "Adaptive Reuse," which is just a formal way of saying "deciding what an old building should be used for next." Five town departments pitched ideas last fall:

  • Cemetery Department — burial space
  • Light Department — battery storage
  • Recreation & Parks — dog park
  • Housing Authority — partnership
  • Community Development — boat storage

Three outside groups also responded. The most ambitious is from Harborlight Homes: 40 units of affordable housing, $28–32 million, keeping the original 1949 building and tearing down the 1963 annex. Community meeting #3 was May 20, 2026.

Eveleth. Has a stranger history. The district had stopped using it as a regular school, then reopened it briefly in 2019–2020 as swing space for kindergarteners while Bell was being closed down. When Brown opened in October 2021, Eveleth went dark again. Its boiler was condemned in November 2025. The Facilities Subcommittee has been talking to Park & Recreation about reusing the building for early education and after-school programs.

Bell. The 2025–26 minutes reference a "Bell School Evaluation Process," but the Bell buildings were torn down in 2020. It's not clear what's being evaluated. The notes mention services for 18–22-year-old students, so this may be a program being planned for one of the other vacant buildings.

What deferred maintenance looks like when it bites

In April 2024, then-chair Sarah Fox walked the high school during a Northeaster to see the leaks firsthand. Her report two weeks later at the School Committee meeting:

"As we walked, I was shown classrooms that had leaks and how they were being mitigated. As our walk continued, if teachers noticed us, they would pop out of their classrooms to report new leaks or leaks that were stopped, but it started up again. The custodians were everywhere with buckets and mops trying to keep up with the leaks … what is clear to me is that the continued priority must be made to move forward with a new replacement roof."

Marblehead School Committee meeting, April 25, 2024. Transcript.

Veterans Middle School got hit a different way in February 2026. A roofing subcontractor wrapped the new membrane over the drainage holes. The next rain ran under the roof and flooded the classrooms below. Insurance covered the new ceiling tiles, lighting, outlets, sprinklers, smoke detectors, pull stations, and horn strobes. The district refused to pay the contractor until everything was fixed. A classroom or two had to be relocated. MHTV (Marblehead's community TV station), which records its programming from the D-wing, still hasn't moved back in.

How it gets paid for

The money comes from two pots. Big projects like the high school roof go in the capital plan. Everything reactive comes out of the operating budget. The reactive bills bounce around. In March 2026 alone, surprise HVAC repairs at Brown and Glover, plus bus and elevator repairs, pulled about $614,000 out of the operating reserve in a single month.

The FY27 budget tightened things further. It cut one full-time maintenance position and moved half of a Facilities Assistant off the school payroll onto rental revenue, as part of $3.2 million in total reductions.

Then voters changed the math. On June 9, 2026, Marblehead approved Tier 3 of the override at $15 million ($8.5 million for the schools). The school side includes a new line: a building capital fund of $500,000 a year, every year. So FY27 does two opposite things at once. The base budget cuts day-to-day maintenance staff. The override adds money for planned replacements. Two different problems, two different fixes.

What's actually moving

  • High school roof and HVAC. Three small sections done over April 2026 vacation. The full project happens summer 2026. New HVAC equipment arrives in July.
  • Override passed. Voters approved Tier 3 on June 9, 2026. The school side includes the new $500K/year building capital fund. The School Committee unanimously endorsed Tier 3 a few weeks earlier on May 21.
  • Maintenance-tracking software. Planned for FY27. The first real way for the district to track which jobs have been done.
  • Coffin School future. Community meeting #3 was May 20.
  • Veterans D-wing. Repaired. MHTV hasn't moved back in yet.

Open questions worth asking

  1. Did the August 31, 2025 written status of each building ever get done?
  2. What does "EBI" actually stand for? The minutes just say "the EBI report" without explaining.
  3. When does the CMMS purchase happen?
  4. The override added $500,000 a year for school buildings. When does the first round go out, and on what?
  5. The "Bell School Evaluation Process" is mentioned in the 2025–26 minutes, but Bell was torn down in 2020. What's actually being evaluated?
