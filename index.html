What's on the ballot?

Two independent questions on June 9, 2026. Town Meeting already approved the spending side on May 4 (1,227 to 159); the ballot decides whether the levy cap moves up to fund it.

Question 1 — Operating override (three tiers)

  • 1AInvest$15M
  • 1BBuild$12M
  • 1CPartial restore$9M

Vote yes on each tier you'd accept. The highest tier that gets a majority wins.

Question 2 — Curbside trash

$2.3M levy carve-out.

Independent of Q1. Either way, residents pay for trash: through the levy (scales with home value) or a Board of Health flat fee around $281/household.

Why is there a deficit?

FY27 expenses grow about $6.2M while one-time revenue drops about $2.3M, leaving an $8.47M gap above what the 2.5% levy cap and normal revenue can cover.

  • Free Cash exhaustedFY26 used $5M of one-time reserves to balance; that source can't repeat. −$4.00M
  • Other expense growthContracted raises, vocational school assessments, fixed costs. −$3.23M
  • Group insurance (health), premiums up roughly 11% −$1.65M
  • Local receipts decline −$0.96M
  • New curbside trash contract −$0.84M
  • PERAC pension assessment, up 9% on the actuary's schedule −$0.46M
  • Offset by levy + other revenue growth +$2.72M

What happens if the override fails?

The town has a backup plan: a balanced no-override budget published in April. It closes the gap with $5M in remaining free cash, staffing cuts, and the elimination of the OPEB retiree healthcare trust contribution.

  • Town positions removed~12% of general-fund town headcount. 22 FTEs
  • School positions removedIncludes layoffs, vacancies, and stipend reductions. 18.25 FTEs
  • Abbot LibraryHours 52 to 24 per week; MBLC certification at risk, ends NOBLE reciprocity. −43%
  • Community Development −59%
  • OPEB trust contributionEliminated, not paid down. Liability continues to accrue. −100%
