<h1>What can we do?</h1>

<p class="page-lead"><em>DRAFT. A working list of specific revenue and cost-savings measures available to Marblehead. Each item answers two questions: <strong>have we tried this</strong>, and <strong>what is the potential</strong>. Open research questions are flagged. Companion pages: <a href="what-have-we-tried.html">What have we tried?</a> and <a href="is-the-board-trying.html">Is the board trying?</a></em></p>

Numbers below are order-of-magnitude unless cited to a primary source. Where the citation is missing, the estimate is a starting point for research, not a finding. Send corrections to the contact at the bottom of the page.

## How to read each item

- **Have we tried this?** Yes, partial, narrowly rejected, or not tried. Cited where the answer is in the public record.
- **What is the potential?** A dollar range, with one sentence on what drives it.
- **What we still need to know.** The specific data, source, or request that would tighten the range.

## Revenue

### 1. Adopt the local room and short-term rental occupancy excise

**Have we tried this?** **Yes, narrowly rejected.** Article 25 at the 2023 <abbr class="g" title="Annual Town Meeting">ATM</abbr> failed by three votes: **391 Yes / 394 No**. Not placed on a warrant since.

**What is the potential?** Roughly **$200,000 to $500,000 per year** at the statutory cap of 6 percent under <abbr class="g" title="Massachusetts General Laws">G.L.</abbr> c. 64G § 3A. The range reflects uncertainty about Marblehead's actual hotel and <abbr class="g" title="Short-Term Rental">STR</abbr> inventory and occupancy.

**What we still need to know:**

- [ ] Active STR listing count and revenue from AirDNA or the state STR registry
- [ ] Current licensed hotel and B&B room inventory from the Building and Health Departments
- [ ] Comparable annual room excise collections from Salem, Rockport, Manchester, Cohasset, and Nahant (<abbr class="g" title="Department of Revenue">DOR</abbr> Schedule A)
- [ ] <abbr class="g" title="Division of Local Services">DLS</abbr> technical assistance memo, if requested by the <abbr class="g" title="Chief Financial Officer">CFO</abbr>

### 2. Adopt the Community Impact Fee on professionally managed STRs

**Have we tried this?** **Not separately.** The fee under G.L. c. 64G § 3D was not separately presented at <abbr class="g" title="Annual Town Meeting">ATM</abbr>; it would typically be bundled with Article 1 above.

**What is the potential?** Modest, likely **$20,000 to $75,000 per year** at 3 percent. The fee only applies to operators with two or more short-term rentals in the Commonwealth, so the base is much narrower than the room excise. The statute requires at least 35 percent of the proceeds be dedicated to affordable housing, infrastructure, or property stabilization.

**What we still need to know:**

- [ ] Count of professionally managed <abbr class="g" title="Short-Term Rental">STR</abbr> units in Marblehead from the state <abbr class="g" title="Department of Revenue">DOR</abbr> registry
- [ ] Average rent per night for those units from AirDNA

### 3. Increase the <abbr class="g" title="Marblehead Municipal Light Department">MMLD</abbr> payment in lieu of taxes (PILOT)

**Have we tried this?** **Partial.** The PILOT from MMLD to the General Fund was raised from $330,000 to $360,000 in the FY27 budget, the first increase in years.

**What is the potential?** Possibly **$150,000 to $400,000 per year** of additional headroom, depending on what peer municipally-owned light departments contribute as a share of utility revenue. MMLD's annual revenue, ratepayer base, and reserves determine the ceiling.

**What we still need to know:**

- [ ] MMLD annual operating revenue and net position from the most recent <abbr class="g" title="Annual Comprehensive Financial Report">ACFR</abbr>
- [ ] PILOT-to-revenue ratios from comparable municipal utilities (Wellesley, Belmont, Concord, Hingham, Mansfield, Reading)
- [ ] Light Commission posture on a further increase

### 4. Audit and revalue the boat excise base

**Have we tried this?** **Unknown.** No reference found in annual reports or budget documents.

**What is the potential?** **$25,000 to $75,000 per year** as a one-time catch-up plus modest ongoing lift. Marblehead has a large recreational fleet and the assessor-administered excise can drift from market values.

**What we still need to know:**

- [ ] Current boat excise commitment and collections from the Assessor
- [ ] Date of the last revaluation
- [ ] Sample comparison of assessed values to current market values for a slice of registered vessels

### 5. Mooring waitlist transfer fee

**Have we tried this?** **Partial.** Mooring fees themselves were raised at the 2022 <abbr class="g" title="Annual Town Meeting">ATM</abbr> (harbor $8 to $10 per foot, other $7 to $9). The waitlist transfer mechanism itself has not been monetized.

**What is the potential?** **$25,000 to $100,000 per year** depending on waitlist turnover, but politically contentious because the waitlist is decades long and viewed as a heritage benefit. Harbor pricing in general is below market for the supply constraint.

**What we still need to know:**

- [ ] Current waitlist length, average wait time, and annual turnover from the Harbormaster
- [ ] Whether a transfer fee is permissible under current bylaws or requires a bylaw amendment

## Cost reduction

### 6. Move Medicare-eligible retirees to a Group Medicare Advantage plan

**Have we tried this?** **Not tried.** No record of a feasibility study or <abbr class="g" title="Public Employee Committee">PEC</abbr> consultation in the public minutes. The town's `what-has-the-town-done.html` page is explicit that no operational efficiency review of retiree benefits has been conducted.

**What is the potential?** Likely the **single largest fast-impact lever**, roughly **$300,000 to $900,000 per year** in town share of savings. The mechanism: leave the <abbr class="g" title="Group Insurance Commission">GIC</abbr> for the Medicare-eligible cohort and directly procure a Group Medicare Advantage PPO that delivers actuarially equivalent benefits at a lower per-retiree premium. Downsides include network restrictions, prior authorization requirements, and federal <abbr class="g" title="Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services">CMS</abbr> rate volatility; see the cautions on this page.

**What we still need to know:**

- [ ] FY25 retiree census split by Medicare-eligible (post-65) vs. pre-65 (PRR to <abbr class="g" title="Chief Financial Officer">CFO</abbr>)
- [ ] Current enrollment distribution by <abbr class="g" title="Group Insurance Commission">GIC</abbr> plan for post-65 retirees
- [ ] Town vs. retiree premium share by hire date
- [ ] Quote range from 2 to 3 carriers (Tufts, Blue Cross MA, Aetna, Humana) for an equivalent Group MA PPO
- [ ] Quote range from 4 to 5 actuarial firms (Segal, Aon, Mercer, USI, Hilb) for a feasibility study

### 7. Energy Savings Performance Contract on schools and town buildings

**Have we tried this?** **Not on buildings.** MMLD received a $1.3M U.S. Department of Energy grant for substation grid resilience, but that is not the same as a buildings-side <abbr class="g" title="Energy Savings Performance Contract">ESPC</abbr>.

**What is the potential?** **$250,000 to $500,000 per year** in avoided energy cost, plus **$2 to 4 million** of deferred maintenance funded out of guaranteed savings rather than capital. Mechanism: the town procures an <abbr class="g" title="Energy Services Company">ESCO</abbr> under G.L. c. 25A § 11I and the <abbr class="g" title="Department of Energy Resources">DOER</abbr> framework. The ESCO performs an investment-grade audit, designs upgrades, and is paid only from the savings it guarantees. The town has no upfront capex and no payment risk if savings underperform.

**What we still need to know:**

- [ ] Five-year electric load profiles per municipal and school building from MMLD
- [ ] Five-year heating fuel and oil consumption from <abbr class="g" title="Department of Public Works">DPW</abbr> and the school facilities director
- [ ] DOER Green Communities preliminary energy assessment, available free to Marblehead as a participant
- [ ] Indicative scope from two or three pre-qualified ESCOs (Ameresco, Honeywell, Trane, ENGIE, Schneider)

### 8. Town and school shared back office

**Have we tried this?** **Not done.** Town and school sides run separate finance, IT, HR, and procurement functions. The 2024 CliftonLarsonAllen Finance Department Assessment touched the town finance side, with 15 of 23 recommendations reported as completed by 2024-03-27.

**What is the potential?** **$200,000 to $400,000 per year** from attrition-based consolidation of redundant back-office functions over 24 to 36 months. Higher if a single ERP replaces parallel systems.

**What we still need to know:**

- [ ] Headcount and salary for finance, IT, HR, and procurement on each side
- [ ] List of software systems run separately by each side
- [ ] CliftonLarsonAllen Finance Department Assessment full report
- [ ] Stakeholder posture from Select Board and School Committee on a joint MOU

### 9. Inter-municipal agreement with a neighbor town for one shared role

**Have we tried this?** **Not done.** The town's record states that no shared-service agreements with neighboring communities exist for dispatch, IT, procurement, HR, finance, or inspection functions.

**What is the potential?** **$50,000 to $150,000 per year** per shared role under an <abbr class="g" title="Inter-Municipal Agreement">IMA</abbr> per G.L. c. 40 § 4A. Realistic first candidates: assistant assessor, veterans agent, alternate building inspector, or a joint procurement officer with Swampscott or Salem.

**What we still need to know:**

- [ ] Current cost of each candidate role on the Marblehead side
- [ ] Posture from Swampscott and Salem on a 4A partnership
- [ ] Any existing 4A precedent on the North Shore for reference structure

### 10. Modernize permitting and inspections software

**Have we tried this?** **Partial.** OpenGov was adopted for procurement (100 percent of formal bids issued electronically as of 2025). Munis is the financial system of record. No dedicated permitting and inspections platform has been adopted.

**What is the potential?** **$50,000 to $100,000 per year** through a 0.5 <abbr class="g" title="Full-Time Equivalent">FTE</abbr> saved on counter staffing plus accelerated recognition of permit revenue. Likely an extension of the existing OpenGov footprint.

**What we still need to know:**

- [ ] Current permit volume and average cycle time
- [ ] Vendor quotes for an OpenGov permitting module, Energov, Tyler, or comparable
- [ ] Inspectional Services staffing baseline

## Governance and process

### 11. Establish an Efficiency and Shared Services Commission with bylaw standing

**Have we tried this?** **Not done.** No standing efficiency commission exists. The CliftonLarsonAllen Finance Department Assessment was a one-time consulting engagement, not a recurring body.

**What is the potential?** No direct dollar impact from the article itself. Indirectly, comparable commissions in peer municipalities have surfaced **$500,000 to $2,000,000 per year** of cost-savings and revenue ideas over their first two reporting cycles. The commission is the institutional vehicle that protects items 1 through 10 from being picked off individually.

**What we still need to know:**

- [ ] Sample bylaw language from peer towns that have stood up similar bodies
- [ ] Composition appropriate to Marblehead (size, terms, appointing authority)
- [ ] Reporting cadence and forcing function (annual report tied to budget cycle)

### 12. Zero-based budgeting on the top three cost centers

**Have we tried this?** **Discussed, not adopted.** The School Committee twice discussed zero-based budgeting (2022-07-19 and 2025-10-17) without adopting it. A three-year operating budget forecast for FY26 through FY28 exists but is not the same as ZBB.

**What is the potential?** One-time **$500,000 to $1,500,000** of reallocation potential per cycle, declining as baselines reset. Most useful as a periodic exercise, once every 5 years per department, rather than annually.

**What we still need to know:**

- [ ] Past zero-based budgeting precedents in MA municipal practice
- [ ] Departmental appetite (Schools, Police, Fire account for about 70 percent of operating)
- [ ] Resource cost of running <abbr class="g" title="Zero-Based Budgeting">ZBB</abbr> (typically 1 <abbr class="g" title="Full-Time Equivalent">FTE</abbr>-quarter per major department)

## Important cautions

This is not a list of free money. Several entries carry real downsides that deserve to be on the page next to the upside.

- **Medicare Advantage migration** can produce member dissatisfaction, prior authorization friction, and provider network restrictions. CMS rate compression since 2024 is shrinking the savings opportunity over time. The decision is not reversible cheaply.
- **Room and STR excise** raises the cost of visiting Marblehead and affects local lodging operators. Even at the cap of 6 percent the burden is modest relative to seasonal pricing, but the political case has to be made on its own terms.
- **Efficiency Commission with formal standing** has been tried in many MA towns; some produce material findings, others produce reports that sit on shelves. Composition and reporting cadence determine the outcome.
- **MMLD PILOT** is functionally a transfer from ratepayers to taxpayers; overlapping populations, but not identical.
- **Shared services** save money slowly. Most savings show up through attrition over multiple years, not headcount cuts in year one.

## Procedural paths

Most of the items above can begin without any Town Meeting vote. Items 6 through 12 are within the existing authority of the Select Board, School Committee, Town Administrator, or Light Commission, working through normal procurement and bargaining processes.

Items 1, 2, and 11 require a Town Meeting vote. Either an Annual Town Meeting in May or a Special Town Meeting (which the Select Board can call at any time, or which 200 registered voters can petition for) can adopt them. Once warned, 10 registered voters can petition to add an article to a Special Town Meeting warrant.
