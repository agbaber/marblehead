Marblehead Override 2026

The override, in plain English.

On June 9, Marblehead voters decide whether to permanently raise property taxes. Here's what the town is asking for, and what people are saying about it.

Marblehead spends more money each year than it collects in taxes. The biggest growing costs are health insurance and pensions for town workers.

A state law limits how much property taxes can go up each year. The town has hit that limit.

The override is a one-time vote to permanently raise that limit. If it passes, taxes go up. If it fails, the town has to cut services like teachers, library hours, and road repair.

Pro

The town needs the money to keep functioning.

  • Health insurance and pensions for town workers cost more every year. The town has to pay them.
  • State law caps how fast property taxes can grow. The town has hit that cap.
  • Without an override, the town will cut services people use: teachers, library hours, road repair.
  • The override lets the town keep paying for what it already does.
Anti

The town should spend less, not collect more.

  • The town has known about this gap for years and didn't slow spending.
  • A lot of the rising costs come from local choices, not state mandates.
  • Saying yes once means another override request in a few years.
  • The fix is reforming what's driving costs, not raising taxes.
