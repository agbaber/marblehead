MARBLEHEAD HOLDINGS, LP
Office of the Managing Partner · Q2 Communication
To: The Editor
May 1, 2026

This is a courtesy note from the new ownership.

Our fund completed a take-private of the Town of Marblehead (the “Town”) in Q2. The diligence was straightforward. An FY27 operating gap of $8.47M; a healthcare line at $15.1M, growing at structural insurance-versus-wage rates; an asset portfolio carrying a meaningful book-to-market gap; and a regulatory environment in which the principal stakeholders meet two evenings a year. The override has been structured into three tiers. The residents have already negotiated against themselves. We are buying a discount.

The plan is conventional.

Naming rights.

Devereux Beach, Abbot Hall, the Causeway, Fort Sewall, the lighthouse, and the school district itself are candidates for sponsorship inventory. The master designation “Marblehead, an Amazon Community” carries the strongest sell-through; secondary placements (street-name partnerships, harbormaster vessel branding, in-classroom inventory K through 12) round out the package. Conservative annual yield, $3.2M. The Spirit of '76 is being valued separately.

Real estate.

Sale-leaseback on the operating buildings (the high school, Veterans, the three elementaries, public safety, Mary Alley, Abbot Hall itself) takes the assets off the municipal balance sheet at a one-time gain to the fund. The Town leases them back at an above-market cap rate. The improved balance sheet expands borrowing capacity, which we then use. The Highlands 40B parcel moves into a related-party vehicle; we expect the Article 4 vote on May 4 to land in our favor either way.

Marine assets.

The harbor is the Town's largest unmonetized franchise. The 2,200-position mooring waitlist converts from a queue to a market: existing entrants retain ordinal priority subject to a recurring holding fee, and positions clear at auction at exit. Mooring rates re-rate to comparable-harbor pricing (three to five times current schedule). The four yacht-club waterfront leases (Eastern, Corinthian, Boston, Pleon) reopen on the change-of-control provision and re-price as franchise agreements. Tucker's Wharf transient inventory moves to dynamic pricing. Conservative annual yield, $4.8M.

Operating expense.

Our consulting partner has identified $4.1M of immediate synergies in the school department:

  • One of the three elementaries converts to a residential asset; the K through 4 cohort consolidates at the remaining two.
  • Food service, custodial, and transportation outsource to national vendors on five-year terms; drivers reclassify as 1099.
  • Paraprofessional headcount aligns to the state minimum. The world-languages program below grade 9 and the elementary instrumental program are sunset.
  • Central office right-sizes to a single cabinet position. The remainder retain consulting agreements through year two.
  • The teachers' union contract reopens under a change-of-control provision we have inserted.

Police response is tiered; premium addresses are flagged for priority dispatch. The Essex Tech assessment line is preserved as a pass-through; the increase is not our concern.

Risk-adjusted opportunities.

Several initiatives sit outside conventional regulatory tolerances. Each has been cleared by counsel on a reserved-against basis. Net economics are accretive in every case.

  • The Q2 trash override carve-out, once approved, consolidates into the general fund. We do not collect for waste. The remitter is the residents.
  • The 83% GIC insurance share is preserved on paper. The 17% employee contribution is netted against a wage withholding we have reclassified as “premium reconciliation.” The unions will object. Their next CBA window is post-exit.
  • Recreation, athletic, after-school, and seasonal DPW staff reclassify from employee to contractor. The Department of Labor's remedy on misclassification is back wages and reclassification, not punitive damages; modeled exposure clears inside year one.
  • Pension contributions move to sixty percent of the actuarially required amount. PERAC's statutory remedy is a letter and a public listing. We have modeled the reputational cost at zero.
  • Enterprise fund surpluses (water, sewer, recreation) consolidate into the general fund. The Department of Revenue may issue a finding. The finding does not carry a fine.
  • A convenience fee is added to property tax bills, sized to capture what an override vote would otherwise have produced. The reserve against a loss at trial is smaller than the program's first-year revenue.

Aggregate cost of compliance, $0.9M. Aggregate value, $11M. We are proceeding.

Exit.

Year three. The likely buyer is a larger fund or a sovereign wealth office. The residents who remain will be informed.

We thank the Town for the warm reception, the Finance Committee for the pre-cleared diligence file, and the override coalition for sizing the gap on our behalf.
