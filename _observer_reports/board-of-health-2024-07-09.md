AGENDA

YWCA Northeast Massachusetts

Three members of the YWCA rape crisis center based in Lynn and who support 29
towns in the areas came to present the services they offer. They provide support for all
victims age12 or older, 24 hours a day, 7 days a week and work with all the regional
hospitals (Salem, Beverly, Addison-Gilbert, etc.) At the hospitals where the survivor
evidence is collected, they provide emotional support and guidance. They accept the
survivor’s story and use an empowerment model. Counseling is their main service via
in-person, zoom, phone, whatever works best.

In addition to direct survivor support and counseling they work with schools on
prevention, giving presentations to students from freshman to seniors. They talk about
healthy relationships, boundaries, types and signs of abuse, types of sexual violence
and resources. Their goal is to educate society and support survivors. This is at no cost
to the school.

The board and Mr. Petty had a number of questions and comments. Mr. Petty asked
the group to send him their information so he could put this on the HD website. There
was a question about what constitutes evidence collection - there are 17-21 steps and
include swabs, DNA collection, pattern of bruising, STD treatment, etc. All ERs in the
area are trained to collect this evidence. A further question was about the difference
between their agency and HAWC - they deal with sexual violence, HAWC with domestic
violence, but both are interwoven. Another question was about their funding - their funds
come from the MA Department of Public Health.
Reorganization

Before nominating and voting a new chair for the BOH this year, Dr. Massaro spoke
noting this will be an exciting year ahead. He sees two major issues for the year - the
completion of the transfer station renovation and preparing for the expansion of the
BOH from three to five members. He views this year as making steps towards that
future. As a relative new-comer to town he is impressed with the self-governing in MHD
and the volunteerism, and lauded Ms. Hazlett as being an exceptional volunteer. He
noted that in conversations before he became a board member that neither he nor
Mr. McMahon wished to be chair - he, Dr. Massaro did not feel ready yet as he had a lot
to learn about MHD first. He felt Ms. Hazlett should continue as chair as she has the
knowledge of the board and other town boards.

Dr. Massaro went on to further to explain that as the board went from a three to five
persons, he felt that there should be smaller subcommittees, each with a vice chair. He
proposed one committee deal with community health and the other with environmental
issues.

Mr. Massaro formerly nominated Ms. Hazlett who seconded her nomination. Prior to the
vote, Mr. McMahon made a statement saying that he had felt unappreciated and
obstructed on the board this past year and felt that the chair had been puttig self above
community. He then went on to list five areas where he felt he had been obstructed but
each of the issues had been advantageous - a new trailer at the transfers station, an
improved website, transparency with spending, speakers for substance abuse, and
more funding for the Marblehead Counseling Center.

Dr. Massaro then said he hoped we could get by last year. He noted that public health
had suffered during the pandemic, and it is clear public health is a local
issue. Marblehead has an opportunity to be a leader.

Subsequently Ms. Hazlett was voted in as chair in a 2-1 vote. It was decided by motion
and vote that Dr. Massaro would be vice chair of community health and Mr. McMahon
as vice chair of environmental issues (e.g transfer station, etc.)

Directors Report

    1. Transfer Station Update
       Mr. Petty gave a number of updates - there is a flag disposal box at the TS; there
is a waste oil tank there; many of the components for the TS renovation are on site and
by purchasing these directly there is a 10% cost saving versus via a contractor. Further
work is proceeding on work and architecture value engineering, hoping to trim
costs. Prefabricated structures as for the swap shed, transaction hut, etc. are being
explored, offering additional savings versus building on site. Scheduling when to put the
project out for rebid is tentatively set for October. Many contractors are already busy
and summer is a very busy time at the TS. Mr. Petty has discussed with possible
contractors about getting appropriate state certification to do this municipal work. That
process is complicated and lengthy (60-90 days processing.)

2.   Processing Bills
       Mr. Petty and the board discussed how they wanted to process and sign the bills
coming to the HD - do they want to see each individual receipt, do they want to see it on
a spreadsheet, do they want to go to the office to sign each bill, do they want the chair
to sign, do they want to use “docu-sign”, etc.

3.  Bathing Beaches
       The five MHD beaches are tested each Wednesday and the results are available
by Thursday and posted on the MA state website. When you get to the MHD town
website, you will be directed to the MA site where you can enter the name of breach for
a result. This is the most efficient way to find out.

4. Budget FY26
       Mr. Petty stated the FY26 budget process is starting early and initial budgets are
due back by September. This is a lengthy process and work is done in conjunction with
the Finance Committee, and when that is completed with return to the BOH for
discussion and vote. A major issue will be the cost of waste collection: there are two
years left on the current contract and the next contract will see a sizable increase. Mr.
Petty is looking across the state for the best solution. A 1% increase of the HD budget
“won’t work” because of employee contracts and the cost of waste removal. The
community needs to know the consequences of this and what fees would have to rise.
