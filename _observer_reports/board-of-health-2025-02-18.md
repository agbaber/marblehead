OBSERVER REPORT

Board of Health - 2-18-2025
Hybrid Meeting - Recorded
LWVM Observer: Tom Krueger

Members in Attendance: Andrew Petty, Tom McMahon, Tom Massaro, Helaine Hazlett

Prior to the first item on the agenda, Dr. Massaro offered an apology to Mr. Petty for his
behavior at the prior meeting. He said that it was fine to disagree about a subject but not
to have a display of anger. He acknowledged that Mr. Petty works “his ass off”, and that
the challenges of public health cannot be met by one person alone. Dr. Massaro and
Mr. Petty differ about what board membership means; Dr. Massaro is in favor of a more
active board and would like to know as much about public health as Mr. Petty.

Statewide Accelerated Public Health for Every Community Act (SAPHE 2.0)
        SAPHE 2.0 follows SAPHE 1.0 (but are two different works) and is part of a
statewide $4B effort. The blueprint began as early as 2014 but essentially from
2019. The first SAPHE did not do what it was supposed to do. MA is made up of 352
municipalities that is often unable to do even minimum requirements and standards and
address problems with current growth. The funding has been inconsistent and
unpredictable. All are optimistic for SAPHE 2.0 and an RFP will be coming soon.
        In response to this statewide effort, Dr. Massaro was asking the MHD board
should work with regional partners (e.g Salem, Swampscott, etc.) or “stand alone.” Mr.
Petty noted the difficulties of working alone as well as issues that cross town lines and
that as long ago as 2012 there was an inter-municipality agreement to try to increase/
improve services on issues, such as, smoking regulations, food code, etc. By working
together they could accomplish more. This regional effort has extended into other
regulations, tattoo and tobacco, and allowed more funds from grants.
        Dr. Massaro wants to “go beyond” and what he found the weakest part of SAPHE
2.0, that is no word about mental health. Mental health, a definite part of public health,
has always been dealt with separately. Why? Mr. Petty responded that “they think that
mental health comes from the health care side, not the public health side.”
        As discussion moved to seeking grants, Dr. Massaro has heard that grants are
“bittersweet”. Mr. Petty opined that first, you need someone experienced in writing
grants, then there is the work, and finally there is the reporting side which is often the
hardest part. Even if there is help getting a grant, the next is the issue of hiring staff
which is temporary. Mr. McMahon noted that temporary people aren’t guaranteed a full
time job and often are looking for one. Ms. Hazlett was wondering if the town’s grant
writer might come to a meeting and would email Mr. Thatcher about that. Mr. Petty
brought paper copies of SAPHE 2.0 so the board could see what things to do.
        Finally, Dr. Massaro wanted this topic included in the Town Report for next year.

Director’s Report
        The project to repair the compactor house prior to the installation of the new
compactor is proceeding with the involvement of the structural engineer, architect, et. al.
Mr. Petty hopes to have the site order by the end of the week. The rescheduling of the
compactor installation will be delayed by many weeks.
        A small fire occurred at the transfer station, necessitating a call to the fire
department. This happened under a leaf pile and is not uncommon with the snow cover
creating the additional heat.
        Regulations for tobacco and tattoo/piercing need to be updated. Mr. Petty passed
out the current regulations and the changes. If all board members are comfortable with
these, they go to the town attorneys to format. A public hearing with two week notice
follows, and after that the regulations can be adopted. The BOH has the legal capacity
to do so. (Historically, in 2003 MA put out a set of guidelines that the BOH then
adopted.) Some of the changes are keeping up with times - controlling menthol, later
flavored, and addressing the new products. Dr. Massaro questioned the cost of each of
the 351 municipalities having to pay attorneys for this process, wondering how to make
this less expensive. Mr. Petty responded that the state of MA could do this, but often it
is the local boards that first do this before the state “takes it on”. He said that each
community is slightly different. One could go to their state legislators to put this
forward.
        Discussion then went into the need to protect MHD citizens while the state
“catches up.” The board talked about the surveys sent to adults and to students about
the perceptions of smoking in the community. The surveys have a perception of much
greater use (50-70%) than the actuality of 3-5%. The discussion segued into the two
surveys that will be coming one - the BSAS (Bureau of Substance Abusive Services) in
March that is more focused and limited and the other hopefully in September (if the
$45K funding is attained) as part of CAHM project.

      The MHD Current learned that the town article that had been passed at Town
Meeting changing the composition of the BOH from a 3 person to a 5-person board, had
never been filed with the MA legislature. It will unlikely be done by the time of Town
Meeting this year. The result is that a 3 person board prohibits any two members
having a discussion or even attending a meeting together outside a formal BOH
meeting, all slowing any work. It was the responsibility of the MHD Select Board to
move it to the legislature.
