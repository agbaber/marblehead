Observer Report

Disabilities Commission 11/12/20 via Zoom LWVM Observer: Nancy Powell

Members present: Laurie Blaisdell (chair), Katie Farrell, Preston Ford, Sue Harris,
Amy Hirschkron, Ed Lang, Samantha Marino, Andrea Mountain, Tim Shotmeyer

Waterfront Access

Meeting opened with discussion of the Mass. Office on Disability ADA 504
Transition Plan for Marblehead that outlines the areas in town where there are
barriers to equal access for people with disabilities. The discussion first focused
on improvements needed to make the waterfront/harbor/beaches more
accessible for people with mobility impairments. Among the specific sites
discussed were the Harbormaster’s Office that lacks an accessible keypad on the
rear door and the dock at Stramski Beach which cannot be accessed by a person
using a wheelchair because the grade is too steep and, like other ramps and
docks, is out of compliance with regulations. The Committee will contact the
Harbormaster in hopes of spurring action on making some overall improvements.

Former Commission member, Susan Roper, is in the process of researching the
feasibility of installing a lift on one of the public docks to enable people with
physical challenges to have access to boats and kayaks. She will explore other
cities and towns that have done this and will contact manufacturers to learn
about lift types.
Transition Plan Funding

Laurie Blaisdell noted that the Town Planner has applied for an MOD grant to
fund projects that help bring the Town into full ADA compliance. This includes not
only the waterfront, but all town buildings that must be examined for needed
upgrades every five years (last done in 2015).

Update in Fort Sewall Upgrade

Ed Lang reports that the newly paved accessible walkway that will encircle the
park is set to be finished by the first week of December. He noted that all abutters
have been cooperative and “good to work with”.

Comments from New Commission Members

Laurie asked new members to indicate any areas of concern they have. This led to
a discussion about certain streets around town where people park their cars on
sidewalks and thereby force pedestrians to use the street which is often difficult
for wheelchair users or those with vision impairments. One member will follow-
up with Marblehead Police Chief for advice.

Members also brought up the problems that arise during snow emergencies when
sidewalks are impassable. Some wondered if the Town could take responsibility
for clearing sidewalks in areas of high traffic (i.e., near schools) and not leave it up
to homeowners who might not be physically able to shovel.
