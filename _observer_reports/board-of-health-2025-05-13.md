Community Health/Creating a Healthier Marblehead (CAHM)
       Dr. Massaro has the latest survey proposal from U Mass Boston and would like
the BOH to approve this. The latest proposal had only 3 focus groups versus 4 in the
original proposal, but Dr. Massaro intends to push for the 4.
       The other good news is that the health assessments will be fully funded with
funds from groups, such as, The Female Humane Society, MHD rotary groups, and
others from the community. This means it is funded with no tax-payer dollars. He is
seeking specifically to move forward with the contract with U Mass Boston Social and

has a PhD, an MPH, and 10 years of public policy work experience.) This was moved
and passed by the board.
       The “calendar” for this assessment starts in early June and will be a hybrid
survey (i.e. both print and digital.)
       Dr. Massaro has met with various groups who would be of interest in the
assessment: the chair of OB/GYN at Salem Hospital, Marblehead Pediatrics, and also
Pediatric Associates of Greater Salem (who see about 50% of MHD children).

Letter to BOH from a resident about a flu study done at the Cleveland Clinic
       The BOH received a letter about a flu vaccine study that was done at the
Cleveland Clinic in the Cleveland area. (This is a “preprint” study, meaning it has not
been peer reviewed or published in a journal.) The study was of 40,000 employees at
the Cleveland Clinic, 80% of whom received the vaccine and 20% who did not. The data
showed a 2% infection rate for the vaccinated and a 1.7% rate for unvaccinated.
        Dr. Massaro wanted to underline that this is a “preprint” study that may or may
not hold up to greater scrutiny (peer reviewed.) The 2% rate as it is a very low % of
influenza infections where normal years are up 25%. Also, that the flu vaccine
decreases the risk and severity of hospitalization; vaccination decreases hospitalization
in children by 40% and in adults by 80%. The question remains why the unvaccinated
had such a low rate. Perhaps other factors were present: the unvaccinated may have
had less or no patient contact, they may have been reassigned to less contact areas,
they have masked more, etc. These considerations make it imperative to wait for the
peer reviewed and published versions. Moreover, it would be essential to see what the
experience was at other institutions, such as MGH/Brigham. Conclusions in medicine
are never based on one study.
        Ms. Hazlett and Mr. McMahon have seen Dr. Massaro’s reply to the resident and
thought the reply was thoughtful.

MCC. (Of note the MHTF website was hacked but all the info was on the MCC website

Directors Report
       Household Hazardous Waste Day
               HHW will occur on 5/31/25 at the Transfer Station from 9am-12pm. The
company that comes recycles fuel, paint (latex and other), pesticides, etc. The fees that
are charged are based on gallons or pounds, with the costs in three different amounts
(small, medium and large.) This day is also shared with Swampscott residents who
account for ~50% of the volume. Mr. Petty asked that residents who use the transfer
station try to avoid these hours as it is very busy.
               In the past there was a HHWD on a Wednesday from 4-7 pm, and
perhaps it can happen again in the fall, but it is up the vendor. This is the final year of
the contract.

        Landfill Monitoring Expenses
              Since the transfer station is a “capped” landfill, ongoing monitoring is
required by MADEP. The company Hailey Ward (a highly regarded company who is
also the “face” with DEP) has been doing this twice a year, collecting water and gas
samples, at the periphery of the landfill to be sure nothing is leaching in the surrounding
areas. This is the responsibility of the town of MHD. As with other contracts, the costs
are rising, and Mr. Petty would like to have a long-term contract with the Hailey Ward.
He outlined the costs for the contract from 2026-2029, with costs gradually rising from
the spring sampling of $26K to $28K and fall sampling from $25K to $26.5K. The
proposed contract acceptance was moved and passed by the board.

       Beach Testing
               MHD has five beaches (Devereaux, Crocker Park, Gashouse, Grace
Olivers, and Stramski and sampling will begin on 6/11 and end on 9/10. The beaches
are sampled once a week (waist deep, elbow deep samples) on a Wednesday with
results back on Thursday. For three of the beaches (Devereaux, Crocker Park and
Stramski), if a sample is positive for bacteria, the beach will remain open pending a
second sample. For the other two beaches, Grace Oliver and Gashouse, a positive
sample will result in the beach being closed until the next sample is negative. Mr. Petty
went on to explain the procedure for collection and said all health department personnel
are needed to help. In addition to the samples, other data is collected - potential
sources, presence of dogs, rash line, tide level, number of people on the beach - and
are included in the report.
