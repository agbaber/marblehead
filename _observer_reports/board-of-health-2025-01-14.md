Marblehead Counseling Center - Ruth Ferguson

Ruth Ferguson, president of the board, came to the BOH to give an update and
highlight some of the achievements for the past year, including new staff, building and
landscaping improvements, sponsoring a Wellness Day, a conversation with Rob
Delaney (Lisa Hughes), a comedy night at the Gerry 5, etc. She thanked the town and
the BOH for the generous funding that helps to keep the center going.

In the past year (2024), 400 clients were served, 53% from MHD, for a total of 7000+
visits. This does not include the extra social service work that is often provided. There
are 235 people on the waiting list, of this 100 are from MHD. The MCC is constantly
contacting people to see if they want to remain on the list. Each situation is assessed
for its urgency and referred on, if needed. The demand still exceeds the supply.

Currently there are 7 licensed clinicians, 2 master level graduate students (they need 2
years of supervision before obtaining a license) and 1 intern from SSU - hopefully 2
more interns will start in June. The MCC would like to hire one more clinician. Kathryn
Percy was hired to replace an outgoing social service coordinator and Terry
McDonough continues in her role.

Specifics improvements to the building include IT completion, first floor renovation so
that all administrative functions are on one floor, but windows, back porch and kitchen
improvements are soon to be done.

Ruth asked for questions. First, why are only 53% of the clients from MHD? Some of the
reasons are a) MCC has had financial struggles and had to broaden its scope to serve
more and accept more insurances; b) logistics - clients often only want certain times
which are not available; c) if someone does call from MHD, the MCC just doesn’t stop
seeing a current client. These facts also explain why there is a waiting list. Terry
McDonough added that the there are currently <25 MHD children on the wait list. Some
of these children have additional needs beyond behavioral health. More staff would help
and the MCC is currently recruiting. The MCC saw >200 people for social service needs
(housing, food, etc.) this past year.

At this point Mr. Petty noted that the HD and BOH currently allocate $60K to the MCC
and would like to return to the sum of $120K that the HD was previously able to give. He
said that these funds are used solely for MHD residents.

7. Leash Law Article (sponsored by Rec and Park Commission)

Betsy Cougar, MHD animal control officer, and Sherry Bedrossian of the Recreation and
Park Commission came to present a Leash Law Article (for Town Meeting) to address
needed new regulations. (They gave the BOH members a 4-page document with pages
1-2 the current regulations (last updated in 2007), and 3-4 the proposed.) The hope is to
have the Rec & Parks, Health, and Police departments all involved. The two most
pressing issues are dogs that are not on a leash and the dog excrement that is either
left or bags of excrement not disposed of. Most MHD residents follow the laws, but ~5%
simply ignore them. Owning a dog comes with responsibility. In the presentation they
listed the various parks, beaches, etc. where leashes are required. There is one “dog
park” (off Lime and Hood Streets) but as was noted it is not easily accessible and has
limited parking. In addition, the previous November - April time when dogs can be off
leash may no longer be appropriate as youth sports often extend into November and
begin earlier. No dogs are allowed on bathing beaches because of health and
environmental risks.

The proposed new leash law would increase the fines for first offenses to $100, second
offenses to $300, and the third and subsequent to $500. Often the first offense is a
warning. (For dog excrement not picked up, the fine would increase from $50 to $250
for each violation)

The BOH will review these documents and there was some question about the size of
the fines, but the BOH will meet again on 1/27/25 and vote on the proposed article.
