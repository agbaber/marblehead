OBSERVER CORPS REPORT

BOARD OF HEALTH – February 4, 2026
Hybrid Meeting - Recorded
LWVM Observer: Tom Krueger

Members in Attendance: Andrew Petty, Tom McMahon, Tom Massaro,
Amanda Ritvo

Chair’s Report

CAHM Wellness Survey - Dr. Massaro reviewed the responses rate and
format of the survey: of 16,792 sent, 2550 responses = 15%, better than
expected; 43 questions with check boxes and many questions with multiple
parts; 21 questions had write-in parts; the data was presented in deciles
(18-39, 40-49, etc.) columns and rows (not a concern, modest concern,
high concern, etc.). He showed two such charts - “under age 18 substance
abuse” and “under age 18 bullying.” Both of these questions had responses
in the mod-high concern areas - 80-90s%. A last question “if you had the
opportunity what would you want the BOH to know” garnered 392
comments - most were about issues like teen drinking or loneliness in
seniors, but some said the BOH should just stick to “physical aspects” -
trash pickup, food safety, disease prevention. The next step in the CAHM
project is to form focus groups during the month of March based on age
deciles. Typical question for such a group would be “If MHD is to become a
place for everyone and for all to achieve greater wellness, what would have
to change?” And “ What programs or resources are need to help achieve or
maintain wellness.” The plan is to have a report by the end of March and
then hold open forums for the town.

Further questions were asked about the focus groups: How to participate?
Contact the HD. Other outreach methods discussed. The format would be
to have focus group sessions 90-120 minutes and discuss 2-3 keen issues.
Different questions would be posed t focus groups of different ages. For
example, in a child age rearing group, a question about bullying might be
asked.

What follows is a brief summary.)
Dr. Massaro had met with the police chief and exchanged emaisl. He
remained optimistic that progress could be made. At the last BOH meeting
a document from the Essex County district attorney was shown.
Dr. Massaro found a video the DA had made and that was shown: a quote
(paraphrased) “too many cases of injury… never a good idea to allow

the week before and had a very good conversation. Dr. M called the school

Of note the police department is running an “addiction and recovery series”
with the first session on 2/3 and will meet for 5 sessions all on Tuesdays.

email “blast” and see if this can be put on the MHD town calendar.

Dr. Massaro said that the BOH would work more on the prevention side
and develop navigation tools to go to resources that are already present.

Associate Chair for Health - Dr. Ritvo gave an update on infectious
diseases. Initially respiratory infections were heading down but now are
back up. 18% of cases are influenza, COVID 5%, and RSV 6%. There have
been 52 pediatric deaths from flu (90% unvaccinated), and CDC estimates
there have been 20M illness, and 11K deaths so far this season. Influenza
A has waned and now it is Influenza B. Still not too late to get vaccinated.
COVID vaccine currently is about 50% effective against ED and UC visits,
and from a recent article 80% effective against death. The vaccine takes
about 4 (not 2) weeks to fully protective.

Measles is surging country wide - 588 cases in 2026 so far. 94% of the
cases are within the country, not brought of foreign origin. 93% of the cases
are in those unvaccinated. There is concern with the World Cup and
tourism this summer, cases could rise. The measles vaccine is highly
effective after 2 doses. Measles is highly contagious: one case can infect
15-17 others. It is most dangerous for young children.

A new shingles vaccine shows promise in the reduction of dementia risk by
20-30%. Note that when chicken pox (herpes zoster) gets reactivated it can
lead to cognitive decline. Why is unknown. The vaccine is about 90%
effective in preventing serious disease.

Mr. Petty said the MA will have 40 Days of Celebration this summer - World
Cup, US 250, the Tall Ships, etc. The health department is working with 15
other north shore communities is public health preparedness. If say a
coffee shop has a case of measles, a nurse will be posted there the next
day offering to vaccinate immediately. Kids would have to stay out of
schools, camps, etc.

Trash and recycling contract - Mr. Petty has received a number of bids
and met with three of the large companies. He is looking at pricing and the
best proposal. He and Dr. Massaro are to meet with town administrator and
CFO to discuss the budget. Mr. Petty reiterated that the contract over the
past 10 years is soon over and that the proposed cost came in under
budget ($844K vs. $1M). He was told that the trash/recycling contract
should be part of the town budget.

Mr. Petty went on to discuss if businesses want to take part in this program.
He talked about how businesses could opt in, etc. As for residences, a new
65-gallon bin for trash and a 65-96 gallon bid for recycling would be
purchased for each. These will be purchased from a third party (=best
deal), and any repair will be done at the waste department. Each bin will
have a QR code identifying which residence has them. Further discussion
concerned the large budget deficit which has gone from $6.4M to $8.5M.
Trash can’t be part of an override because of public safety and state
requirements. There would be dire effects from cuts to services in town. Of
note, if one looks at the average taxes of towns in the state, MHD is on
lower side.
