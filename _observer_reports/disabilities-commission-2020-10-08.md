OBSERVER REPORT

Marblehead Disabilities Commission
October 8, 2020 4:15PM via Zoom
Present: Laurie Blaisdell (Chair), Katie Farrell, Preston Ford, Sue Harris, Amy Hirschkron,
Cheryl LaLonde, Ed Lang, Samantha Marino, Andrea Mountain, Rev. Tim Shotmeyer
Guest: Rob Dever, Highway Department

The meeting opened with request by the Chair to approve minutes of last meeting
January 23, 2020.

New commission appointee, Amy Hirschkron, was introduced.

New Highway Department Director, Ed Dever, an invited guest, discussed the need for
sidewalk repairs throughout town. Asked about the possibility of an override to fund
this, he indicated the work will be an ongoing priority for the Highway Department. The
department will need to take a methodical approach, committing to redoing adjacent
sidewalks wherever they “touch a road”. This means widening sidewalks, adding curb
cuts and enhancing pedestrian crossings where feasible and appropriate. He has already
begun to collaborate with the Town Administrator, Water and Sewer, the Engineering
Department and Tree Warden on a long-term approach to a long-term problem. There is
grant funding already in place to add 36 handicapped ramps in the next few months in
priority locations around town. A grant from the Department of Transportation will fund
a variety of safe mobility initiatives.

An update was given on the status of the Rail Trail redesign which will be aimed at
making the entire trail safer and more user-friendly for wheelchair users as well as
cyclists, runners and walkers. Some recent small-scale improvements have already
made the terrain easier to navigate by wheelchair according to commission member,
Cheryl LaLonde.
Ed Lang gave an update on the redesign of Fort Sewall which has been funded by
personal contributions, $270,000 of which are earmarked for bringing the restrooms,
walkways and ramps into ADA compliance and will give full access to those with mobility
impairments.

The $50k tree planting project approved at 2019 Town Meeting was put out to bid, but
none of the nine entities that had expressed interest attended the briefing session or
submitted bids even though the deadline for doing so was extended twice. The obstacle
is that vendors are reluctant to guarantee a successful outcome for trees planted in the
one place (Atlantic Street at CVS) deemed to meet all criteria.
