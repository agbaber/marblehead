OBSERVER REPORT

MARBLEHEAD DISABILITIES COMMISSION- January 13, 2022 – Via ZOOM
LWVM Observer – Nancy Powell

Members Present: Laurie Blaisdell (Chair), Katie Farrell, Sue Harris, Amy
Hirschkron, Cheryl LaLonde, Ed Lang, Samantha Marino, Andrea Mountain

1. Harbor Update: The Commission has been working with the Harbors and
Waters Board on plans to open up Marblehead Harbor to those with mobility
impairments, including making boating accessible to all regardless of physical
ability. Toward that end Amy reported she and Sue have completed their research
on sling lifts (for boarding and disembarking from boats) that would be
appropriate for installation at Tucker’s Wharf. The cost of the most expensive
option would be $3,500 and it could be added to the Town insurance policy
without incurring a premium increase. Input will be sought from a disabled sailor
regarding viability of the plan before approaching the Harbormaster to get on the
agenda for their next board meeting to seek approval and guidance for the
project.

2. Meeting with the Select Board: Commission members plan to invite the BOS
to a future meeting to address a variety of issues that impact the Town’s disability
community. Laurie presented several ideas to discuss including: clarifying the
Commission’s purpose as described in its Mission Statement; the importance of
making sure all Town departments, boards, and commissions consult with the
Commission about any new projects in order to insure ADA compliance and giving
examples of Town projects that have gone forward without considering ADA
requirements and thereby necessitated making costly changes; and the need to
educate department heads about ADA requirements. Laurie will send out a draft
of her proposal for issues to address with the BOS. The Commission will continue
this discussion at their next meeting.

3. Town Map of Accessible Locations: Sue reported on her meeting with
Katherine Koch, the new Executive Director of the Chamber of Commerce,
regarding the idea of highlighting on both their annual Visitors’ Guide and their
website those businesses and points of interest that are wheelchair accessible.
After discussion, and because the Guide includes only those Chamber members
who have paid for an ad or listing, the Commission will consider two additional
options—adding notations to the map available at the Town Information Booth,
as well as creating a separate flyer with a complete listing of accessible businesses
and public locations that could be inserted into the Guide and/or be made
available at the Information Booth. Sue will follow up with Katherine about these
options.

4. 139 Pleasant Street: Laurie reported she received notice from the Architectural
Access Board regarding this property whose owners have requested a variance to
ADA requirements. A virtual hearing on this matter has been scheduled for
February 7th at 11am. The Commission agreed to send another letter to the AAB
detailing the history of this property and expressing opposition to the owners’
variance request. Laurie, Sue and Amy will work on the letter and a script for
Laurie in the event she is able to actively participate in the hearing.

5. Brown School: Laurie will follow up with Jeff Dougan (Massachusetts Office on
Disability) about the status of ADA corrections that he identified on his site visit
last fall.
