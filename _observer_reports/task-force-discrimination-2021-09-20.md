OBSERVER REPORT

Marblehead Task Force Against Discrimination; September 20, 2021 via Zoom
LWVM Observer: Shari Pressman

Members present: Helaine Hazlett, Anne Marie Hunter, Deacon John (Joe)
Whipple, Christopher Bruell, Alexis Earp, Candace Sliney, Reece Dahlberg, Chief
Dennis King

Welcome and Introductions
Guests: Police Chief Dennis King was welcomed; Natalie Belli, 8th grade teacher,
who started a monthly Book Club and invited students who’ve read a book title
The Assignment to participate in tonight’s book discussion: Tessa Francis, Lucas
Rosen, Meredith Kennedy and Henrick Adams;
Louis Meyi, representative from the Marblehead Racial Justice Team (MRJT); Leah
Bokenkamp, representative from Indigenous People’s committee and Celia Sliney,
prospective student rep to TFAD.

Marblehead Racial Justice Team’s “Hands Up for…” Initiative
Louis Meyi reported working with Leah, distributing “Hands Up for” around town
this summer, distributing signage of people of color around Marblehead and
working on an almost completed video.

Debrief from Farmers’ Market Initiative
Helaine reported that the booth was popular at the market.
She also reported that the distribution of flyers was very successful; they were
also distributed to the YMCA, JCC, Devereaux School and Park and Rec camp
Updates and responses to recent incidents in Marblehead
Chief King had no new updates on recent incidents; he said he was impressed
with the civic engagement in town with Social Justice at the forefront. In Salem,
he had Racial Equity experience as well as racial profiling and as a graffiti expert.
He noted that 5 out of 21 tagging incidents were hate incidents. King said that
the ADL has been “phenomenal”. He is working on making the Marblehead Police
Department more transparent and noted that his “door is always open” at the
police station or through email.

Discussion of the book The Assignment by Liza Wiemer
Natalie Belli’s students had read the book and joined with TFAD members in the
discussion. The book is based on a real, controversial assignment by a teacher at
a school in upstate New York. Several people present suggested that the town
hold an All Town Read.

Updates on Town and School Engagement
TFAD currently has two student representatives as members.
In response to a published letter, Helaine noted that TFAD currently is Diversity
and Inclusion-based. The committee has no openings, but anyone interested is
encouraged to apply to become a member.

Updates from Marblehead Coalition
“Marblehead Speaks Out Against Hate” will be presented on September 23 at 7
pm via Zoom. ADL will make a presentation on hate crimes and responses to
them, followed by a panel discussion including Chief King, Pastor Jim Bixby, Rabbi
David Meyer, Henry Turner, Marblehead resident and principal of Newton North
High School, Marblehead Assistant Superintendent of Schools Nan Murphy,
Helaine Hazlett and Jackie Belf-Becker.

Leah Bokencamp, on behalf of the Indigenous Peoples Day planning committee,
reported that Claudia Fox Tree will speak about the invisibility of Indigenous
people on Thursday evening, October 7 at 7 pm via Zoom.

On Sunday, October 10, the Nettukkusqk Singers will perform live with singing
and drumming at the Jeremiah Lee Mansion at 1:00.
