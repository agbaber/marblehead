OBSERVER REPORT
Marblehead Municipal Light Board: 5/27/21 LWVM Observers: Jody Howard and
Nancy Powell
Members Present: MMLD Commissioners- Chairman Hull, Burke, Johnson and Wolf,
Homan and General Manager, Kowalik

Joe Kowalik led a discussion of methods for encouraging customers to lower their
energy consumption at peak times of the day when the cost is higher. He outlined an
approach being piloted now in Groton MA called the 4/40 Program. The 4/40 rate
structure (0.04 cents vs 0.40 cents per kilowatt hour) would encourage customers to
lower their consumption at peak times (between 4-8pm) by postponing high electric use
(EV charging, air conditioning, etc.) during peak hours. Currently costs are shared
across all MMLD customers even if they do not contribute to those costs. The new rate
structure would accomplish multiple objectives. It is a true representation of where
MMLD’s costs are incurred and would fairly distribute higher evening costs over the
customers who use electricity when higher capacity and transmission costs occur. It
also gives those same customers a way of reducing their costs by shifting their use to
the 20 hours each day when generation costs are low. MMLD plans to build awareness
about shaving the peak and time-of-use pricing through social media announcements,
alerts, flyers, and billing inserts.

General Manager- Kowalik gave a Project 2015A update noting that Massachusetts
Municipal Wholesale Electric Company (MMWEC) which had pushed the plant to satisfy
surge capacity requirements for Peabody Municipal Light and the region has authorized
a 30-day pause for the project amid concerns raised by North Shore residents and
public officials about safety, quality of life and environmental impacts surrounding the
project that was first proposed in 2015. While Commissioner Wolf spoke about the
importance of looking at newer clean energy technology to address surge requirements,
Kowalik questioned whether renewable energy alternatives such as batteries, solar, and
wind would be reliable enough to provide emergency surge capacity in the case of a
catastrophic event such as what happened this winter in Texas. MMLD plans to call a
meeting to invite public participation in the coming weeks.
