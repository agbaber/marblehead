OBSERVER CORPS REPORT
BOARD OF HEALTH - 10-27-25
Hybrid meeting - Recorded
LWVM Observer: Tom Krueger

Members in Attendance: Andrew Petty, Tom McMahon, Tom Massaro, Amanda Ritvo

CAHM Update -Dr. Massaro gave an update about the responses to the health survey. So far
there have been 1500 responses and it will need another “bump” to get a higher response. This
means more ads in the newspapers, a rolling backboard at football games, perhaps table cards at
restaurants, etc. The plan is to extend the time to 11/11 for responses. The higher the response
rate, the more value to the survey. What is needed are more responses from the 18-30 and 30-39
age groups. The “stretch goal” is for about 3,000 responses.

MMHTF -The Marblehead Mental Health Task Force was begun in response to the mental
health issues that arose during the pandemic. During its first years, it was very active: an
informative website, columns in the newspapers, etc. Since that time it has become apparent that
the MMTF would need an affiliation with the MCC, whose role is face to face work with
clients. This new affiliation is now called “Marblehead Cares.” Much ensuing discussion was
how to deactivate the MMTF website. There was also discussion about how much “traffic” the
site got, how the content would be directed to the MCC, and if the site could include more about
substance abuse. Thatcher Kezer said that he could take the website off the town website.

DMH Grant RFA - Dr. Massaro brought to the board’s attention that the state is offering grants
for “behavioral health promotion and prevention.” This would be from the office of the
aforementioned. Their mission is to promote the same with innovative, evidence based,
data driven approaches for the promotion and prevention of mental health conditions, including
substance abuse. Dr. Massaro presented a slide with the “objective for social impact” which
included reducing stigma, prevention of suicide, violence, substance abuse, as well as promoting
positive childhood and health experiences. The funds dedicated to this initiative is $3M, but the
competition for the grants will be “fierce”: 300 people were on the call, and there will be likely
only 15 grants. The grant funding depends on whether programs are being developed, are about
to be implemented, and are already developed but will need to sustain and expand, i.e., three
different tiers. If MHD were to apply, it would be at tier 1, a developing one. With the board’s
approval Dr. Massaro would like to apply for one of these grants. There was a discussion about
how these funds could be used: early intervention, a mini course for the community about public
health, also what the police department does, at school for help with social emotional programs,
etc. In conclusion, the board agreed with going ahead. The timeline for applications is 11/17.
The group discussed how and when to meet to get input from each member within this timeline.
Waste Management -Mr. McMahon told the group that a presentation of the future waste
management options will be discussed at the COA on 10/30 from 3-4 pm. He has been getting a
lot of feedback already: there is not much support for every other week recycling pickup; no
“push back” about the barrel sizes. Mr. Petty will bring the 65, 35, and 96 gallon toters to the
meeting. Mr. Petty will do a presentation about the RFP for waste management; a Q and A will
follow, followed by an open house. He noted there WILL be a limit for curbside recycling,
regardless. Additional recycling can always be brought to the transfer station. Also, he said that
there is always a “cost” to transfer station for this removal.

The license plate readers LPR have been working well. There have only been a few people
stopped. The sale of stickers has exceeded $600K this year. Dr. Massaro was asked about the
possibility of a limited license for those who only use the transfer station infrequently. Mr. Petty
said that there is in effect one already: a person can always use the commercial “weigh and
pay”. This cost is only $0.16 per pound.

Curbside leaf collection will be for 7 weeks, and all leaves have to be in paper bags.

Community Health -Dr. Ritvo showed the board the sign that will be posted at Riverhead. The
sign shows a swimmer graphics within a red circle and a horizontal slash. The wording is
“Warning - Water Not Suitable for Swimming” and below “Bacterial Levels Are Not Monitored
At This Location.”

Transfer Station Project Update - Mr. Petty said that all the documentation and permits have
been obtained and work will soon begin. He is awaiting a schedule from the contractor.
Commercial accounts (which number about 400) will be notified about the work and when there
will be closures for work. The transfer station will be open to residents, with occasional need to
reroute traffic.
