OBSERVER CORPS REPORT

BOARD OF HEALTH - 9-10-24
Hybrid Meeting, Recorded
LWVM Observer: Tom Krueger

Members in Attendance: Andrew Petty, Tom McMahon, Tom Massaro, Helaine Hazlett

Charter Committee Discussion - Department of Health and Board of Health

        Mr. Petty began the discussion by enumerating the responsibilities, regulations
and duties of local health departments. This is in the interest of protecting the public
health with decisions made at the local level. This long list included writing and
maintaining annual reports, retaining 3 different types of records, reporting dangerous
diseases from the community, from deaths, from dairy farms; providing anti-rabies
vaccine, reporting/investigating food poisonings, administering the sanitary code, etc.
The duties continued to involve sanitary waste, landfills, septic and garbage problems,
nuisance complaints, bakeries, seafood, pool/beaches, camps and motels, burial
permits, participating in public health emergency preparedness, etc. etc.
        In further discussion after the enumeration, Mr. Petty noted that the town
designates a board of health who then has a public health director perform the duties.
Regarding budget, he is supposed to spend 30% of his time with public health and 70%
with waste services. In practice his actual work depends on the greater need. It was
also mentioned that for a town of 20,000 the MA DPH would suggest that the HD
budget be $739K - in reality, it is actual $324K. Mr. Petty tries to supplement this
shortfall with grants, e.g., Public Health Excellence Grant, which provides $114K. There
are other grant opportunities - regional substance abuse grants, opioid funds, and other
small grants. In the state’s eye, the HD needs a more robust funding and staffing.
        The budget cycle for FY25-26 has already begun with the initial budget to be
submitted on 9/6/24. It is a long process and this is just the beginning.
        Following this initial presentation, Dr. Massaro began the discussion of what the
role of the Board of Health could and should be. He began saying that public health is
not often prioritized. The role of public health in the US is behind the times and
backwards compared to other first world countries. This is the result of a market driven
orientation which public health does not fit into. Before the pandemic the question was
how could public health serve the public. Today the role/definition should be expanded
to how to make our citizens healthier. He went further to say that in MHD the distinction
between the BOH and HD is not clear. In fact, as he reviewed the various bylaws at the
state and MHD level, there is little or no mention of the BOH and only one mention of a
director of health. He went further to reiterate he wanted to make the health of the
community better - more health promotion. (As example, the funding that Colorado
found to have all newborns leave in car seats. Public health should deal with population
needs.) In conclusion he wishes to figure out how to organize towards this goal.
       Mr. Petty noted that the HD has been level funded for years which has prevented
some other programs. He said that budget time is an opportunity for the BOH to ask for
more to funds to aspire to these goals.
       The topic returned to the ambiguous structure of the BOH and HD, one of which
doesn’t actually support the health director. It was noted that Mr. Petty after 15 years
has never had a performance review despite all his good work. Perhaps a town charter
may make changes to the structures of health department and BOH.

Community Health Update
        The Mental Health Task Force met on 9/9 and has added a new member - a
student from the high school who is both bright and articulate. Mr. Brett Mueller from
Granite Recovery Center talked to the group about the difficulties with treatment and
recovery units, in particular the short supply of mental health professionals. The student
will soon give the group the perspective of the mental health of Gen Z. Dr. Lebon, the
chair of the MHTF, will come to the BOH meeting in January.
        In the spirit of addressing the best practices of the community health needs,
U Mass Boston conducted a survey about mental health needs. Swampscott had a
more expanded survey of the health needs of their town that they found very useful- the
cost of which was $35K. Dr. Massaro presented to the board a document titled
Marblehead Cares - A Community Health Needs Assessment. “The proposed needs
assessment will serve to inform the Board of Health and other Town leaders about
unmet health needs in the community and will provide guidance on future steps towards
making Marblehead a well-community for all….” Attached were three area for best
practices - 1) conduct a maternal and child health landscape assessment… 2) mental
health best practices- to strengthen partnerships of public safety, social services,
school, etc. for identifying children and young adults at risk, and 3) age and dementia
friendly best practices. He noted that there is state grant money available and urged the
BOH to support a motion for the town administrator to submit grants for the first two
needs assessments. The board moved and passed this motion.

Bills: Mr. McMahon read the list of bills paid by the health department over the past
month. This included the vendor, amount, and reason for the bill.

Directors Report
       Mr. Petty noted that we are near the end of mosquito season, but across the
state there have been cases of EEE and West Nile. It is important to be aware of this,
especially as one is traveling. The recommendations continue to be to use insect
repellants, wear long sleeves, and caution at dusk.
       He went on to discuss rodent control which is always a problem in a coastal
community. When the department is contacted, they give out a flyer which discusses
some mitigation practices. Since it has been dry, rodents often seek water sources, as
well as fruits and vegetables and gardens. If there is major concern, they recommend
hiring a pest control company.
