OBSERVER CORPS REPORT

Marblehead Disabilities Commission - February 14, 2024
(Hybrid Meeting)
LWVM Observer: Nancy Powell

Members Present: Laurie Blaisdell (Chair), Dee Cuffe, Briggete Duffy, Amy Hirschcron
(remote), Ed Lang (remote)
Members Absent: Cheryl LaLonde, Andrea Mountain
Guests Present: Kyle Wiley

1. Review Marblehead ADA Report

The Commission has found a possible solution to the problem of contractors who
occasionally neglect to address ADA regulations prior to starting a new construction
project and then must go through a costly and time-consuming rebuilding process in
order to comply. Ms. Blaisdell shared a document she drafted based on one the City of
Cambridge has developed called “Steps to Building a Permit” that delineates how
accessibility standards would be incorporated into the building permit process and how
accessibility guidelines will be enforced. Those applying for building permits for new
commercial projects or renovations which cost more than $100,000, for example, must
follow ADA guidelines. The commercial permit link will be found on the Town website
and the term “Accessibility” will be listed on the permit procedure in ViewPoint.

Ms. Hirschcron will look at the ADA consultant’s Transition Plan to see if involvement in
the building permit process can or should be included in the job description for the
Town’s ADA coordinator. The MDC will review this at the next Commission meeting in
March. It appears the ADA Transition Plan has yet to be made available to the public as
promised. Ms. Hirschcron will create a list of action items that were to have been
completed by now and offer support to the Town Administrator in implementing them as
needed.
2. 139 Pleasant Street

Ms. Blaisdell received correspondence from Mr. William Joyce from the Architectural
Access Board (AAC) in Boston. This project has continued to be out of compliance with
accessibility requirements for a commercial building and has gone over the 30-day
window without plans submitted. It is now officially gone beyond the date by which an
appeal could have been filed. The AAC will determine if the owner will be fined and will
update Ms. Blaisdell once a decision has been made. Commission members
recommended Ms. Blaisdell write a letter urging the AAB to impose consequences for
failing to comply with ADA regulations and ignoring requests made by the AAB as other
businesses in Marblehead have actively been making strides in complying with ADA
laws.

3. Follow-up Riptide ramp

The Riptide owner decided to comply with the ADA law (rather than seek a variance)
and is installing ramps inside both the front and rear entrances. Ms. Blaisdell will reach
out to local newspapers to let them know the Commission is beyond pleased. The hope
is that other businesses will see that positive publicity will ensue when they make an
effort to offer equal access to those with mobility impairments.

4. Follow-up Harbormaster Ramp

Ms. Blaisdell will engage a construction professional to correctly measure the new ramp
between the State Street dock and the Harbormaster’s office because initial rough
measurements were calculated and it was found to be out of compliance. Ms. Blaisdell
will compile the findings and forward them to the Harbormaster who has indicated he
will rework as needed to meet ADA guidelines.
