OBSERVER CORPS REPORT

BOARD OF HEALTH 9-29-25
Hybrid Meeting, Recorded
LWVM Observer: Tom Krueger

Members in Attendance: Andrew Petty, Tom McMahon, Tom Massaro, Amanda Ritvo

Approval of minutes - the minutes of 7/10/25, 7/22/25 and 8/13/25 were approved

Community Health Assessment Marblehead (CAHM)

Dr. Massaro reported on the progress of the CAHM. The primary researcher from UMB,
Dr. Caitlyn Coyle made a presentation to the Select Board on 9/24. She discussed all
the general goals of the study - assessing the health and well of MHD, how it will inform
strategic planning, provide data for all the stakeholders, and act as a baseline for
documenting progress and future projects. Much attention was directed toward the
privacy and security of the data.

On 9/29 16,739 postcards asking people to fill out the survey were sent to all MHD
voting members and students >18 years old. Seventeen percent of this population are
18-29 years old. This can be a challenging group to get replies from as they are off at
college, out of town, etc. Dr. Massaro stated that they would like to get back at least 5-
6,000 surveys. In hopes of increasing participation, he shared two different flyers that
were created (one by the COA and the other by Jack Attridge). This can be printed on
8x11 paper, and distributed at the YMCA, COA, and other places (JCC, etc.) houses of
worship). In addition, Mr. Petty said that 12 plastic mounted posters have already been
made and more could be done. A banner about the survey will be hung by the MMLD
over Tedesco Street during October. There will be ads in both MHD newspapers.

The hope is that the returns will come in the next 3 weeks, but this time frame may have
to be extended. He will be meeting with Dr. Coyle on 10/8 to see how it is going. With
surveys there is usually an initial good return and then it tails off. As this might happen,
Mr. Petty has a large Listserv to remind people as well as the use of the reverse CODE
RED.
Dr. Massaro hopes that this survey will be taken very seriously. He is basically saying
“tell us what you want us (BOH, HD) do for you.”

Dr. Massaro shared some of the slides that were used in the presentation. One showing
the links among wellness, emotion, spirit, mindfulness, connection, environment, and
purpose - the multiple areas of life. Another slide addressed why you should complete
the survey: a) be heard, b) be part of the solution, c) it only takes a few minutes (10-12
minutes), and d) support your neighbor. There was also a slide that addressed the
security and privacy - a) responses are anonymous, b) data is collected on a password
protected platform which is on the UMB server, c) only UMB researchers have access
to this data, d) no raw data will be shared with HD or BOH, and e) the reports with
aggregated data will be made public. Interestingly, these results can be linked to other
data that is already collected at the state level. MA does a “healthy aging community
profile” on every town.

The data will hopefully be available by Thanksgiving time and preliminary analysis by
early 2026.

Community response to recent tragedy

Mr. McMahon will be meeting soon with members of the school committee, Select
Board, Park & Rec to discuss ideas to prevent further tragedies. Some of the potential
ideas include: curfew for driving (12am-5am) for under 18 years olds, reviving a
program from long ago whereby an impaired student could call another to drive them (a
safe driver program), and perhaps educating students with a “Arrive Alive” simulator.
This device simulates what it’s like to drive impaired which each participant (student)
able to experience the effects. There is a company that comes to the site and runs it.
For MHD if all the high school and 7th and 8th grade students participated, it would run
for 9 days at a cost of $22, 500. Further discussion was about potential funding
sources.

Dr. Massaro talked about a webinar he recently viewed on cannabis. (He will get the
slides for the next meeting.). He noted that the cannabis industry is using the same
tactics that the tobacco and alcohol industry used: it’s not addictive, it doesn’t cause
problems, etc. There is no national oversight, and no state has looked at the
consequences of cannabis. Even doctors have little information about the
consequences.

Community Health Update

Dr. Ritvo discussed the confusion about the COVID vaccinations for ‘25-26. She noted
that the members of CDC ACIP (Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices) were
all replaced many without health expertise and some were “anti-vaccers”. They shifted
from broad recommendations to an individual approach, with no new evidence. MA
DPH, however, took clear steps by stating that all those eligible persons can to be
vaccinated and not requiring a prescription. The MA DPH aligned its recommendations
with the professional doctors’ organizations - AAP, AAFP and ACOG. This also reflects
the consensus of the NE Public health collaborative which includes CT, ME, NY, RI, PY,
MA and others. The recommendations are for all those > 18 and especially those >65
to be vaccinated; infants 6-23 months; 2-18 on a risk-based approach, and all pregnant
women in order to confer immunity for their baby in the first 6 months.

Transfer Station

Mr. Petty reported that the transfer station renovation consisting of a new scale house,
site work, moving the scale to its permanent location, and other work has now been put
out to bid. The bids were received and the winning amount is $1,590,000 from Deiulis
Brothers of Lynn. The second bid that was made was for $1,708,435. The board moved
and approved taking this bid.

The project will require some disruption and down-time, but this will be primarily felt by
the commercial business. While the pit is “down”, the trash will be taken directly to
Resco. The project will begin with concrete work this fall, and construction of the
building will be during the winter. When this is all done, attention will be paid to a new
swap shed and the feasibility of sorting demolition and construction material on site.

Trash/Recycling

Mr. Petty turned the discussion about the future curbside trash and recycling changes
looming ahead. Currently, each household is allowed a 65-gallon toter or 2 x 35 gallon
toters for trash. (All trash has to be bagged, in the container, which has to have a tight
lid. All this to prevent tip-over and rodents.) Also, currently, with recycling there is no
limit to the amount. He reminded all that there should be no waste in the recycling.
(There are Recycle Smart stickers.). Recycling should NOT be bagged. The reason why
this was possible was that in the previous contract (which will expire September 2026)
Republic was responsible for all the recycling. MHD does about 3000 tons of recycling
per year. In the future MHD will have to pay for recycling at $80-90/ton and this will be
added to any new contract.

This discussion is all leading to each residence having a standard barrel size, 65
gallons each for trash and recycling. The HD would buy these in bulk, one of each for
the 8000 households in MHD at a cost of $900K that could be paid for over 5 years.
(Buying in bulk greatly reduces the cost of each container to ~$50 versus $100 that one
might pay at Home Depot. Additionally, each could be RFID tagged, so it would be
known who owns a container.) Using these standardized barrels would allow a truck
with one driver using an automated arm to load, resulting in a saving of having one not
two workers. The other benefit is that the town would know exactly how much the cost
of curbside trash and recycling would be.

Further discussion was about the separate needs of the historic parts of town where
such a system would not work because of narrow streets, etc. Clearly, these areas
would have collections as it currently is done. Some discussion was about every other
week collection of recycling as happened during the final weeks of the strike. (This
would result in some significant savings.) Salem does every other week collection
successfully. Other thoughts were about the recycling that business currently put out.
For now, it hasn’t mattered to MHD since Republic “owned” that. This would have to
change with any new contract. Also, it is important that only recycling be put in
recycling. Companies use optical scanners and if they see more that 5% trash in the
recycling, it is charged as trash $130 versus recycling at $80.

When asked about whether or not the Finance Committee is aware of the upcoming
changes, he said they were. The costs of trash and recycling is currently ~$1M and will
likely jump to $1.6-1.7M with any new contract. Mr. Petty is going to put out an RFP with
a list of options and can price accordingly. Mr. McMahon noted that there is oftentime
initial resistance to change, and both he and Mr. Petty stressed the need for public
input. An RFP will have to go out soon, as companies need almost a year’s lead time to
obtain trucks, employees, etc. Mr. Petty again addressed the question of why doesn’t
MHD just do this itself. He reiterated that the costs (truckers, employees, heavy work ->
employees out, etc.) were just too high. Finally, Mr. Petty is looking at qualified
companies, big and small, who can do this work.
