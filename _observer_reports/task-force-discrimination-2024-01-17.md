OBSERVER CORPS REPORT

Task Force Against Discrimination 1/17/24
 Zoom (recorded)
LWVM Observer: Shari Pressman

Members in Attendance:

Helaine Hazlett (co-chair), Diane Gora (co-chair), Deacon John (Joe)
Whipple, Chris Bruell, Reece Dahlberg, Chief Dennis King (ex-officio), Kim
Gubelman

Introductions:

Guests: Julia Ferreira (Assistant Superintendent), METCO Director Caja
Johnson, Pastor James Bixby, Leigh Blander, Louis Meyi, Cindy Tower-
Loewen), METCO parents of students in MPS, and Jacqueline Wade,
President Boston METCO PCO and METCO (parent)

Discussion with Caja Johnson, METCO parents, and members
Marblehead Racial Justice Team (MRJT)

Chris noted that TFAD has a 30-year history in Marblehead. It should “let
folks know we exist” and be more proactive than reactive in being a
resource for people to assist making their experience better and help
people feel welcome here.

Pastor Bixby said we are in a crucial moment. The School Committee is in
the news every week. The MRJT has raised the alarm bell a couple of
times and has sent letters to the School Committee that were summarily
ignored. TFAD was commissioned by the Select Board, which “relies
heavily on it to incorporate all people who are here to be safeguarded for
good moral measure, but also for good legal measure. The Select Board
doesn’t want to have to pay legal fines because they’re finding out that
discriminatory actions are taking place in town. MRJT has informed the
Select Board that the School Committee is going down that path currently.
He described the Committee in a “freefall” in its loss of moral standing.
Pastor Bixby described flags as symbols of comfort and acceptance. He is
“morally very concerned” but believes the Select Board will pay a lot more
attention if there’s a good chance they’ll face a lawsuit about their flag
policy.

“School Committee is going to cost the town a lot of money”.

He asked what tools and policies and possible leverage the TFAD has as
an “interior” organization. He hopes that it and the MRJT are “aligned and
work together using both our lanes” to turn the town around as soon as
possible so that we don’t face costly lawsuits, lose more candidates for
superintendent and other positions, and welcome all people who want to go
to this school and be educated.

METCO Director Caja Johnson said she was so grateful to have support
within the community from MERGE and TFAD. She noted that “we, as
adults and educators, have to “model basic humanity and a sense of
belonging for our students”. She noted the “huge sacrifice and commitment
parents have made to send their children over an hour away to a different
community”. She asked that METCO families speak next.

The grandmother of a METCO student said the School Department has
made drastic mistakes toward students that need to be corrected.

She went on to say that the issue is very near and dear to her, but she
can’t fix it. She said she’s taken all the steps by going to the principal and
to the superintendent but feels as though she’s treated with discrimination
and being pushed under the rug. The news doesn’t tell the students’ and
families’ stories, only the teachers involved.

Chris and Helaine thanked her and said they could hear the pain in her
voice.

The mother of the METCO student said “her very young child’s not having
a good experience in Marblehead” and that nobody sees what other
children are doing to him. She reported that he felt “afraid and unsafe” in
Marblehead, not supported in trauma, looked at as an outsider, and treated
differently. She felt a “step-by-step” reorganization is necessary and is
completely disappointed in the school system.
Jacqueline Wade, Boston METCO Parent Council Organization President,
a teacher in a Boston public school and a METCO parent of a student in
Marblehead, pointed out that growing up in Marblehead schools, where
many of the students have gone to school together all their lives and may
know each other outside of school, is different from a city school
experience, where students haven’t all been together. Her son has faced
what she called possible unknown or unintended bias, even from teachers.
She said part of being an ally in school is to find “teachable moments” and
safe spaces for people to learn, ask questions, and possibly unlearn what
the community and TV have taught. She has checked her own bias dealing
with her own students.

Chris suggested more interpersonal training in Marblehead. Pastor Bixby
noted that several high-quality, internally developed, interpersonal trainings
were abandoned at the school level because of the School Committee
pursuing the leadership they were interested in doing. The pastor believes
the trainings would have given teachers the equipment to deal with what
we’ve heard today.

Chief King replied directly to the mother of the young child, apologizing as a
chief, a person, and a parent that her son feels unsafe and unwanted in
Marblehead and added that her words were impactful. He said the police
department has been doing a lot of implicit bias training over the last five to
seven years. He said that whatever she needed from him to address and
correct it, he would try.

The grandmother said she was sorry and didn’t know that a police officer
was involved in the meeting. She said she’d spoken with Chief King and
would like to talk later. She said evidence had been submitted by the
School Department and that criminal charges need to be brought. Chief
King will meet with the mother and grandmother offline again.

Diane, a teacher for 30 years at Glover, said she doesn’t know the details,
but hearing her colleagues described in negative terms is disheartening.
She said she works with caring individuals. She understands that this has
been the family’s experience and isn’t negating it; she just hasn’t had the
same experience with colleagues as the family has had.
Diane also said that except for this past year, teachers have spent the last
three years training in implicit bias so that students can see themselves
reflected in curriculum (mirrors) and feel included in others’ experiences
(windows).

Jacqueline said she didn’t know one teacher who goes into this line of work
thinking, “I want to ruin these kids and be racist”. She feels that teachers
can make mistakes unintentionally. Colleagues are caring and give 110%
of themselves. She noted that what the family is going through is larger
than what we are aware of in this forum.

Chris said that we must be able to eliminate the types of experiences the
families have had. It’s concerning and that’s one reason why families were
invited.

Chris added that as a committee appointed by the Select Board, TFAD
needs clearly defined expectations from the Board and methods that TFAD
can use.

Caja closed by thanking the families brave enough to log on, a wonderful
staff, and organizations within the community that are very supportive.
Marblehead Public Schools has work to do. Parents apply to METCO for
the right reasons. How do we come together and take “action” steps?

The mother thanked everyone for listening. Teachers come with the intent
of being great and not harming, but her son could have died. She’d like to
come to the next meeting and closed by asking TFAD to do something fast.

Update on Marblehead Police Department News--Chief Dennis King

MPD is continuing to monitor the community for any hate incidents and is
being vigilant. The hoax incident at Temple Emanuel provided an
opportunity to work with the temple as a partner. If the perpetrator(s) is
(are) found, most likely the case would be dealt with federally. MPD is also
working with Community Road institutions.

Discussion: February Book Read

Diane suggested the picture book The Other Side by Jacqueline Woodson
as a February vacation book read. Older students will read and discuss the
book at home and at school and then some will join TFAD at the beginning
of a meeting. Previously, Natalie Belli has lead discussions with the book
group she facilitates. Primary grade students could respond with an art
activity. Diane has activities for younger students. Reece will talk with local
bookstores to secure books for everyone participating and added that the
district will usually provide some funding for books. Reece would like to
include METCO students and parents.
