TASK FORCE AGAINST DISCRIMINATION 3-25-26
In Person at Abbot Public Library Meeting Room
LWVM Observer: Shari Pressman

Members in Attendance: Helaine Hazlett, Diane Gora, Deacon Joe Whipple,
Reece Dahlberg, Jean Guastaferri, Louis Meyi, Jay Morrison, Maura Dartley-
Rocco Guests: Kim Gubelman, Laura Meyi

James Book Discussion: The 2024 novel James, by Percival Everett had been
chosen as a community book read for adults in celebration of Black History
Month. Complimentary books were available at Abbot Public Library. The
novel had been described as a “revisiting” of The Adventures of Huckleberry
Finn by Mark Twain, so the James discussion became somewhat of a
comparison of the two novels. Participants discussed code switching—the
practice of African Americans speaking in one dialect when talking with white
people and an educated speech with each other. People who’d listened to the
novel had difficulty with the “twang” of Missourians and found that reading
while listening was useful. The frequent use of the “n” word is offensive to
modern readers, but the consensus of those attending the discussion
supported teaching the novel uncensored, clarifying that the word was meant
to be demeaning and racist. Mark Twain had, in an introduction to Huckleberry
Finn, anticipated criticism when his novel was published and for future
generations. He was careful to validate the speech he used as well-
researched and accurate for that time and place.

Dr. Martin Luther King, Jr. Breakfast: Held at Old North Church on January
19th; an estimated 150 people attended; event was moved to the main
sanctuary to accommodate the attendees; organized by Susan Morrison, wife
of Marblehead Racial Justice Team member and liaison to TFAD, Jay Morrison.
Organizers hope to make it annual event.
International Holocaust Remembrance Day was held January 30 at two
events: noontime at Abbot Hall and evening at Temple Sinai. Each event drew
about 50 people, with Police Chief Dennis King at the candlelit noon event.
Select Board Chair Dan Fox spoke at both events along with MHS senior
Sasha Genezer granddaughter of Holocaust survivors. Sasha shared stories
her grandparents told her about their experiences at Auschwitz.

on June 14.
