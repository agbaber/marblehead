OBSERVER CORPS REPORT

Board of Health - 1-9-24
Hybrid Meeting, Recorded
LWVM Observer: Tom Krueger

Members in Attendance: Andrew Petty, Tom McMahon, Joanne Miller, Helaine Hazlett

Tobacco Control - the tobacco control officer was unable to attend because of an
emergency and will be rescheduled to 3-12-24.

Mental Health Task Force (MHTF) - Ms. Miller mentioned that Chris Herron will be at
the high school 1/10 and the MHTF will have a table at the event. In addition, members
of the task force met with the Marblehead Current editorial team and about the
continued collaboration, e.g., the monthly article the MHTF submits. There have been
many suggestions to explore - navigating mental health in the digital age, tele-therapy
(done by the MCC), and discussion supporting the “screenager” program, active in
Lynnfield, a media movie system, which covers the various mental health concerns of
kids.

In addition, the MHTF wants to buttress relationship with MHD public schools. A
meeting is planned with Julia Ferraria, the assistant superintendent. The MHTF may
engage in a project of Charley Applestein, who has a program on positive trauma
strength-based training, at the high school. They also want to do some articles looking
at older populations.

Peter Slack continues to maintain the MHD Cares website and adds new content. He
will teach Ms. Miller how to analyze how the website is being used by the community.
The analysis showed in 2023 there were 5,742 unique visitors to the website, 10,354
visits.

Transfer Station Update - Mr. Petty said the transfer station construction will be out to
bid 1/17/24 and due back 2/21/24. There will be a day when contractors can come to
ask questions. The budget and the total funds available are $1,258,000. When this is
put out to bid, there are certain things that need to be done - the “base”- and then
“alternates” depending on funds available. The BASE (3 items) bid includes pit
renovation: the compactor on site, and there is an agreement to have this installed by
the company who purchased the compactor as well as to do the funnel work. Pit work
would be the cladding, the out-front work, the manned shack; then the site work (new
scale pit), regrading, etc.; and finally, the scale house foundation. (The temporary trailer
is out of the way of upcoming work.) Next are the ALTERNATES (5 items) - new scale
house, the transaction pad, the swap shed pad, the transaction hut (where residents
can buy stickers, etc.), and the swap shop building. (More transactions will be done
online, etc. so receipts can be shown to TS personnel),

Mr. Petty said that once construction starts the biggest interruptions will be to the
commercial side. The plan is to leave the current scale in so material can continue to
weigh things and stay operational. During this phase, the board may need to meet more
often to discuss things as they come up. The one thing that has not occurred so far is
the permit from DEP - this was supposed to be granted before the new year. The
bidding can go forward despite this, but actual construction can only take place when
the permit is granted.

A question came up about the bids - the bids will be “broken” down as in the steps
above, so “base” bid will be lumped together with the “alternates.” By 2/21 Mr. Petty will
have more information about bids and any need for more resources. Another question
concerned the projected size of the swap shed - currently it is the size of two shipping
containers, the new one would be a little larger. The reason the “pad” (foundation)
alternate is higher priority than the building is that if funds were not available perhaps
other means could be used to build the shop shed itself.

BOH Website - Mr. Petty passed out handouts and began the discussion about the top
two items, mental health, and physical health. He mentioned that there is a North Shore
Public Health Collaborative website. The Board could look at their resources and
potentially incorporate them into the MDH website. There are various toolkit resources
available on it that the Board might choose. There is also on the NSPHC website
physical health resources. Ms. Miller wondered where the MHD Cares website fits into
this discussion. Perhaps the best idea would be to have a redundancy with the BOH
website, and MHD Cares website. This is just the beginning, and it will evolve.

Directors Report

Budget - Mr. Petty said that budget season is beginning. On 1/24/24 the State of the
Town is the kickoff. Then he will meet with the liaisons, will bring the budget to the
board, and discuss. The current direction is to have a “level” budget: level services,
level funded. He mentioned discussion about funding for the MCC, perhaps an
additional staff member at the TS which is very busy, and an additional person in the
office to answer phones. All agree that everything is “tight” in the town. Mr. McMahon
asked if an extra person might allow the TS to be open at lunch and on Sunday. Mr.
Petty said that the staffing is currently so tight that if someone is sick at the TS, he must
send someone from the HD office (Marty). To actually operate during lunchtime there
would have to be three people available. Later Mr. Petty noted that they will keep the
current employees, that there are large contractual increases and as a consequence the
HD is always over the 2.5% increase.

BOH Workshop - this is commonly done by groups to enhance communication. Mr.
Petty asked the board about their thoughts about that. The board discussed the logistics
of doing this. This would be an open to the public meeting. Ms. Hazlett mentioned a
potential facilitator, Hannah Bowen, executive director of The Resolution Center in
Beverly. The board will do a “doodle poll” to find a time.
Other - Ms. Miller and Tom Massaro went to the Wellesley Transfer Center. The staff
there were very welcoming and showed pride in their facility (which is huge). There they
separate out all their recycling materials, so they can sort out the “valuable” items out
and sell them as a commodity. They even have a bailing machine on site - e.g., bail all
the clean white paper. They have buyers for their product. They also take out all the
“redemptions” (bottles and cans) and take those to a redemption center. This is perhaps
something that MHD could do. (There is a bottle bill in front of the legislature to increase
the fee from five cents to ten cents redemptions.) Note - Wellesley does NOT do
curbside pickup.

Mr. Petty will be working on the town report which is due 2/9/24; before that he has to
do a state report on all the trash and recycling done, etc.

Ms. Miller asked precisely “what opening the bids” means: Mr. Petty responded that it
literally means opening up the envelopes containing the bids from each contractor and
seeing what they bid for the "base" work as well as the "alternate" work. All envelopes
are opened at the same time at Abbot Hall. The lowest qualified bid wins the contract.
