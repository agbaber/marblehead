--- title: "Marblehead Levy Limit Over Time" ---

Marblehead Prop 2½ Levy Limit, FY2001–FY2027

The legal cap on Marblehead's base property tax levy, drawn as a step function because the limit is set once per fiscal year. Source: MA DOR DLS.

Massachusetts towns operate under a Prop 2½ levy limit: the maximum the base property tax levy can be in that year. The limit grows automatically by 2.5% on the prior year's limit (not on what was actually collected), plus new growth from new construction, plus any voter-approved overrides. The limit is set once per fiscal year and held constant within the year – hence the step shape.

Prop 2½ levy limit

The limit grew from $28.6M in FY2001 to a projected $77.5M in FY2027 – about 4.0% compounded annually. Most of that is the automatic 2.5% statutory increase plus new growth from new construction. The visible bumps in FY02, FY04, FY05, and FY06 are the four operating overrides voters approved in that period, which permanently raised the base by about $4.9M total. No general operating override has passed since FY2006.

What this chart does and doesn't show

  • Shows: the Prop 2½ base levy limit. This is the maximum amount the town is legally authorized to raise from property taxes for general operations, before any debt-exclusion bonds are added.
  • Does not show: the actual amount collected in property taxes. In Marblehead, the actual levy has tracked the limit very closely – the town has not had meaningful unused capacity in any year shown.
  • Does not show: debt exclusions for specific bond payments (school renovations, library expansion, etc.). Those add temporary capacity on top of this limit and decay as the bonds are paid off.

Why this matters for the FY27 override

If voters approve any of the three override tiers on June 9, 2026, that amount is added to the levy limit base permanently. From the FY27 vote forward, the entire new base – including the override amount – grows at 2.5% per year automatically. The MA Division of Local Services is unambiguous on this point:

"The override results in a permanent increase in the levy limit of a community, which as part of the levy limit base, increases at the rate of 2.5 percent each year."

– Levy Limits: A Primer on Proposition 2½, MA Division of Local Services

So the visible jumps in FY02–FY06 on this chart are the closest historical analog for what an FY27 override step would look like – except much larger. A Tier 3 ($15M) override would add roughly three times the FY02–FY06 cumulative override authority in a single year.

One nuance specific to the FY27 override: the town plans to phase in collection over three years (drawing $4.3M in FY27, $10.5M in FY28, full $15M in FY29). The full $15M is added to the legal limit in FY27 regardless – the phase-in is a choice to underlevy in the first two years. The unused portion during the phase-in becomes "excess levy capacity" the town could tap in any future year without needing another override. This would be a departure from Marblehead's historical pattern of levying right at the cap.

Source. Levy limit values FY00–FY27 from MA DOR DLS "Municipal Levy Limit" reports for Marblehead (municipality code 168), retrieved from the DLS Gateway. Compiled in data/marblehead_levy.csv. FY26 and FY27 values are forward-looking budget-planning numbers, not yet finalized.

Step rendering. The line is drawn as a step function because the levy limit is set once per fiscal year and held constant within that year. A smooth line would imply continuous growth, but the legal mechanism is discrete: each July 1, the prior year's limit is multiplied by 1.025, new growth and any approved overrides are added, and the new ceiling is set for the entire upcoming fiscal year.

What "2.5% on the limit" means. The MA DLS primer is explicit: "It is important to note that a community's levy limit is based on the previous year's levy limit and not on the previous year's actual levy." This means a town that underlevies (collects less than its limit allows) does not lose the corresponding 2.5% growth – the ceiling continues to rise on the full prior limit. For Marblehead this is mostly academic, since the town has historically levied right at the cap, but it becomes load-bearing for the FY27–FY29 override phase-in window.
