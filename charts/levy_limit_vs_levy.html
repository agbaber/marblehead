Marblehead Prop 2½ Levy Limit, FY2001–FY2027

The legal cap on Marblehead's base property tax levy, drawn as a step function because the limit is set once per fiscal year. Source: MA DOR DLS.

Massachusetts towns operate under a Prop 2½ levy limit: the maximum the base property tax levy can be in that year. The limit grows automatically by 2.5% on the prior year's limit (not on what was actually collected), plus new growth from new construction, plus any voter-approved overrides. Set once per fiscal year and held constant within the year.

Prop 2½ levy limit

The limit grew from $28.6M in FY2001 to a projected $77.5M in FY2027, about 4.0% compounded annually. Most of that is the automatic 2.5% statutory increase plus new growth from new construction. The marked bumps in FY02, FY04, FY05, and FY06 are the four operating overrides voters approved in that period, which permanently raised the base by about $4.9M total. No general operating override has passed since FY2006.

Marblehead has levied within 0.27% of its maximum allowable levy in every single year since FY2007 (the data range available). When the town has had levy authority, it has used it.

What this chart shows and doesn't show

  • Shows: the Prop 2½ base levy limit. The maximum amount the town is legally authorized to raise from property taxes for general operations.
  • Does not show: the actual amount collected in property taxes. In Marblehead, the actual levy has tracked the limit closely – the town has had essentially no unused capacity in any year shown.
  • Does not show: debt exclusions for specific bond payments (school renovations, library expansion, etc.). Those add temporary capacity on top of this limit and decay as the bonds are paid off. Marblehead's debt exclusions add about $9M to the actual levy in FY2026.
  • Does not show: the full town budget. The levy is one revenue source. Total general fund spending in FY26 was about $106M, funded by the levy plus state aid, local receipts, free cash, and other sources.

Why this matters for the FY27 override

If voters approve any of the three override tiers on June 9, 2026, that amount is added to the levy limit base permanently. From the FY27 vote forward, the entire new base – including the override amount – grows at 2.5% per year automatically. The MA Division of Local Services is unambiguous on this point:

"The override results in a permanent increase in the levy limit of a community, which as part of the levy limit base, increases at the rate of 2.5 percent each year."

– Levy Limits: A Primer on Proposition 2½, MA Division of Local Services

Source. Levy limit values FY00–FY27 from MA DOR DLS "Municipal Levy Limit" reports for Marblehead (municipality code 168), retrieved from the DLS Gateway. Compiled in data/marblehead_levy.csv. The 0.27%-of-cap finding draws on DLS "Excess Levy Capacity and Override Capacity" data for Marblehead FY2007–FY2026, in data/marblehead_excess_levy_capacity.csv. FY26 and FY27 levy limit values are forward-looking budget-planning numbers, not yet finalized.

Step rendering. The line is drawn as a step function because the levy limit is set once per fiscal year and held constant within that year. The legal mechanism is discrete: each July 1, the prior year's limit is multiplied by 1.025, new growth and any approved overrides are added, and the new ceiling is set for the entire upcoming fiscal year.

What "2.5% on the limit" means. The MA DLS primer is explicit: "It is important to note that a community's levy limit is based on the previous year's levy limit and not on the previous year's actual levy." A town that under-levies (collects less than its limit allows) does not lose the corresponding 2.5% growth – the ceiling continues to rise on the full prior limit.
