--- title: "Marblehead Levy Limit Over Time" ---

Marblehead Prop 2½ Levy Limit, FY2001–FY2035

Historical levy limit through FY2027, with projected paths under each override tier through FY2035. Source: MA DOR DLS.

Massachusetts towns operate under a Prop 2½ levy limit: the maximum the base property tax levy can be in that year. The limit grows automatically by 2.5% on the prior year's limit (not on what was actually collected), plus new growth from new construction, plus any voter-approved overrides. The limit is set once per fiscal year and held constant within the year – hence the step shape.

What the projections show

  • The override is permanent, not one-time. Whatever tier passes (or doesn't) determines the levy ceiling forever, with the override amount baked into the base that grows 2.5%/yr automatically.
  • By FY35, the gap between tiers grows. Tier 3 sits about $18.3M above the no-override baseline at FY35 – the original $15M plus eight years of compounding on top. Tier 1 is about $10.9M above baseline by then. The compounding magnifies the original step.
  • The chart's y-axis is set to $120M to fit the projections. That extends well past the historical chart range. To put it in scale: a Tier 3 outcome would, by FY35, raise Marblehead's levy ceiling above what the entire town's residential and commercial property tax bills total today.

How the FY02–FY06 overrides compare

Marblehead's last successful general operating override was FY2006. The four override wins between FY02 and FY06 added about $4.9M of permanent levy authority over five years, in increments of $300K, $1.4M, $0.5M, and $2.7M. A Tier 3 ($15M) override in a single year would be roughly three times the entire FY02–FY06 cumulative addition, all at once.

Those FY02–FY06 override steps are the closest historical analog the chart contains for what June 9, 2026 would do. The visual comparison: notice how steep the FY27 step looks under any tier compared to the FY02–FY06 cluster. That's not a chart artifact – it's what the override mechanically does to the ceiling.

What this chart does and doesn't show

  • Shows: the Prop 2½ base levy limit. This is the maximum amount the town is legally authorized to raise from property taxes for general operations.
  • Does not show: the actual amount collected in property taxes. In Marblehead, the actual levy has tracked the limit closely – the town has had essentially no unused capacity in any year shown. The proposed FY27–FY29 phase-in (drawing $4.3M, $10.5M, then $15M for Tier 3) would temporarily underlevy, but the limit itself jumps the full amount in FY27.
  • Does not show: debt exclusions for specific bond payments (school renovations, library expansion). Those add temporary capacity on top of this limit and decay as the bonds are paid off.
  • Does not show: spending or free cash. The levy limit is a ceiling, not a budget. For revenue vs. expenses trajectories under override scenarios, see the deficit dynamics model.

Why this matters

The MA Division of Local Services is unambiguous on what the override does to the limit:

"The override results in a permanent increase in the levy limit of a community, which as part of the levy limit base, increases at the rate of 2.5 percent each year."

– Levy Limits: A Primer on Proposition 2½, MA Division of Local Services

So the FY27 vote isn't just "do we add $9M / $12M / $15M to next year's tax bills" – it's "do we permanently shift the ceiling that compounds for every future year." That's why the projection branches keep diverging from the no-override baseline rather than converging back.

One nuance specific to the FY27 override: the town plans to phase in collection over three years (drawing $4.3M in FY27, $10.5M in FY28, full $15M in FY29 for Tier 3). The full tier amount is added to the legal limit in FY27 regardless – the phase-in is a choice to underlevy in the first two years. The unused portion during the phase-in becomes "excess levy capacity" the town could tap in any future year without needing another override. This would be a departure from Marblehead's historical pattern of levying right at the cap.

Projection methodology. Projections FY28–FY35 compound the FY27 base at 2.5% per year, the statutory minimum under Prop 2½. New growth (typically $200K–$500K per year for Marblehead) is excluded from projections because future new growth is not a known quantity. Including expected new growth would raise all four projection lines by roughly the same percentage, so the relative spread between tiers and baseline is unchanged. For each tier, the full override amount is added to the FY27 limit regardless of whether the town levies up to that amount – per DLS, the limit grows on the prior year's limit, not on actual levied amount.

Step rendering. Lines are drawn as step functions because the levy limit is set once per fiscal year and held constant within that year. A smooth line would imply continuous growth, but the legal mechanism is discrete: each July 1, the prior year's limit is multiplied by 1.025, new growth and any approved overrides are added, and the new ceiling is set for the entire upcoming fiscal year.

What "2.5% on the limit" means. The MA DLS primer is explicit: "It is important to note that a community's levy limit is based on the previous year's levy limit and not on the previous year's actual levy." This means a town that underlevies (collects less than its limit allows) does not lose the corresponding 2.5% growth – the ceiling continues to rise on the full prior limit. For Marblehead this is mostly academic, since the town has historically levied right at the cap, but it becomes load-bearing for the FY27–FY29 override phase-in window.
