Cost Burden and Tax Relief in Marblehead

Before any override is voted on: 1,672 owner-occupied households (24.7%) already pay more than 30% of income on housing. Marblehead grants 144 statutory tax exemptions in a typical year.

The US Department of Housing and Urban Development calls a household "cost-burdened" when housing costs (mortgage, property tax, insurance, utilities) exceed 30% of income. American Community Survey table B25106 measures this directly for every Massachusetts community. In Marblehead, 1,672 of 6,776 owner-occupied households already meet that definition. Another 1,357 sit in the 20-29% band: one modest tax bump from crossing the line.

Combining the age cross-tab: 868 cost-burdened owner households are under 65, and 804 are 65 or older. The age split matters because Marblehead's statutory relief programs are almost entirely 65+.

What Marblehead's relief programs cover

Massachusetts authorizes municipalities to grant property tax exemptions and deferrals under G.L. c. 59, sec. 5. Each "clause" is a separate program. Marblehead's assessor's office lists the following adopted programs:

The Marblehead FY2024 Annual Report's assessor's section states: "The Board of Assessors granted 144 statutory exemptions for a total of $182,907.62 to qualified applicants and senior work-off volunteers (as of December 2024)." That averages $1,270 per recipient. The state Cherry Sheet reports a $129,339 reimbursement to Marblehead for veterans, blind, surviving-spouse, and elderly exemptions in FY26.

The relief gap

The town's statutory exemption programs touch about 144 households in any given year. The cost-burdened owner population is roughly 1,672. Even if every single exemption went to a cost-burdened recipient (an upper bound; some go to veterans and blind residents who may not be cost-burdened), the reach is 8.6%.

Across all Marblehead owners, the under-65 missing middle is the structural gap. The 65+ cost-burdened cohort has theoretical access to Clauses 41C, 41A, 17D, and the state Circuit Breaker tax credit, though most do not claim them in practice. Under-65 cost-burdened households have access to the veteran and blind exemptions if they qualify on those grounds, but no general-purpose program exists for working-age homeowners in financial difficulty. The proposed means-tested senior exemption (2025 Town Meeting Article 28) would extend reach within the 65+ group if the state legislature enacts the enabling act; it would not change the under-65 picture.

Scope. This page measures cost burden among owner-occupied households. It does not measure renter burden. Marblehead has 1,411 renter-occupied households; renter cost burden is tabulated by ACS table B25074 and is not analyzed here. Override-driven property tax increases flow through to renters indirectly via rent.

What ACS measures. "Cost burden" in this chart uses the HUD threshold of 30% of household income on selected monthly owner costs (mortgage principal and interest, property taxes, hazard insurance, utilities, condo fees, mobile home costs). The override changes the property tax component only. The chart does not model the override; it establishes the baseline against which any override interacts.

Why the under-65 cost-burdened count is structural. Of the major clauses Marblehead has adopted, Clause 41C requires age 65+, Clause 41A requires age 65+, Clause 17D requires age 70+ or surviving-spouse status, and Clause 5K requires retirement age. Clauses 22 and 37A do not have age requirements but require veteran status or legal blindness respectively. The under-65 cost-burdened cohort (868 households) is largely outside this matrix.

Why the 144 number is generous. The 144 figure includes recipients of clauses unrelated to cost burden (some veteran-status and blind-clause recipients are higher-income and not cost-burdened). Treating all 144 as cost-burden relief overstates the reach. A more conservative read is that 100-130 of the 144 are at the intersection of cost burden and statutory relief.

What is not modeled. Massachusetts Circuit Breaker (G.L. c. 62, sec. 6(k)) is a state income tax credit, not a local exemption. It is available to homeowners 65+ with income under approximately $72,000 single / $109,000 joint (2024 limits) whose property tax exceeds 10% of income. Statewide claim rates suggest most eligible seniors do not claim it. Marblehead-specific Circuit Breaker uptake is not reported in publicly available data.
