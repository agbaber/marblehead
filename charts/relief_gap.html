Cost Burden and Tax Relief in Marblehead

Before any override, 1,756 of Marblehead's 7,061 owner-occupied households already pay more than 30% of household income on housing. The town grants about 144 tax exemptions a year.

A household is "cost-burdened" when housing costs (mortgage, property tax, insurance, utilities) exceed 30% of household income. The federal government uses that threshold to decide who qualifies for affordable-housing programs, and it is the standard cutoff in housing-policy research.

The Census Bureau measures it through its annual survey of about 3.5 million American households. Surveyors ask each household what it earns and what it pays for housing; the ratio is computed per household and totaled across the community. In Marblehead the survey covers 7,061 owner households and 1,228 renter households. Of the owners, 1,756 are over the 30% line, and another 1,439 sit between 20% and 30%, close enough that a modest tax bump would push them over.

Who the cost-burdened are

By income, nearly every owner earning under $35,000 is cost-burdened, and most under $50,000. By age, the 65-and-older share (34%) is roughly double the under-65 share (20%). The age cut matters because Marblehead's tax-relief programs are almost all restricted to seniors.

Marblehead's tax relief programs

Massachusetts gives every town a menu of property tax relief programs under state law (G.L. c. 59, sec. 5). Each program is a separate "clause." Marblehead has adopted these:

ProgramWho qualifiesWhat it does
Clause 41CElderly low-income Age 65+, modest income and asset limits. Limits are adjusted annually for inflation (Clause 41D, adopted at 2024 Town Meeting). Credit on the property tax bill, about $500 to $1,000.
Clause 41ASenior deferral Age 65+. Income limit set by state default ($20,000) unless the town raises it. Marblehead does not advertise a higher local limit. Defers the property tax bill until the home is sold or the owner dies. Balance accrues interest at the state-default 8% simple rate.
Clause 17DSenior, blind, surviving spouse Age 70+, or surviving spouse of any age. No income test. About $175 credit.
Clause 22 seriesVeterans Veteran status with a service-connected disability rating. $400 to $2,000 credit, by disability level.
Clause 37ALegally blind Certified legally blind. $500 credit.
Clause 5KSenior tax work-off Age 60 or 65 and up, town-administered. Up to about $1,500 credit, earned by volunteering hours for the town.
Means-tested senior exemptionProposed Age 65+, household income up to the state Circuit Breaker limit (roughly $72,000 single). Pending. Home rule petition adopted at 2025 Town Meeting (Article 28) awaiting state legislative approval.

The town's FY2024 Annual Report records 144 statutory exemptions granted that year, totaling $182,907 in reduced tax bills. That averages about $1,270 per recipient against an average tax bill of $11,055. State reimbursement was $129,339 (FY26 Cherry Sheet).

The relief gap

Set the two numbers side by side: 1,756 Marblehead owner households are cost-burdened today. The town's statutory exemption programs touch about 144 households in any given year. Even if every single one of those 144 went to a cost-burdened recipient, the reach is 8.6%.

The structural reason: most of Marblehead's adopted relief programs require the householder to be 65 or older (Clauses 41C, 41A, 17D, 5K) or to qualify on a non-age basis (veteran status, legal blindness). The 65+ cost-burdened cohort has theoretical access to multiple programs. The under-65 cost-burdened cohort, 853 owner households, has access only to the veteran and blind exemptions if they qualify on those grounds. There is no general-purpose Marblehead property tax program for a working-age homeowner in financial difficulty.

The proposed means-tested senior exemption (2025 Town Meeting Article 28) would extend reach within the 65+ group if the state legislature enacts the enabling act. It would not change the under-65 picture.

Scope. This page measures cost burden among owner-occupied households. It does not measure renter burden. Marblehead has 1,228 renter-occupied households; renter cost burden is tabulated by ACS table B25074 and is not analyzed here. Override-driven property tax increases flow through to renters indirectly via rent.

What ACS measures. "Cost burden" uses the HUD threshold of 30% of household income on selected monthly owner costs (mortgage principal and interest, property taxes, hazard insurance, utilities, condo fees, mobile home costs). The override changes the property tax component only. This page does not model the override; it establishes the baseline against which any override interacts.

Why the 144 number is generous. The 144 figure includes recipients of clauses unrelated to cost burden (some veteran-status and blind-clause recipients are higher-income and not cost-burdened). Treating all 144 as cost-burden relief is the upper bound on the program's reach into the cost-burdened cohort. A more conservative read is that 100-130 of the 144 are at the intersection.

What is not modeled. Massachusetts Circuit Breaker (G.L. c. 62, sec. 6(k)) is a state income tax credit, not a local exemption. It is available to homeowners 65+ with income under approximately $72,000 single / $109,000 joint (2024 limits) whose property tax exceeds 10% of income. Statewide claim rates suggest most eligible seniors do not claim it. Marblehead-specific Circuit Breaker uptake is not reported in publicly available data.
