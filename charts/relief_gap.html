Cost Burden and Tax Relief in Marblehead

Before any override is voted on, 1,672 of 6,776 owner-occupied households already pay more than 30% of income on housing. Marblehead grants about 144 statutory tax exemptions in a typical year.

The US Department of Housing and Urban Development calls a household "cost-burdened" when housing costs (mortgage, property tax, insurance, utilities) exceed 30% of household income. American Community Survey table B25106 measures this directly for every Massachusetts community. In Marblehead, the count today, before any override, is 1,672. Another 1,357 sit in the 20-29% band: a modest tax bump from the line.

Who the cost-burdened are

The 1,672 cost-burdened owner households break down sharply along two cuts. By income, nearly every Marblehead owner earning under $35,000 is cost-burdened, and most up to $50,000. By age, the 65-and-older share is much higher than for the under-65 population.

What Marblehead's relief programs cover

Massachusetts authorizes municipalities to grant property tax exemptions and deferrals under G.L. c. 59, sec. 5. Each "clause" is a separate program. Marblehead's assessor lists the following adopted programs:

The Marblehead FY2024 Annual Report's assessor's section states: "The Board of Assessors granted 144 statutory exemptions for a total of $182,907.62 to qualified applicants and senior work-off volunteers (as of December 2024)." That averages $1,270 per recipient. The state Cherry Sheet reports a $129,339 reimbursement to Marblehead for veterans, blind, surviving-spouse, and elderly exemptions in FY26.

The relief gap

Set the two numbers side by side: 1,672 Marblehead owner households are cost-burdened today. The town's statutory exemption programs touch about 144 households in any given year. Even if every single one of those 144 went to a cost-burdened recipient, the reach is 8.6%.

The structural reason: most of Marblehead's adopted relief programs require the householder to be 65 or older (Clauses 41C, 41A, 17D, 5K) or to qualify on a non-age basis (veteran status, legal blindness). The 65+ cost-burdened cohort has theoretical access to multiple programs. The under-65 cost-burdened cohort, 868 owner households, has access only to the veteran and blind exemptions if they qualify on those grounds. There is no general-purpose Marblehead property tax program for a working-age homeowner in financial difficulty.

The proposed means-tested senior exemption (2025 Town Meeting Article 28) would extend reach within the 65+ group if the state legislature enacts the enabling act. It would not change the under-65 picture.

Scope. This page measures cost burden among owner-occupied households. It does not measure renter burden. Marblehead has 1,411 renter-occupied households; renter cost burden is tabulated by ACS table B25074 and is not analyzed here. Override-driven property tax increases flow through to renters indirectly via rent.

What ACS measures. "Cost burden" uses the HUD threshold of 30% of household income on selected monthly owner costs (mortgage principal and interest, property taxes, hazard insurance, utilities, condo fees, mobile home costs). The override changes the property tax component only. This page does not model the override; it establishes the baseline against which any override interacts.

Why the 144 number is generous. The 144 figure includes recipients of clauses unrelated to cost burden (some veteran-status and blind-clause recipients are higher-income and not cost-burdened). Treating all 144 as cost-burden relief is the upper bound on the program's reach into the cost-burdened cohort. A more conservative read is that 100-130 of the 144 are at the intersection.

What is not modeled. Massachusetts Circuit Breaker (G.L. c. 62, sec. 6(k)) is a state income tax credit, not a local exemption. It is available to homeowners 65+ with income under approximately $72,000 single / $109,000 joint (2024 limits) whose property tax exceeds 10% of income. Statewide claim rates suggest most eligible seniors do not claim it. Marblehead-specific Circuit Breaker uptake is not reported in publicly available data.
