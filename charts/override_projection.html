Marblehead Levy Limit Projection, FY2027–FY2035

Where the Prop 2½ ceiling lands under each override outcome, and how the gap between scenarios compounds. Source: MA DOR DLS.

Each tier of the June 9, 2026 ballot adds a different amount to the legal levy ceiling. The ceiling jumps in FY27 (full override amount, regardless of phase-in) then compounds at 2.5%/year automatically. Eight years out, the gap between tiers is the original step plus eight years of compounding on top.

No override (2.5%/yr baseline)
Tier 1: $9M (Restore)
Tier 2: $12M (Build)
Tier 3: $15M (Invest)

Each tier branch starts with the full override step at FY2027, then compounds at 2.5%/year through FY2035. The shaded band over FY27–FY29 marks the planned phase-in window: even though the legal cap jumps the full amount in FY27, the town would only collect $4.3M, $10.5M, and $15M of Tier 3 in those three years respectively. By FY35, Tier 3 sits about $18.3M above the no-override baseline – the original $15M plus eight years of compound growth.

The override is a permanent shift to the levy ceiling, not a one-time charge. The fan widens because the override amount compounds at 2.5%/year forever; it doesn't converge back.

What "phase-in" means here

The chart projects the legal levy limit, not the actual amount collected. Per DLS, the full override amount is added to the limit on day one of FY27. The town's planned phase-in only affects how much of that authority is actually levied in the first three years:

YearTier 1 leviedTier 2 leviedTier 3 levied
FY27$1.3M (14%)$2.8M (23%)$4.3M (29%)
FY28$6.3M (70%)$8.7M (73%)$10.5M (70%)
FY29$9.0M (100%)$12.0M (100%)$15.0M (100%)
FY30+Steady-state at full draw, growing 2.5%/yr

The unused authority during phase-in becomes "excess levy capacity" the town could tap any future year without another override. This would be a departure from Marblehead's historical pattern: in 20 years of DLS data (FY07–FY26), the town has never had more than 0.27% of its cap unused. Statewide, only ~22% of MA towns leave 5%+ unused in a typical year, and they're either tiny rural communities whose limit has grown past their service needs or commercial-heavy cities choosing not to push business taxes higher.

Why all four lines diverge rather than converge

The override doesn't expire. The MA Division of Local Services primer:

"The override results in a permanent increase in the levy limit of a community, which as part of the levy limit base, increases at the rate of 2.5 percent each year."

– Levy Limits: A Primer on Proposition 2½

So once a tier wins, that amount sits permanently in the base. Each year the limit grows 2.5% on the prior year's limit (not on what the town actually collected), so the override portion compounds alongside the base. Eight years on, Tier 3 is not just $15M higher than no-override – it's about $18.3M higher, because that initial $15M has been earning 2.5%/yr.

One thing the chart deliberately leaves out: the override only changes the ceiling, not the spending. Town Meeting still appropriates the actual budget each year. So while the override permanently expands what the town could levy, residents and Town Meeting retain annual control over what is actually collected and spent within that ceiling. The ceiling is the rigid one; Town Meeting is the flexible one.

Phase-in source. Tier-by-tier phase-in dollar amounts from the April 15, 2026 Select Board override presentation, also reflected on what-is-the-override.html.

Projection methodology. The 2.5%/yr compounding rate is the statutory minimum under Prop 2½. New growth (typically $200K–$500K per year for Marblehead) is excluded from projections because future new growth is not a known quantity. Including it would raise all four lines by roughly the same percentage, so the relative spread between tiers and baseline is unchanged.

FY35 endpoint values. No override: $94.5M. Tier 1: $105.4M. Tier 2: $109.1M. Tier 3: $112.8M. The "$94M / $105M / $109M / $113M" rounding on the chart preserves the spread but loses the cents.
